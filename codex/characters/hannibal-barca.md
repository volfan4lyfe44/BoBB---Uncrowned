*The Ghost-General of the Ashmarch*

**Age at Death**: ~64 (committed suicide to avoid Roman capture) **Origin**: Carthage, 183 BCE **Covenant Role**: Commander of irregular, mobile warbands **Personal Banner**: A blackened elephant standard wreathed in laurel flame **Philosophy**: Vengeance through excellence. Resentment sublimated into genius.

---

## 🧠 **Historical Legacy in Brief**

- Son of Hamilcar Barca; sworn enemy of Rome from youth.
- Led one of the most audacious military campaigns in history: marched an army, including elephants, over the Alps into Italy.
- Won multiple major battles (e.g. Cannae) but lacked political and logistical support to take Rome itself.
- Spent the latter part of his life in exile, pursued by Rome until his suicide.

> **Hannibal died not from defeat, but from being denied a final battle.**

---

## 🔥 **Hannibal’s Function in the Covenant of the Eternal Empire**

### **1. Why He Joined Alexander**

- Hannibal sees Alexander as both **legacy and opportunity**—a successor to imperial glory, yes, but also a leader who wields the resources Hannibal *never had*.
- He is drawn to Alexander’s strength, but **not his divinity**. Hannibal doesn’t kneel. He collaborates.
- His decision is **pragmatic and strategic**: siding with Alexander ensures relevance, freedom to wage war, and **the chance to fight without the chains of Carthage behind him**.

> *“I followed no gods in life. I follow no gods now. But I follow strength when it points toward Rome.”*

### **2. Jealousy and Rivalry**

- Hannibal **resents Alexander's legend**—a man who conquered the known world and died before his flaws could undo him.
- He considers himself **Napoleon’s equal**, if not his superior in raw tactical brilliance. But he knows history does not agree.
- His relationship with Napoleon is one of **tense mutual respect**, laced with quiet jabs and unspoken comparisons.

> Napoleon: *“You lost to Rome.”* Hannibal: *“No—I was not allowed to win.”*

### **3. How He Fights in Eirion**

- Commands **asymmetric, elite forces** that specialize in **unconventional engagements**, deception, and ambush.
- Prefers battlefields shaped by chaos and terrain—he **never plays by standard rules**.
- His units often win **without direct confrontation**—crippling morale, logistics, or internal cohesion first.

---

## ⚔️ **Narrative Use and Interpersonal Tension**

- **With Alexander**: Loyal... for now. He respects power. But **Alexander's claim to godhood is offensive** to him. Hannibal sees himself as a man who made his own legend without needing worship.
- **With Napoleon**: Political rivalry simmering beneath tactical respect. They may team up in a future betrayal—or stand in final judgment of each other.
- **With Charles XII**: Hannibal finds him reckless and useful. “A hammer, not a mind.”
- **With Attila**: Hannibal considers him a force of nature, not a peer. He’s **cautiously contemptuous**, knowing Attila could destroy everything—including strategic victory.

---

## 🧠 Summary in a Sentence

> **Hannibal Barca is the warlord whose brilliance was never allowed to finish its arc—now, in Eirion, he fights not for Alexander’s dream, but for his own reckoning with history.**
