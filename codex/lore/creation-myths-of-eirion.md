## 1. **The Threadborn Myth**

> *The Oldest Weaving. The First Snap.*

**As the Ancients tell it:**

Before time, all stories were one thread—golden, eternal, and unbroken. This thread wound through creation, tying gods to men, kings to beggars, beginnings to ends.

But the thread grew heavy with contradictions.

One day, a story was born with too much weight—a soul so defiant, so vivid, that its presence strained the Loom. It could not be cut, could not be forgotten. It *snapped* the thread.

Where it broke, a tangle formed: a knot of memory, pride, sacrifice, and sorrow.

That knot became **Eirion**.

The gods tried to burn it. Time tried to bury it. But every spark of purpose too great to be severed wove itself into the world.

> *Eirion is not where life goes when it ends.* *It is where stories go when they demand a second ending.*

## 2. **The Song of the Unraveled**

> *As kept by the Remnants of the Old Voice*

Once, the universe was silence, save for a single voice. This voice sang the cosmos into being, one note for every life, every moment.

But some notes rang louder than others. They lingered.

They refused to resolve.

They **echoed**, discordant and bright, refusing to fade into the final chord.

So the Voice made a place for them—**a resonant chamber**—where unfinished songs could continue. Where unresolved notes could find harmony, or shatter.

This place was not born. It was *sustained* by will.

> **Eirion is not a realm.** **It is an echo chamber of immortal resonance.**
>

## 3. **The Fire Beneath the Thread**

> *An Alexanderian heresy—whispered among his generals*

Long before kingdoms, there was fire. Not the fire of stars or war—but the fire of *intention*. Pure will, unshaped.

That fire sought form. So it bound itself into thread. Line. Pattern. Story.

But fire hates containment.

And so, when certain wills flared too hot—too bold for fate’s design—they burned through their thread and fell *beneath the loom*.

There, they forged their own realm, where no law binds them but power.

Alexander believes this place—**Eirion**—is not a prison. It is **a furnace**. A proving ground.

> *“If the gods will not crown us, then we shall burn our names into their sky.”*
