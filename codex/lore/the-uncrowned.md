*Their names were never carved in stone. Their endings were never written. Yet still, they rise.*

---

### 🔹 Who Are the Uncrowned?

The Uncrowned are not rulers. They are not saints. They are not even rebels—not in the way the Covenant defines rebellion.

They are the **unfinished**.

Drawn to Eirion by threads of fate too strong to be severed, they are the souls **whose deaths echoed without resolution**—whose final acts sparked something greater than themselves, but who never lived to see what that spark became.

They arrive in Eirion at the threshold of purpose, not exaltation. Some died in obscurity, some in glory, some with their hands still raised for peace or fire. All were denied the *conclusion* their stories demanded.

> “They were men and women whose lives could have mattered more—if not for the knife, the bullet, the lie, the silence.”

---

### 🔹 What Makes Them Different?

Where the Covenant demands allegiance, the Uncrowned demand **reckoning**. Where others seek conquest, they seek **clarity**.

They do not wear titles. They do not claim dominion. They walk Eirion not as kings of memory, but as **pilgrims of consequence**—each bearing a name that history almost forgot, and the burden of discovering what it was meant to mean.

Each Uncrowned soul is faced with a simple, impossible question:

> **If your death was not enough to finish the story—what do you do with what remains?**

---

### 🔹 The Bastion of Broken Banners

The Bastion is not their fortress. It is their *threshold*. A monument to sacrifice and ambiguity, where names are carved and scratched away, where banners hang in stillness, waiting to catch wind again.

Here, the Uncrowned confront the memory of what they were, and the fear of what they might become.

Some stay forever—unable to face what lies beyond. Some leave, walking into the Wane in search of trials, answers, or atonement.

A few return. Most do not.

---

### 🔹 The Name Itself

> *“Uncrowned” was once a slur. A term spat by Covenant soldiers to mock the aimless.”*

But like many names in Eirion, it became a badge.

It means:
- You were *almost* something.
- You *could have* become everything.
- And now, in death, you must choose whether your story ends in silence—or in **truth, earned and unfinished**.

---

### 🔹 Known Figures

The Uncrowned include but are not limited to:
- **Peyton Bray** – The soldier who died believing in nothing
- **Robert Gould Shaw** – The martyr who died for men the world tried to forget
- **Charles Gordon** – The general who mistook obedience for purpose
- **Sophie Scholl** – The girl who died for words, and now watches what they become
- **Nathan Hale** – The boy who still believes one life is enough, if spent well

More arrive with each passing moment. Some ascend. Some vanish. Some fall.

---

### 🔹 Their Role in the War for Eirion

They are not a faction. They are not an army. They are a **question without an answer**.

Alexander fears them not because they are strong, but because they are **unfinished**. And **unfinished stories** are the most dangerous kind—because they still have the power to change.



## POINTS OF CONFLICT

### 1. **Purpose vs Action**

- **The Uncrowned** are contemplative. They ask: *What does my story mean? What was I supposed to become?*
- **The Last Cohort** are active. They say: *We don’t have time for that. We fight so others can decide who they are.*

**Tension:** The Uncrowned may see the Cohort as reckless or reactionary. The Cohort may see the Uncrowned as passive, even cowardly.

> *Scholl to Louverture:* “You act as if your cause justifies itself. But what if it’s another empire waiting to be born?” *Louverture to Scholl:* “And you wait for truth to come from a mirror while the world burns around it.”

---

### 2. **Means vs Ends**

- **The Last Cohort** is willing to use tactical deception, assassination, and sabotage.
- **The Uncrowned**, especially those like Gordon and Hale, may struggle with moral compromise.

**Tension:** Debates arise over whether victory justifies dishonorable methods. Shaw and Lannes might clash over battlefield ethics. Bray and Lewes could bond over moral ambiguity.

---

### 3. **Faith vs Secularism**

- Characters like **Gordon** (Uncrowned) and **Joan of Arc** (Last Cohort) may share religious conviction, but interpret it differently.
- Others, like Bray or Spartacus, are deeply skeptical of any divine will—seeing it as a tool of control.

**Tension:** Spiritual tension could threaten unity or even cause betrayal. One group might see the other's belief system as a weakness ripe for exploitation.

---

### 4. **Structure vs Identity**

- The **Cohort** has rank, structure, and a mission: resist and outlast the Covenant.
- The **Uncrowned** have no hierarchy—they’re individuals, chosen by thread and doubt.

**Tension:** The Cohort may pressure the Uncrowned to take action, to *commit*. The Uncrowned may resist becoming part of anyone’s banner again.

---

## 🤝 POINTS OF AGREEMENT

### 1. **Opposition to Tyranny**

- Both factions stand against **Alexander’s dominion**, even if for different reasons.
- Both understand the **danger of conquest framed as divinity**.

> *“We may not agree on what freedom means,”* Shaw might say, *“but we know what it does not look like.”*

---

### 2. **Respect for Sacrifice**

- Both groups are built from individuals who died **unfinished**, often for a cause.
- There’s mutual respect between **martyrs**, **rebels**, and **idealists**.

**Shared Values:**
- Memory matters.
- Death without choice is theft.
- Legacy must not be owned by tyrants.

---

### 3. **Need for Unity (Even If Temporary)**

- A greater threat—Alexander’s Covenant—requires **tactical alliances**.
- Characters like Shaw, Louverture, and Scholl could form bridges between the factions.
- A shared battle or loss could bind them, even if philosophical rifts remain.

---

### 4. **Recognition That “Truth” Is Still Being Written**

- Both sides acknowledge that their work isn’t done.
- They are united by the understanding that **history is not a closed book—but a living script**.
