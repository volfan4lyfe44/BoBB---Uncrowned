# Chapter 3: The Last Sentinel

### THE FAITHFUL SOLDIER

*"Justice being taken away, then, what are kingdoms but great robberies?"* — Augustine, *The City of God*, Book IV

*"Faith without works is dead."* — James 2:26

The city had been under siege for three hundred and seventeen days.

From the palace roof, he watched the sun set over the Nile for the last time. The waters reflected gold and crimson—beautiful and indifferent to the forty thousand souls trapped within the city walls. He had promised to save them all. Another promise he could not keep.

His Bible lay open beside his evening tea, the pages worn thin from desert heat and daily reading. Fifty-one years old, veteran of the Crimea and China, former governor of the Sudan, sent back to evacuate the city as the Mahdi's forces swarmed across the desert. He had sent away all he could—his staff, the servants, even the boy who brought him figs and asked about Heaven. But he had stayed. How could a man of honor do otherwise?

"You must not be here," he had said. "You must live. I must wait."

The messenger approached with the dispatch in trembling hands.

"The relief column?" the general asked.

"Two days away still, sir."

He nodded, unsurprised. Empire moved slowly, even when its servants were dying. He had held Khartoum for ten months, longer than anyone thought possible. Now, time had run out.

He stood on his balcony above a city cracking with hunger. It was quiet, the hush of waiting for what had already been decided. There were no screams, not yet.

In his hand, he held his final message. Not to the Queen. Not to Gladstone. Not to any man. To God. It read, *If I am taken . . . I will not raise my hand against them. I am dust. I am as You made me.*

They came at dawn, as he knew they would. Thousands of Mahdists pouring over the weakened defenses, their spears catching the first light. Below in the courtyard, his small guard assembled one final time.

He did not reach for his revolver. Instead, he straightened his uniform, brushed dust from the sleeve, and descended the stairs to the palace entrance. A British general would meet death standing, facing his foe.

As the palace doors splintered, he stood alone in his white uniform. Behind him, all of England's promises. Before him, the desert's fury.

The first spear struck him in the chest.

As he fell, the air changed—cooling, clarifying. The sounds of slaughter quieted. Leaves stirred, though no wind moved through the palace.

And somewhere in the space between heartbeats, he heard a voice speaking a tongue *older* than any he knew.

"Your post is not abandoned. It has only moved."

He turned. A man stood behind him, wrapped in light.

The Faithful Soldier smiled faintly.

*And in 1885, the city's gate was opened.*

*But the rescue came two days late.*

*Another flame gone to ash.* *Another legacy left unwritten.*

*Another chair at the table of the Uncrowned… filled.*
