# Book 1 Continuity and Fact Check

**Status:** Review notes, 10/16/2026. Companion to the [Book 1 plot outline](book-1-plot-outline.md).

This is the vetting pass behind the outline: every conflict, rule problem, and historical question found while reading the full manuscript (Chapters 1 to 7) against the Codex and planning notes. Nothing in the manuscript or Codex has been changed. Per [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md), these are notes for the author. Small wording fixes are listed at the end for a later polish pass.

Historical items marked **Confirmed** were checked against sources (listed at the bottom). Items marked **Verify** are from general knowledge and should be checked before publication.

## 1. Peyton Bray: timeline and background

The biggest continuity problem in the draft. The chapters and the Codex disagree with each other on almost every fact of Bray's service.

| Fact | Ch 5 | Ch 6 / Ch 7 | Codex |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year of death | 1970 | (none) | "circa 1968" |
| Age at death | 19 | (none) | "early 20s" |
| Time in Vietnam | "three months in-country" | Tate dies, then Bray "stayed. Through three more tours." | (none) |
| Home | (none) | "back in Georgia" (Ch 7) | Greeneville, Tennessee |
| Football position | linebacker | (none) | tight end |
| How he got to the Army | draft lottery on TV; turned down scholarships | "seventeen years old," holding Army letters with Tate (Ch 7) | drafted |
| After Tate's death | (none) | "stopped attending the grief groups, stopped answering the calls from home" (Ch 7) | (none) |
| Companion | Tate Cromwell (Ch 6, 7) | | "Will (Remnant)" in Relationships; Tate elsewhere |

**What can't work as written:**

- Three months in-country cannot hold Tate's wound, months of opium dependence, his death, and three more tours. A full tour was 12 months, so three more tours means at least three more years.
- No one received draft notices at 17. Registration was at 18.
- Grief groups and calls from home don't fit a soldier who never left Vietnam.
- The televised draft lottery was held 12/01/1969, so a lottery draft points to induction in 1970, not 1968. (Verify.)
- A college scholarship would have brought a student deferment in those years, so "turned them down after the draft" needs a reason. (Verify the deferment rules for 1969 and 1970.)

**Recommended fix (the author's call):** Give Bray a reason to give up the deferment that also deepens his guilt. **He turned down the scholarship to go with Tate.** Tate had no deferment coming; Bray chose to keep their promise ("We stay together"). Then:

- Drafted together in 1968 at 18 or 19, before the lottery. Ch 5 loses the lottery line.
- In Vietnam in 1968. Tate is wounded near Phu Bai (fits the fighting around Hue that year), refuses a medical discharge, and declines over months.
- After Tate dies, Bray extends his tour instead of going home. Change "three more tours" to "extended, then extended again."
- Dies in 1970 at about 21. Ch 5 changes "Nineteen years old, three months in-country." Codex changes "circa 1968" to 1970.
- Ch 7: "seventeen" becomes "eighteen" or "nineteen"; the two letters become Tate's draft notice and Bray's scholarship offer, so the scene shows the choice. Reframe the grief groups as letters from home he stopped answering.
- Pick one home state. Tennessee fits the recruiting scenes with the University of Tennessee and Wake Forest.
- Pick one position. Ch 6 also has him "sinking a last-second winning shot," which reads as basketball. Fine if he played both; otherwise cut.
- Replace "Will (Remnant)" in the Codex with Tate Cromwell.

The alternative is to keep Ch 5 exactly as is (19, 1970, three months) and compress Tate's whole story into Bray's first three months. That loses the slow erosion Ch 7 does so well. Not recommended.

## 2. Rule conflicts: magic and unexplained mysteries

The world guide says the crossing is the only supernatural element and that every other mystery needs a grounded answer. These passages still break that rule.

**Manuscript**

| Where | Problem | Suggested fix |
|---|---|---|
| Ch 7, Scene 1 | Cromwell's dog tag appears in the blanket: "He hadn't brought it with him. He was sure of that." Reads as supernatural. | Bray wore it on his own chain. The chain broke in his sleep. Keep his certainty in Ch 7 and reveal the truth in Ch 11 (outline). |
| Ch 6, Scene 2 | The feeling of being watched "from underneath," "as if the land itself were listening." | Fine as a feeling if it is later explained: the plain-watchers. |
| Ch 7, Scene 2 | "As though even Eirion struggled to decide whether he belonged here." | Fine as figurative, as long as nothing literal follows. |
| Ch 3, closing | "And in 1885, the gate was opened. But no one crossed back through it." Uses "gate" for arrival, which may confuse readers once the Gate of Still Flame is the way out. | Consider a different word, or keep it as deliberate irony and note that choice. |

**Codex** (leftovers from the earlier fantasy version)

- **"Echoes"** in the Gordon, Hale, and Sophie entries are magical manifestations: Gordon's "Echoes stand firm, but do not advance unless Gordon truly believes"; Hale's "power grows through symbolic resonance"; Sophie's Echoes "take the form of whispers, graffiti, or half-formed voices," and her presence makes Covenant Echoes "hesitate, defect, or fracture." Recommend redefining as ordinary followers (native or Eternal) or cutting.
- **Sophie's "quill-etched bone knife ... earned through remembered truth."** Keep the knife, drop "earned through."
- **Jackson's banner** "burns, a flag of fire ... impossible to extinguish." Make it a red or fire-sigil banner.
- **Alexander's "thread too powerful to cut"** and the Uncrowned's "threads of fate." Already flagged in the world guide as figurative. Fine if kept that way.
- **Uncrowned entry: "More arrive with each passing moment."** Conflicts with arrivals being rare (outline rule R2).

## 4. Historical accuracy in the prologue

| Chapter | Claim | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Ch 4 | "The third defendant, their professor" | **Confirmed error.** The third defendant on 02/22/1943 was Christoph Probst, a fellow student. Professor Kurt Huber was tried separately on 04/19/1943. |
| Ch 4 | "The trial had lasted exactly one hour" | **Verify.** Accounts generally describe a trial of several hours that morning, with execution the same afternoon. |
| Ch 4 | "In four days, she and her friends had distributed over a thousand leaflets" | **Verify.** The White Rose produced six leaflets over months. On 02/18/1943 Hans and Sophie scattered copies of the sixth at the university, where they were caught. |
| Ch 4 | Her question to the judge about standing before God | Not in the trial record as far as I know. Her documented words in court are different. Fine as fiction if the author is comfortable inventing them; worth a note in the author's afterword. |
| Ch 4 epigraph | "When tyranny is law, rebellion is duty..." attributed to Hannah Arendt | **No source found.** Appears only on uncited quote sites. A similar line is often misattributed to Thomas Jefferson. Recommend replacing. |
| Ch 3 | "Fifty-two years old" | **Error.** Gordon was born 01/28/1833 and died 01/26/1885, age 51. The Codex already says 51. |
| Ch 3 | "The messenger approached with the telegram" | **Verify.** The Khartoum telegraph line was cut early in the siege. Final messages went out by runner or steamer. "Dispatch" or "message" would be safer. |
| Ch 3 | "three hundred and seventeen days" | **Verify.** The siege is usually dated 03/13/1884 to 01/26/1885, about 319 days. |
| Ch 3 epigraph | "Empire without virtue is but dominion over sand..." attributed to Tennyson | **No source found.** Not on Wikiquote or in any Tennyson text the search reached. Recommend replacing. |
| Ch 2 | "the youngest colonel in the Union Army" | **Verify, likely wrong.** Shaw was 25. Several Union colonels were younger. Try "one of the youngest." |
| Ch 2 | "the first all-Black regiment in the North" | **Verify, likely wrong.** The 1st Kansas Colored Infantry and the 1st South Carolina Volunteers were raised earlier. The 54th was among the first raised in the North and the most famous. |
| Ch 2 | His sergeant catches the colors "from his falling hand" | **Verify.** The famous flag rescue was Sgt. William Carney's, who took the colors from a fallen color bearer, not from Shaw. Fine as fiction; consider "from the falling color bearer." |
| Ch 1 | A public hanging on a Sunday | **Confirmed.** 09/22/1776 was a Sunday. |
| Ch 1 | The friendly British captain who studied at Cambridge | **Verify.** The officer usually credited with kindness to Hale is Capt. John Montresor, an engineer. The provost marshal, William Cunningham, was known for cruelty. Fine as fiction; worth knowing. |
| Ch 5 epigraph | Chesterton: "The true soldier fights not because he hates..." | **Verify.** The first sentence is a real Chesterton line. The second sentence ("And sometimes, the truest courage...") does not appear to be his. Recommend ending the quotation after the first sentence. |
| Ch 1, 2 epigraphs | MacArthur (1962 West Point speech) and A. Philip Randolph | Both appear genuine. Verify wording against a primary source. |

**Note for the outline:** The proposed Ch 14 moment where Rommel tells Sophie her leaflet was dropped over Germany depends on a real event: a copy of the sixth leaflet was smuggled to Britain and dropped by the RAF in 1943. **Verify** before drafting. Whether Rommel would have known of it is uncertain; have him say he heard of it, not that he saw it.

## 5. Structure and pacing in Chapters 6 and 7

- **Two shelter sequences in Ch 6.** Scene 1 and Scene 2 each find a shelter, build a fire, and drink condensation. Consider merging so the second shelter adds only what is new (the artifacts, the hat, the note).
- **Two carved posts in Ch 7.** Scene 2 already has the wooden post with two scripts. Scene 4's outline repeats it. The outline proposes replacing Scene 4's discovery with the first sight of other people.
- **Solo stretch.** Bray is alone for roughly 8,500 words across two chapters before meeting anyone. That is long for a reader who has just met five strangers in the prologue. Consider trimming Ch 6 and 7 to about 6,000 words combined, so the Bastion arrives sooner. This is a suggestion only; the author decides.
- **Fire from two dry sticks** on a plain with almost no wood (Ch 6, Scene 1). Consider having him find tinder in the shelter, as he does in Scene 2.
- **Two dog tags** (Tate's in Scene 1, the empty grave's in Scene 2). The echo works if the second one is meant to pay off later. If not, cut it.
