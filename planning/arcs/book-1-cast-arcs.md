# Book 1 Cast Arcs

**Status:** Proposal, 10/16/2026. Goes deeper than the summary table in the [Book 1 plot outline](../book-1-plot-outline.md#4-character-arcs-in-book-1). Toussaint's decided beats live in his [arc notes](toussaint-louverture.md). The setting behind these arcs is in [Book 1 history and setting](../worldbuilding/book-1-history-and-setting.md).

Each arc answers five questions: what the character wants, what they fear, what they get wrong, what Book 1 forces them to face, and where they end up.

---

## The pattern under the cast: killed by your own side

Reading the Codex and prologue together, a pattern shows up that the author may want to use on purpose. Most of this cast was betrayed or abandoned by their own side:

| Character | How their own side failed them |
|---|---|
| **Bray** | Shot by a fellow American. |
| **Stonewall Jackson** | Shot by his own pickets. |
| **Rommel** | Forced to suicide by his own government. |
| **Toussaint** | Arrested under a promise of safety and left to die in a French prison. |
| **Joan** | Never ransomed by the king she crowned. |
| **Gordon** | Left in Khartoum while relief moved slowly. |
| **Shaw** | Buried in a mass grave, his men paid half wages by the army they served. |
| **Sophie** | Condemned by her own country's court. |

**Why it matters:** Bray thinks his death is uniquely absurd, a punchline. Book 1 can show him, a little at a time, that nearly everyone around him died the same way. That does not make his death mean more. It makes him less alone. It also gives Rommel his best line of attack in Ch 14 ("Our own side killed me too"), and sets up a later meeting between Bray and Jackson, two men killed by friendly fire who chose opposite roads.

## Peyton "Moose" Bray

**Want:** Answers, and then, once he has them, a reason to keep going.

**Fear:** Being alone. Everything else follows from it: the pact with Tate, letting Tate stay, extending his tour, and walking away from everyone in Eirion before they can leave him.

**What he gets wrong:** He thinks his problem is that he believes in nothing. His real problem is that he believes in one thing, staying with his people, and it has cost lives. Tate's, first.

**Book 1 forces him to face:**

1. He kept Tate in Vietnam because he could not stand to be there alone (Mirror Hall, Ch 23).
2. He was shot from his own line, and he has always known it (said aloud in Ch 29).
3. People here are not banners. He can stand with them without wearing one (village, rescue, vote).

**Belief track:**

| Chapter | Where Bray is |
|---|---|
| 6, 7 | Moving to keep from thinking. "Away from what I left." |
| 9 | Disappoints the watcher, who thought he was a god. Lets them guide him anyway. Accepts help for the first time. |
| 10, 11 | Refuses to carve his name. Learns about the dog tag he has worn all along. |
| 14 | Rommel's offer tempts him because it is honest about war. He says neither yes nor no. |
| 15 | Sees the sun, and a village. Recognizes the village. |
| 19 | Acts. Kills a man who was just following orders, like he did. |
| 21 | Owns what he did in front of everyone. |
| 23 | Mirror Hall. Sees Tate alive, in the life where Bray let him go. |
| 26 | Volunteers, "Not for a banner." |
| 29 | Says the truth about his death to Lewes. |
| 30 | Carries a dead man through the haze, as he carried Tate. This time he knows it, and sets him down. |
| 35 | Carves his name. Peyton, not Moose. |

**Ends Book 1:** Committed to people, not causes. Still afraid of being alone, but he knows it now.

## Toussaint Louverture

All beats below build on the [decided arc](toussaint-louverture.md).

**Want:** A free people with a structure that keeps them free after the war.

**Fear:** Disorder: freedom bleeding out into the sand, as the Codex has him say.

**What he gets wrong (proposed deepening):** He believes the structure needs him at its center. In 1801 he wrote a constitution naming himself governor for life (verify). He has spent 167 years in Eirion being the one who holds the Cohort together. That is the same shape as Napoleon's ambition, smaller and kinder.

**Book 1 forces him to face it, through Napoleon:** In Ch 25, Napoleon's best argument is not the seat. It is the mirror: "You made yourself governor for life. You are me without the crown." It is partly true, and Toussaint knows it.

**How the vote answers it:** He votes to bring in the Uncrowned because they refuse every crown, including any he might reach for. He is building a check on himself.

**Also new:** He goes to the parley expecting to be taken (Ch 22). He has read the careful wording of Napoleon's letter, which promises safety to the captives and says nothing about him. He bets on Napoleon's vanity: Napoleon will want to talk before he does anything else, and talk buys Lewes time. He tells only Lewes. In captivity he is still working: reading the fort, the guards, and the clerk.

**Ends Book 1:** Freed, owing his life to Lannes and to people he called passive. He has a clue, and a new piece of self-knowledge he will not say aloud yet.

## Napoleon Bonaparte

**Want:** To sit at the center of the pantheon, not beneath Alexander.

**Fear:** Being a lesser god in someone else's story, as at Saint Helena, outlived by his own legend.

**What he gets wrong:** He thinks everyone has a price, and that loyalty is a form of admiration he can win.

**Book 1 deepening:**

- **The guns.** He was an artilleryman. Eirion took away his weapon. Every battle here is fought with a hand tied. That makes his powder work personal, not just ambitious. (Ch 13)
- **He keeps his word to the letter.** He releases the captives exactly as promised, then takes Toussaint, whom the letter never mentioned. He is not a liar. He is a lawyer of promises. (Ch 24)
- **He wants Toussaint, not his death.** Toussaint is the only commander who has beaten him in Eirion. Napoleon would rather own that talent than fight it twice. (Ch 25)
- **The letters from Fort de Joux.** Toussaint asks whether he ever read them. Recommended answer: "I read the first." It is worse than silence.
- **Lannes.** In Ch 31 he realizes how Toussaint read him so well in the campaign: someone who knew his habits was advising the Marshal. The defeat and the betrayal become one wound.

**Ends Book 1:** He tells Alexander nothing about Lannes, doubles the powder work, and is more dangerous for having been hurt.

## Jean Lannes

**Want:** To stop his oldest friend from becoming what he warned him about.

**Fear:** Facing Napoleon and finding he still loves him enough to go back.

**Motive (proposed):** Lannes died after Aspern-Essling in 1809, and later accounts claim he reproached Napoleon for his endless wars (disputed; verify before using). He arrived in Eirion and found Alexander's empire, the thing he feared Napoleon becoming, already built. He joined the Cohort. When Napoleon arrived twelve years later and chose Alexander, Lannes asked for every posting where the eagle wasn't. For 150 years the Covenant kept Napoleon in the east, so it was easy.

**The hidden role (proposed):** In this campaign Lannes advises Toussaint on how Napoleon thinks. "He will go around. He always goes around." Toussaint beats Napoleon partly with Napoleon's own best friend's knowledge. This is what makes the reveal in Ch 31 cut twice.

**Why he leads the rescue against orders:** Toussaint ordered him to stay away to protect his secret. Lannes goes because he owes Toussaint the campaign, and because he is tired of hiding from the one man he cannot stop thinking about.

**Votes no** in Ch 34: the Uncrowned are not soldiers, and now that Napoleon knows him, the Cohort will be hunted harder. He will not march people who don't know why they fight.

**Ends Book 1:** Exposed. Book 2 opens with Napoleon trying to win him back.

## Spartacus

**Want:** No more chains, anywhere, for anyone.

**Fear (proposed):** That two thousand years of dead cohorts died for nothing. He cannot stop, because stopping would make it true.

**What he gets wrong:** He believes any structure becomes a cage, including Toussaint's. He respects Toussaint and fears what the Cohort will become if it wins.

**His history with the Bastion (proposed):** He built it to mourn, and left because mourning freed no one. Every Uncrowned soul is, to him, someone who chose his first mistake.

**Book 1 beats:** Wants to strike in Ch 8. Held in reserve all through Ch 16 and 17. Then gets the charge that breaks the line, at the moment Toussaint picks. The partnership works when Spartacus trusts the timing. Calls the parley a trap (Ch 22). Leads the diversion at the rescue (Ch 30). Votes no (Ch 34).

**Seed for Book 3:** The Cohort crosses the Gate first. For Spartacus, that will mean finally being allowed to stop.

## Joan of Arc

**Want:** That no one is abandoned the way she was.

**Fear:** That her faith was always just a tool, and she is still using it.

**What she gets wrong:** Native volunteers believe she is holy, and she lets them because it keeps them brave. It is the same thing Alexander does with his godhood, on a smaller scale. She knows.

**Book 1 beats:** Rallies a breaking wing in the battle (Ch 17). Forces the rescue in council: "No one came for me." (Ch 27) Leads the diversion with Spartacus. Votes yes.

**Seed:** A later conversation with Sophie or Bray about using belief.

## Jock Lewes

**Want:** To win without becoming the thing he fights.

**Fear:** His own inventiveness. In life he designed an explosive used to destroy aircraft on the ground (verify the details of the Lewes bomb). In Eirion he knows he could make powder, and he refuses.

**Role:** Runs the scouts and the network through the plain-watchers. The "J.L." and the bowline knot in Ch 7 are his. Plans the rescue. The cistern route and the ropes are his.

**Bond with Bray:** The only two 20th-century soldiers in the Cohort's war. They speak the same language of war. Bray tells him the truth about his death (Ch 29).

**Votes yes:** The Hall of Names is intelligence the Cohort has never had.

**Seed:** The pressure to make powder grows in Book 2, especially if Spartacus wants to arm the natives.

## Charles Gordon

**Want:** To do his duty, so that he never has to decide what his duty is.

**Fear:** That faith alone was never enough, and that he stayed in the Bastion for 85 years so he would not have to find out.

**What he gets wrong:** He treats the Accord as sacred because he was handed it, not because he ever judged it just.

**Book 1 beats:**

| Chapter | Beat |
|---|---|
| 7 | His note at the cairn shows he is already thinking of leaving. |
| 15 | Healer and trader in the village. Gentle, and good at it. |
| 19 | Will not raise his hand, as at Khartoum. |
| 21 | Receives Jackson's letter. |
| 23 | Bray, back from the Mirror Hall, tells him he will go if Gordon asks, but not to be forgiven. Gordon refuses Jackson: "I have kept this Accord for sixty years because I was told to. No one ever asked whether it was just." |
| 30 | Raises his hand to protect the watcher. His first blow in a hundred years. |
| 35 | Leaves the Bastion with the alliance. |

**Seed:** He has never entered the Mirror Hall. The Codex hints at a suppressed part of his identity. Keep it unspoken in Book 1.

## Robert Gould Shaw

**Want:** That no one under his care dies forgotten.

**Fear:** That his belief got his men killed.

**Book 1 beats:**

- Greets Bray at the gate (Ch 9).
- Gives Bray the Codex line in Ch 15 or 21: "You can't stop believing just because belief hurt you."
- First to back Bray after the village. Opens the Bastion to the refugees.
- Loses his sergeant in the rescue (see below), so his own line is tested.
- Stays as keeper (Ch 35), still believing.

**The sergeant (proposed new character):** In Ch 2, "one of his sergeants" catches the colors from Shaw's hand. Make that sergeant fictional and named by the author. He died later that night on the fort, crossed in 1863, and has followed Shaw ever since. He served in the Bastion, then joined Lewes's scouts when Hale left. See "The rescue's cost" below.

**Note:** The author has a PR coming that fixes prologue continuity. If Ch 2 changes this moment (for example, to the historical color bearer), this idea needs to change with it.

## Sophie Scholl

**Want:** That truth is heard and answered for.

**Fear:** That words without weapons are forgotten, and that she died for a gesture.

**Book 1 beats:**

- **Ch 11:** Asks Bray what he died for. Won't accept "nothing."
- **Ch 14:** Faces Rommel. He tells her, as something he heard, that her last leaflet reached Britain and was dropped over Germany by British planes (verify). Her words were carried by the enemy of her enemy. It shakes her fear and complicates it.
- **Ch 21:** Makes Bray say plainly what he did. Accountability, not punishment.
- **Ch 26:** Asks Bray whether the rescue is atonement or a choice.
- **Ch 34:** Sets her condition: the alliance answers to the natives it fights among.

**Ends Book 1:** Joins the alliance as its conscience, by her own terms.

## Nathan Hale

**Want:** To give his life for something again, and have it be enough this time.

**Fear:** That the first time was not enough.

**What he gets wrong:** He is in love with sacrifice. That is the danger the Codex names: "a martyr twice lost."

**Book 1 beats:**

- Left the "Keep walking" note and the tricorn on the trail (Ch 6), a custom he started for newcomers.
- Fights well in the battle.
- Brings the rescue request (Ch 26). His Ch 1 memory, Washington asking who would volunteer, is echoed when Bray steps up.
- In the rescue he moves to hold the stair alone. The sergeant stops him: "You already did this once, son." The sergeant holds it instead and dies. Hale lives, saved from his second martyrdom by someone else's.

**Ends Book 1:** Alive, shaken, and owing a debt he does not know how to carry. Charles XII's pull on him in Book 2 is now dangerous.

---

## Erwin Rommel

**Want:** Order he can be competent inside.

**Book 1 role:** The Covenant's envoy to the Bastion (Ch 14), and the Strategos sent to review Napoleon's failure (Ch 32).

**Pitch to Bray:** "You were a good soldier in a war your country lied about. So was I. Our own side killed us both. Here the war is at least honest about what it is."

**Ch 32 seed:** He smells the sulfur in the Watch-Fort. He says nothing to Napoleon and writes nothing to Alexander. Why is the author's call for Book 2. The Codex says he is the general most likely to break.

---

## Stonewall Jackson

**Offstage.** Guarantor of the Accord. His letter to Gordon (Ch 21) is courteous, scriptural, and absolute. Plants the Gordon and Jackson letters subplot from the Codex.

## New characters the plot needs

These are proposals. Names, genders, and details are the author's call. These notes use "they" until the author decides.

### The watcher

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | A young plain-watcher, Bray's age in looks, from a family that has watched the plain for generations. |
| **Want** | To do their duty: bring the god safely to the house of the open gate. |
| **Arc** | Begins by treating Bray as a god. Bray is hungry, frightened, and mortal, and the watcher is visibly disappointed. Ends by helping him anyway, as a man. The first native in the series to see an Eternal One plainly. |
| **Key beats** | Shadows and guides Bray (Ch 7, 9). Teaches him the haze paths and a few words (Ch 15). Loses a younger sibling to the Covenant in the village raid (Ch 19), who comes back in the exchange (Ch 24). Guides the rescue, but only with Bray (Ch 26, 29). Gordon protects them in the fort (Ch 30). |
| **Gift** | Gives Bray their people's word for one who watches the plain. Bray does not understand what it means yet. In Book 3 he becomes a watcher of another threshold. |

### The sergeant

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Shaw's color sergeant from Ch 2 (fictional). In Eirion since 1863. One of Lewes's scouts. |
| **Role** | The rescue's cost. |
| **Bond with Bray** | Built in Ch 26 and 29. Two men from armies that sent Black and poor men to fight. They talk about home, and about who catches the flag. |
| **Death** | Holds the inner stair in the Watch-Fort so the others can get out. The flag never touched the ground; this time he is the one who doesn't. Bray carries him out of the haze before he understands the man is already dead. |
| **Alternative** | If the author prefers no link to Ch 2, any member of Lewes's team introduced by Ch 8 works. Do not use Hale or the watcher (reasons in the outline, decision 5). |

### The taken arrival

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | The bound figure on the Finders' led horse in Ch 7. A new Eternal One taken off the plain days before Bray could have met them. |
| **Role** | A thread that runs the length of Book 1. Bray sees them taken (Ch 7). Toussaint meets them in the Watch-Fort's cells, waiting to be "sent east" (Ch 25). In the rescue, Toussaint refuses to leave them, which slows the escape and costs the sergeant his life (Ch 30). Their testimony at the vote confirms what "sent east" means to the Covenant (Ch 34). |
| **Identity** | The author's call. The cast so far is mostly European and American. This is a natural place to widen it, with a fictional figure or a historical one who died close to 1970. A real person who died that recently should be handled with care, or avoided. |

### The clerk

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | A native clerk in the Watch-Fort, from the Covenant heartland, raised to believe the Eternal Ones are gods. |
| **Turn** | Watches Napoleon's chemists making forbidden fire, and starts to doubt. Toussaint, who was a coachman and steward before he was a general, speaks to him as an equal. No god has done that before. |
| **Role** | Shows Toussaint the transfer ledger with "crossed" (Ch 28). Unbars the cistern door during the rescue (Ch 30). |
| **Fate** | The author's call. If he stays behind, Napoleon will find out. If he comes with them, the alliance gains a man who can read Covenant records. |

### The river lord

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | Head of the strongest marches clan. |
| **Role** | Makes the alliance that wins the battle (Ch 12, 16, 17). The price: the Cohort swears it will rule no one in the marches after the war. |
| **Why it matters** | Gives the natives political weight on the page, in the way the reference series give every side its own politics. Answers part of Toussaint's question, "What do we do after we win?": we leave. |

### The colonel

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | An Eternal cavalry officer of Napoleon's era, serving in his army. |
| **Role** | Commands the detachment that clears the village (Ch 19). His order is the one Bray hears in Captain Thompson's voice: hit whatever you see. Respects Gordon by reputation, and tells him the Accord gives him no voice outside the walls. |
| **Why an Eternal** | Bray can only understand Eternal speech (rule R4). The order has to reach him in words. |
