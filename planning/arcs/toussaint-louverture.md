# Toussaint Louverture: Character Arc

Working arc notes. Built from the author's answers on 10/15/2026 and the existing Codex entries. Nothing here is final.

## Author's direction

- He is always the voice of reason to Spartacus.
- He is the deciding vote on whether the Last Cohort and the Uncrowned work together.
- A "Frenchman vs. legacy Frenchman" conflict with Napoleon: a what-if matchup between two strong tacticians who never met in battle.
- Born enslaved in Saint-Domingue, he educated himself and rose to general and governor at a time when former slaves had almost no path upward. Wit, cunning, hard work, and determination got him there.

## The core of the arc

His own Codex summary says he fights "for the republic that never gave him a seat." Napoleon's heresy in the Covenant is that every great leader is divine and deserves a seat at the table.

So the arc can turn on one temptation: **the man who denied Toussaint his seat in life offers him one in death.** Toussaint, who distrusts absolute power and asks "What do we do after we win?", has to decide whether structure and recognition are worth serving an empire.

## Why the Napoleon conflict works

Both men claim the legacy of the French Revolution.

- Toussaint fought under the French Republic after it abolished slavery, and governed Saint-Domingue in France's name.
- Napoleon brought back slavery in the French colonies, sent an army to retake Saint-Domingue, and had Toussaint arrested and shipped to a prison in France, where he died.
- Toussaint wrote to Napoleon from prison. As far as the record shows, Napoleon never answered. This lines up with a Cohort line already in the Codex: "They write letters home no one reads."

The French thread can run wider than these two. Jean Lannes, one of Napoleon's marshals and closest friends, and Joan of Arc, France's oldest symbol, are both in the Cohort. Napoleon faces his own country's ideals on the other side of the field.

## Historical anchors (verify before using)

These are from general knowledge. Confirm dates and details with a reliable biography before they go in the manuscript.

- Born enslaved around 1743 in Saint-Domingue (now Haiti).
- Joined the revolt in the early 1790s. Fought for Spain at first, then switched to France in 1794 after France abolished slavery.
- Known for guerrilla tactics, use of terrain, alliance-making, and diplomacy with Britain, Spain, and the United States.
- Issued a constitution in 1801 naming himself governor for life while still professing loyalty to France.
- In 1802, Napoleon sent the Leclerc expedition. Napoleon restored slavery in the French colonies that same year.
- Arrested in 1802 after being invited to a meeting under a promise of safety, then sent to Fort de Joux in the Jura Mountains.
- Died there on 04/07/1803. Haiti declared independence on 01/01/1804, after his death.

## Decisions (10/16/2026)

- **Toussaint and Napoleon meet in person**, briefly, with Toussaint as Napoleon's captive.
- **Jean Lannes leads the rescue.** In doing so he reveals himself to Napoleon, who never knew his close friend was in Eirion, let alone fighting against him.
- **The alliance vote lands at the end of Book 1.**
- **The capture and rescue also move to the end of Book 1 (decided 10/16/2026).** While captive, Toussaint learns hints of the thing Alexander guards and fears most. That mystery carries into Book 2.

## Why the capture and rescue work

- **It echoes his real death.** In life, Toussaint was lured to a meeting under a promise of safety, arrested, and left to die in a French prison. Being Napoleon's prisoner again puts him back in that cell, and this time someone comes for him.
- **It is the right setting for the offer.** Napoleon offering the seat to a man in chains makes the temptation sharper and the refusal mean more.
- **It gives Napoleon two wounds in one scene.** He loses his prisoner, and he learns that Lannes, his friend, has chosen the other side. Napoleon's Codex entry says he "has no patience for martyrdom." Lannes choosing a cause over him is something he cannot dismiss.
- **It binds Toussaint and Lannes.** The Codex already calls Lannes the balancing force between Toussaint's politics and Spartacus's passion. After the rescue, that balance is personal.

Historical anchors for Lannes (verify before using): one of Napoleon's earliest marshals and among the few who spoke to him with blunt familiarity. Mortally wounded at Aspern-Essling in 1809. Napoleon is reported to have been deeply shaken by his death. Lannes died 12 years before Napoleon, so he would have been in Eirion long before Napoleon arrived.

## Possible beats

### Book 1

1. **Introduction at the war council.** Spartacus wants to strike. Toussaint asks what happens after the strike. The Cohort follows Spartacus, but waits for Toussaint to speak.
2. **He learns Napoleon commands in the Covenant.** He keeps it to himself. Discipline over vengeance. Spartacus would want to charge.
3. **First test of the Uncrowned.** He sizes up Shaw and Bray. He sees Shaw as someone who could build "new order without new empire," and Bray as important but lost.
4. **The what-if battle.** A Covenant force under Napoleon meets the Cohort. Toussaint wins by refusing Napoleon's kind of battle: terrain, retreat, patience, and allies. Napoleon now knows who he is facing. Lannes stays out of Napoleon's sight.
5. **The capture.** Napoleon takes Toussaint, possibly through a parley or a promise of safety, mirroring his real arrest.
6. **The meeting and the offer.** Their first face-to-face conversation. Napoleon offers him a seat among the Covenant's gods. Toussaint refuses, in chains, with no rescue in sight.
7. **The hint.** In captivity, Toussaint overhears or uncovers signs of something Alexander guards and fears above all else.
8. **The rescue and the reveal.** Lannes leads the mission. Napoleon sees his old friend fighting against him for the first time.
9. **The vote (decided: end of Book 1).** Toussaint returns and casts the deciding vote. The Cohort and the Uncrowned band together to uncover what he learned in captivity.

### Book 2

1. **Aftermath.** Napoleon is shaken by Lannes, and possibly more dangerous. Toussaint owes Lannes his freedom.
2. **The quests.** The alliance hunts for the truth behind what Alexander fears. Toussaint's hint is their first lead.
3. **The Gate revealed.** By the end of Book 2, the Gate is understood as a way to move on. Toussaint's question, "What do we do after we win?", finds its answer.

### Book 3

1. **The Cohort crosses first,** with Toussaint's support. See [Gate of Still Flame notes](../worldbuilding/gate-of-still-flame-notes.md).

## Questions for the author

- **Lannes's motive:** Why did Lannes join the Cohort instead of waiting for his friend? Did he see what Napoleon became, or did he choose before Napoleon arrived?
- **Hiding:** How has Lannes stayed hidden from Napoleon until the rescue: a different name, staying away from battles against Napoleon's forces, or something else?
- **After the reveal:** Does Napoleon try to win Lannes back, or treat him as the worst kind of traitor?
