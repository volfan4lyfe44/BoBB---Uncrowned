# Book 1 Plot Outline: The Bastion of Broken Banners

**Status:** Proposal, drafted 10/16/2026. Nothing here is final until the author approves it.

This outline builds a full Book 1 plot from what already exists: the seven drafted chapters, the Codex, the [series outline](series-outline.md), the [Toussaint arc notes](arcs/toussaint-louverture.md), the [Gate notes](worldbuilding/gate-of-still-flame-notes.md), and the [Eirion world guide](worldbuilding/eirion-world-guide.md). The companion [continuity and fact check](book-1-continuity-check.md) lists every conflict found in the current material and how this outline resolves it.

**How to read the tags:**

- **[Decided]** The author has already decided this. The outline builds on it unchanged.
- **[Drafted]** Already on the page in the manuscript.
- **[Proposed]** New. The author should accept, change, or cut it.

The author writes the book. This is a scaffold to argue with, not prose to adopt.

## 1. The book in one paragraph

Peyton "Moose" Bray, a drafted soldier killed in Vietnam, wakes alone on a grey plain in Eirion and walks until he finds the Bastion of Broken Banners, a neutral fortress where the Uncrowned sit with their unfinished lives. Down in the marches below the plain, Toussaint Louverture, the Last Cohort's strategist, outfights a Covenant army commanded by Napoleon Bonaparte. Napoleon's retreat spills onto the native villages at the plain's edge, and Bray, who died on a mission to clear a village, watches the same thing happen from the other side. He acts, and breaks the old Accord that kept the Bastion out of the war. Napoleon baits Toussaint into a parley under a promise of safety, as he did in life, and takes him. In captivity Toussaint refuses Napoleon's offer of a seat among the Covenant's gods, and learns the first hint of the thing Alexander guards and fears most. Jean Lannes leads the rescue, with Bray at his side, and reveals himself to Napoleon. Toussaint comes home and casts the deciding vote: the Last Cohort and the Uncrowned will stand together.

## 2. The dramatic question and theme

- **Book 1 question:** Is it possible to commit to something again without becoming what you fought against?
- **Theme:** A seat at the table versus a place at the side of others. Napoleon offers seats. Alexander offers eternity. The Bastion offers a place to sit and wait. Bray and Toussaint each choose, at real cost, to stand with people instead.
- **Why this question fits the series:** Book 2 asks where the alliance is going (the quests), and Book 3 asks when to stop (the Gate). Book 1 has to answer the earlier question: whether to start at all.

## 3. Ground rules this outline assumes

These are the world-logic rules the plot depends on. Several are not yet decided. Each one is the simplest answer that keeps the crossing as the only supernatural element **[Decided]** and gives every mystery a grounded payoff **[Decided]**.

| # | Rule | Status | Why the plot needs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Time runs the same on Eirion as on Earth, and souls arrive in the order they died.** Alexander arrived about 2,300 years before Bray. Lannes arrived 12 years before Napoleon. | Proposed. Matches the existing notes ("Lannes would have been in Eirion long before Napoleon"; Gordon has spent "decades" in the Bastion). | Explains how Alexander's empire is old enough to be worshipped, why the Bastion is built from many eras, and why it is the *Last* Cohort. |
| R2 | **Eternal Ones are rare.** A handful cross each year across the whole grey plain, not thousands. | Proposed. The Uncrowned entry says "more arrive with each passing moment." Recommend softening that line. | Makes every arrival worth fighting over. Creates the race to reach newcomers first. |
| R3 | **Armies are mostly native.** A few Eternal Ones command; native soldiers fill the ranks. The Covenant runs a native empire. The Cohort draws on escaped and free natives. | Proposed. Fits the world guide. | Without it, no faction has enough people to fight a war. It also makes every battle cost natives, which feeds Bray's Book 3 position. |
| R4 | **The crossing gives Eternal Ones a shared understanding of one another's speech and writing. It does not cover native languages.** | Proposed. Already implied on the page: Hale hears "a language unspoken but fully known" (Ch 1), and Bray reads Spartacus's inscription as "not a language, but an articulation" (Ch 6). | Lets Toussaint and Napoleon talk, and lets Bray read Spartacus. Keeps a language wall between Eternal Ones and natives, as in Vietnam. |
| R5 | **What is worn crosses; what is held does not.** Clothes, boots, dog tags, and rings come through. A rifle in the hands does not. | Proposed. | R6 | **Eternal Ones eat, sleep, bleed, and heal normally. Killed is final.** | Decided in substance ([Eternal Ones entry](../codex/lore/the-eternal-ones.md)). | Bray starves in Ch 7. A death in the rescue has to be permanent to matter. |
| R7 | **The grey plain is a high, cold plateau under a permanent mineral haze.** Thin air, no visible sun, little food. Armies cannot live on it for long. | Proposed. Grounds what Bray already experiences: harsh air, breathlessness, cold, no sun. | No army can hold the plain, so no faction controls arrivals. Small parties race to find newcomers instead. Bray's first sight of the sun below the haze becomes a strong beat. |
| R8 | **The Bastion Accord.** Long ago the Bastion's keepers and Alexander agreed: the Uncrowned take no part in the war, and the Covenant does not enter the Bastion. | Proposed. Grounds the Codex line that the Bastion is "the only place in Eirion where one is not pursued." | Alexander keeps it because it serves him: the Bastion holds would-be enemies in reflection. | R9 | **Alexander suppresses innovation, not just powder.** | Proposed. Extends the decided powder ban. | Explains why Eirion is still medieval after 2,000 years of arrivals who knew more. |

**Two more grounded answers the plot relies on (proposed):**

- **Who built the Bastion?** Spartacus. The inscription Bray finds in Ch 6 ends: "This will be my eternal vigil. A monument to the failures of men." That reads as the founding idea of a fortress hung with broken banners. Spartacus built the first walls to mourn, then left because mourning freed no one, and founded the first cohort. Later arrivals built on what he left. This gives Spartacus a personal reason to distrust people who stay.
- **Why "Last" Cohort?** Spartacus has raised cohort after cohort over two thousand years, and Alexander has destroyed each one. This is the one that is still standing. The name carries the whole history in one word.

## 4. Character arcs in Book 1

### Peyton "Moose" Bray (primary POV)

| | |

| **Starts** | Alone, walking. "I don't know if I'm walking toward something or just away from what I left behind." **[Drafted, Ch 7 outline]** |
| **Believes (the lie)** | Promises are a noose you tie yourself (Ch 7). Every banner turns into a mission to clear somebody's village. Safer to follow nothing. |
| **Wound** | Tate Cromwell. Bray let Tate refuse a medical discharge because he did not want to be alone, and Tate died of it. Secondary wound: he was killed by his own side, and has not admitted it. |
| **Wants** | Answers: where he is, what happened, what any of it was for. |
| **Needs** | To act for people without needing a banner, and to admit the selfish part of what he did to Tate. |
| **Ends Book 1** | Has chosen to stand with others, on his own terms. Carves his name in the Hall of Names. Not healed. |
| **Seeds for later books** | His bond with the native plain-watchers (Book 3 non-interference). His habit of holding on to people (Book 3 Watcher of the Gate, whose job is letting them go). The friendly-fire truth, admitted but not yet faced. |

**Why the Book 3 ending needs this arc:** In life Bray could not let one friend go home. In Book 3 he becomes the one who lets everyone go home. Book 1 must show the flaw clearly enough that the ending pays it off.

### Toussaint Louverture (second POV)

All beats come from the [arc notes](arcs/toussaint-louverture.md) **[Decided]**. This outline adds the reasons and the order.

- **Starts:** The Cohort's voice of reason. Distrusts the Uncrowned as one more kind of inaction.
- **Pressure:** Napoleon, the man who denied him a seat in life, offers him one in death.
- **Choice:** Refuses the seat in chains. Then votes to bring in the Uncrowned, because people who refuse every crown are the Cohort's best defense against becoming another empire. The man who refused one seat votes to make sure no one in his alliance builds one.
- **Proposed addition:** He walks into the parley knowing it may be a trap, because captives' lives are on the table. That makes him principled, not careless, and it makes his capture a mirror of 1802 rather than a repeat of the same mistake.

## 5. Structure

**Length target:** About 105,000 to 115,000 words, 35 chapters. The prologue stays short (about 2,400 words). Main chapters average about 3,500 words.

**Point of view:** Bray carries about 55 percent. Toussaint carries about 35 percent. Three short interludes (two Napoleon, one Lannes) carry the rest. The prologue already uses five viewpoints, so readers are prepared for more than one.

**Parts:** Part 1 and Part 2 titles are the author's. Parts 3 to 5 have working titles only.

| Part | Chapters | Act | Ends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Prologue, The Last Breath | 1 to 5 | Prologue | Bray's name is written among the Uncrowned. **[Drafted]** |
| 2: The Bastion of Broken Banners | 6 to 14 | Act I (setup) | Bray leaves the Bastion with Gordon. |
| 3: Working title, The Grey March | 15 to 20 | Act II, first half | The village. Bray breaks the Accord. (Midpoint) |
| 4: Working title, A Seat at the Table | 21 to 28 | Act II, second half | Toussaint is a prisoner; Bray volunteers for the rescue. |
| 5: Working title, The Vote | 29 to 35 | Act III | The vote, and Bray carves his name. |

### The two threads on one map

The plot works because every thread is about the same piece of ground: the passes between the grey plain and the marches below it.

- Whoever holds the passes catches new arrivals before they reach the Bastion or the Cohort.
- Alexander sends Napoleon to take them **[Proposed]**. That is why the campaign happens now.
- Toussaint denies Napoleon the passes. Napoleon's retreat falls on the villages at the plain's edge, which is where Bray is.
- Napoleon then holds Toussaint in a fort guarding one of those passes. Only native paths through the haze reach it unseen, and Bray has walked them.

| Beat | Bray thread | Toussaint thread |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Arrival, Bastion, Accord, Rommel's pitch | War council, Napoleon's army, Lannes keeps away |
| Rising | Down to the native village | The battle; Napoleon retreats |
| **Midpoint** | The village is cleared; Bray acts | Arrives at the burned village; meets Bray (**threads touch**) |
| Pressure | Covenant demands Bray; Mirror Hall | Parley, capture, the offer, the hint |
| Climax | Rescue (**threads merge**) | Rescue; Lannes revealed |
| Resolution | Carves his name | Casts the deciding vote |

## 6. Chapter-by-chapter outline

Rough word counts are targets, not limits.

### Part 1: Prologue, The Last Breath

**Ch 1 to 5. [Drafted]** Hale, Shaw, Gordon, Scholl, and Bray at the moment of death. No structural changes proposed. Fact corrections are in the [continuity check](book-1-continuity-check.md).

### Part 2: The Bastion of Broken Banners

**Ch 6. The Grey Waking (Bray). [Drafted]** Wakes on the plain, finds Spartacus's inscription, the cave of artifacts, dreams of Tate, finds the stone map. Pacing note in the continuity check.

**Ch 7. Away From What I Left (Bray). [Scenes 1 to 3 drafted]**

- Scenes 1 to 3 as drafted.
- **Scene 4, proposed revision.** The outline's wooden post with two scripts already happens in Scene 2. Replace it with the "distant thunk" leading to his first sight of other people: a mounted Covenant party far off, crossing the plain with a bound figure on a led horse. A newly arrived Eternal, taken. Bray hides. He now knows two things: he is not the only one, and people here are hunted. This sets up the race for arrivals with no exposition.
- **Scene 5, as outlined.** The torn field manual, charcoal, the line "I don't know if I'm walking toward something or just away from what I left behind." The lone figure, watching. This is the native plain-watcher.

**Ch 8. The War Council (Toussaint), about 4,000 words.** Introduces the Cohort in the marches below the plain. Lewes reports a Covenant army moving toward the passes. Spartacus wants to strike the column on the road. Toussaint asks what happens after the strike, and the room waits for him. Joan and Lannes introduced. Lewes's scouts bring word of the army's standard: a golden eagle. Toussaint knows whose it is and keeps it to himself, choosing discipline over vengeance **[Decided beat]**. Lannes asks to take the far flank. Plant it; do not explain it.

**Ch 9. The Watcher on the Plain (Bray), about 3,500 words.** The figure shadows Bray, then steps in to steer him away from a Covenant search party. No common language: gestures, a drawn line in the dust, a shared bit of food. The watcher leads him to the edge of sight of the Bastion and will go no further. Bray walks the last stretch alone. The fortress as the Codex describes it. Shaw at the open gate: "Then you'd better come inside. We've been waiting for you."

**Ch 10. The Hall of Names (Bray), about 3,500 words.** Shaw shows him the Hall, lit by memory-light. The custom: you carve your own name when you are ready, and some scratch theirs out when they leave or lose faith. Bray will not carve his. Gordon introduced as the Bastion's steward. Bray learns the rules in conversation, not lecture: no aging, killed is final, the shared tongue, the Accord.

**Ch 11. The Quiet Courtyard (Bray), about 3,500 words.** Sophie introduced. She asks him what he died for and does not accept "nothing." The tricorn and "Keep walking" turn out to be Hale's, now gone to the Cohort. Bray learns the shape of the world: Covenant, Cohort, natives, the Bastion's trade with the plain-watchers for food. The dog tag: Bray finds his own chain under his shirt, broken, and realizes Tate's tag came off his own neck. He had worn it since the night Tate died.

**Ch 12. The Ground Napoleon Wants (Toussaint), about 4,000 words.** Napoleon advances to force a decisive battle. Toussaint will not give him one: he withdraws, strips the forage, and draws on native allies in the marches. Spartacus chafes. Hale introduced in the Cohort. Lannes, on the far flank, stays out of sight of the eagle.

**Ch 13. Interlude: The Eagle in the Marches (Napoleon), about 2,500 words.** Napoleon needs a clean victory to move toward the center of the Covenant. He is being bled instead. Shows his staff, his contempt for martyrdom, and a sealed wagon he lets no one near: powder. He learns his opponent's name. It is a name he once signed orders against.

**Ch 14. The Envoy (Bray), about 4,000 words.** Rommel comes to the Bastion under the Accord to meet new arrivals, as the Covenant is allowed to do. His pitch to Bray: you were a good soldier in a dishonest war; I was one too; here the war is honest. Sophie confronts him. Proposed moment: Rommel tells her that her last leaflet was carried to Britain and dropped over Germany by British planes. The enemy delivered her words. Bray gives no answer, which is its own answer for now. **Act I turn:** Gordon is walking down to the plain-watchers' village to trade and asks Bray to come. Bray leaves the Bastion.

### Part 3: The Grey March

**Ch 15. Below the Haze (Bray), about 3,500 words.** The descent off the plain. The air thickens. Bray sees the sun for the first time in Eirion. The plain-watchers' village: fields, children, the watcher again. Bray learns a few words. He sees what the Bastion gives the village (Gordon's medicine and knowledge) and what the village risks by guiding arrivals. A farming village at the edge of someone else's war. He knows exactly where he has seen this before.

**Ch 16 and 17. The Battle (Toussaint), about 7,500 words across two chapters.** The what-if battle **[Decided]**. Toussaint picks broken ground where Napoleon's concentrated columns cannot deploy. He makes Napoleon commit, cuts his supply line with native allies who know the country, and gives Spartacus his charge at the one moment it will break the line. Joan rallies a wavering wing. Hale fights well. Napoleon does not lose the army. He loses the campaign and retreats. Now he knows who he faces **[Decided]**. Lannes stays out of Napoleon's sight **[Decided]**, by a narrow margin.

**Ch 18. Interlude: Forage (Napoleon), about 2,000 words.** In retreat, Napoleon orders his columns to live off the villages at the plain's edge, and to punish any that fed or guided the Cohort. Lewes's network runs through the plain-watchers. He also sees a use for captives.

**Ch 19. The Clearing (Bray), about 4,500 words. MIDPOINT.** A Covenant detachment comes to the village. The officer's order is the same as Captain Thompson's in Ch 6: hit whatever you see. Gordon invokes the Accord and will not raise his hand, as at Khartoum. Bray acts. He gets villagers out by the haze paths the watcher showed him, and in the fight he kills a Covenant soldier, a native in Covenant service. Many escape. Some are taken, including people the watcher loves. The Accord is broken.

**Ch 20. Ashes (Toussaint), about 3,500 words.** The Cohort, following Napoleon's retreat, reaches the burned village. **The threads touch.** Toussaint sizes up Bray: important, still lost **[Decided beat]**. Hale and Gordon recognize each other. Bray asks Toussaint to help get the captives back. Toussaint asks him what he will do after. Bray has no answer. The Cohort moves on; Bray and Gordon take the refugees up to the Bastion.

### Part 4: A Seat at the Table

**Ch 21. The Broken Accord (Bray), about 4,000 words.** Refugees inside the Bastion, which has never sheltered natives. The Uncrowned divide. A letter arrives in Stonewall Jackson's hand, addressed to Gordon: hand over the man who killed a soldier of the Covenant, and the Accord stands; refuse, and it is over. Shaw backs Bray. Sophie makes Bray say plainly what he did and why, with no cover.

**Ch 22. Safe Conduct (Toussaint), about 3,500 words.** Napoleon offers a parley under safe conduct to exchange the captives from the village. Spartacus calls it a trap. Toussaint: "I know. I have been here before." He goes because the captives' lives are real, and he arranges for Lewes's scouts to shadow him. He orders Lannes to stay away.

**Ch 23. The Mirror Hall (Bray), about 4,000 words.** Bray enters the sealed chamber alone and sits with his reflection until he cannot stand it. Per the Codex, it shows possibility, not past **[Decided, grounded]**. What he finally admits to himself: the version of him who made Tate take the discharge and go home, and Tate alive in it. He comes out changed. Gordon gives his answer to Jackson: he will not hand Bray over. The Accord is over by Gordon's own choice.

**Ch 24. The Parley (Toussaint), about 3,500 words.** Napoleon keeps the letter of his promise: the captives are released and walk away safe. Then he takes Toussaint. "I promised their safety." Lewes's shadows see it and cannot stop it.

**Ch 25. The Offer (Toussaint), about 4,500 words.** A cold stone fort high on a pass, an echo of Fort de Joux. Their first face-to-face meeting **[Decided]**. Napoleon offers him a seat among the Covenant's gods, and, in private, more: a place beside Napoleon when he sits at the center instead of Alexander. To tempt him, Napoleon reveals what he knows of Alexander's secret, a place far off that no general may visit, guarded by Attila's men, and that Jackson saw once and never speaks of. Toussaint refuses, in chains, with no rescue in sight **[Decided]**. He smells sulfur in the fort.

**Ch 26. Someone Must Go (Bray), about 4,000 words.** The freed captives reach the Bastion with Hale, who brings the Cohort's request. Lewes needs a guide who knows the haze paths to the fort. The watcher will lead, but only with Bray. Bray volunteers, an echo of Hale's "Someone must go" in Ch 1. Sophie asks if this is atonement or a choice. Bray: "Not for a banner." Gordon decides to come.

**Ch 27. Interlude: The Backbone (Lannes), about 3,000 words.** Lannes's history with Napoleon, and why he has kept away for a century and a half (see section 8). In council, Joan forces the rescue: "No one came for me." Spartacus wants a full assault. Lannes overrules him for a small team, and gives himself the lead against Toussaint's order.

**Ch 28. The Ledger (Toussaint), about 3,500 words.** Captivity. A native servant in the fort, who owes the Covenant nothing, lets him see a page of Covenant records. Souls sent to Alexander's guarded place are entered not as "dead" or "deserted" but under a third word, "crossed." Napoleon has read the same records and thinks it is a prison. Toussaint is not sure. This is **the hint [Decided]**, kept grounded and specific.

### Part 5: The Vote

**Ch 29. Through the Haze (Bray), about 3,500 words.** The approach by the native paths: Lannes, Lewes, Hale, Bray, Gordon, the watcher, and a few of Lewes's people. Bray and Lewes talk at night. Bray says it aloud for the first time: the shot came from his own line.

**Ch 30. The Fort (Bray), about 4,500 words.** The break-in and the fight. Gordon, who would not raise his hand at Khartoum or at the village, chooses to now, to protect someone else. The cost: one of the team dies, permanently. See section 9 for who.

**Ch 31. Two Wounds (Toussaint), about 3,000 words.** The escape across the fort's yard. Napoleon comes out and sees Lannes in the torchlight, fighting for the other side **[Decided]**. The scene is a look, not a speech. Napoleon loses his prisoner and his friend in one moment.

**Ch 32. Interlude: The Empty Cell (Napoleon), about 2,000 words.** Napoleon alone. He tells Alexander nothing of Lannes. He orders the powder work doubled. One line of grief he does not let anyone see. Sets up Book 2's "Napoleon deals with Lannes's betrayal" **[Decided]**.

**Ch 33. The Cost (Bray), about 3,000 words.** A burial in the haze. Bray writes in the field manual. The watcher and Bray, without a shared language, understand each other.

**Ch 34. The Vote (Toussaint), about 4,500 words.** The war council, with the Uncrowned there for the first time: Gordon, Shaw, Sophie, Bray. Sophie's condition: the alliance answers to the natives it fights among. Spartacus votes no. Lannes votes no. Joan votes yes. Lewes votes yes. Toussaint casts the deciding vote **[Decided]**, for the reason in section 4. He then lays out what he learned in captivity. Gordon realizes the Hall of Names has kept centuries of records, and that some names were scratched out with no explanation.

**Ch 35. The Hall of Names (Bray), about 3,000 words.** Back at the Bastion. Shaw stays as its keeper; Gordon prepares to leave with the alliance. Toussaint and Gordon set the "crossed" ledger beside the Hall's scratched-out names. The alliance has its first lead **[Decided ending]**. Last scene: Bray takes a knife to the wall and carves PEYTON BRAY, not Moose. Final line is the author's. One option is a new entry in the field manual that answers the Ch 7 line: he is walking toward something now.

## 7. What Alexander fears: the Book 1 portion

**[Decided]** The Gate's true nature is revealed at the end of Book 2. Book 1 delivers only a hint, and the hint must point to a grounded answer.

**Proposed chain of clues across the series:**

1. **Book 1 (Ch 25, 28, 35):** Alexander guards a distant place. Attila's men hold it. Covenant records list souls sent there as "crossed." The Bastion's Hall of Names has unexplained scratched-out names going back centuries.
2. **Book 2:** The quests follow those names and that place. The alliance learns it is the Gate of Still Flame, that it is real, and that Alexander has taught for two thousand years that it means oblivion.
3. **Why he fears it (Book 2 payoff):** If Eternal Ones learn they can leave in peace, his empire of the dead dissolves. If natives learn their gods can walk away, the worship that holds his empire up breaks. And for him personally, it would ask him to admit he is a man, not a god, as his Codex entry says.

Readers know the Gate exists from the Codex and from the Ch 3 tag. In Book 1 the characters know it only as a rumor of oblivion, so the hint can be fair without giving the game away.

## 8. Lannes: proposed answers to the open questions

These answer the three questions in the [Toussaint arc notes](arcs/toussaint-louverture.md#questions-for-the-author).

- **Why the Cohort and not waiting for his friend?** Lannes died in 1809 after Aspern-Essling. Accounts say Napoleon sat at his bedside, and some later accounts claim Lannes reproached him for his endless wars (disputed; verify before use). Lannes arrived in Eirion and found Alexander's empire, everything he feared his friend becoming, already built. He joined the Cohort to fight that. When Napoleon arrived twelve years later and chose Alexander, Lannes could not face him.
- **How has he stayed hidden for about 150 years?** (Napoleon arrived in 1821; Bray arrives in 1970.) He has not hidden so much as stayed away. He goes by his Cohort title, the Shieldbearer. He takes commands on fronts where the eagle is not. Napoleon never looked for an infantry commander among rebels. Until this campaign, the Cohort was never a big enough threat to draw Napoleon himself.
- **After the reveal?** Recommend Book 2 opens with Napoleon trying to win him back, and only when that fails does he name him traitor. That is truer to how Napoleon treated those he loved, and it is more painful.
