## 1. The book in one paragraph

Peyton "Moose" Bray, a drafted soldier killed in Vietnam, wakes alone on a grey plain in Eirion and walks until he finds the Bastion of Broken Banners, a neutral fortress where the Uncrowned sit with their unfinished lives. Down in the marches below the plain, Toussaint Louverture, the Last Cohort's strategist, outfights a Covenant army commanded by Napoleon Bonaparte. Napoleon's retreat spills onto the native villages at the plain's edge, and Bray, who died on a mission to clear a village, watches the same thing happen from the other side. He acts, and breaks the old Accord that kept the Bastion out of the war. Napoleon baits Toussaint into a parley under a promise of safety, as he did in life, and takes him. In captivity Toussaint refuses Napoleon's offer of a seat among the Covenant's gods, and learns the first hint of the thing Alexander guards and fears most. Jean Lannes leads the rescue, with Bray at his side, and reveals himself to Napoleon. Toussaint comes home and casts the deciding vote: the Last Cohort and the Uncrowned will stand together.

## 2. The dramatic question and theme

- **Book 1 question:** Is it possible to commit to something again without becoming what you fought against?
- **Theme:** A seat at the table versus a place at the side of others. Napoleon offers seats. Alexander offers eternity. The Bastion offers a place to sit and wait. Bray and Toussaint each choose, at real cost, to stand with people instead.
- **Why this question fits the series:** Book 2 asks where the alliance is going (the quests), and Book 3 asks when to stop (the Gate). Book 1 has to answer the earlier question: whether to start at all.

## 3. Ground rules this outline assumes

These are the world-logic rules the plot depends on. Several are not yet decided. Each one is the simplest answer that keeps the crossing as the only supernatural element **[Decided]** and gives every mystery a grounded payoff **[Decided]**.

| # | Rule | Status | Why the plot needs it |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Time runs the same on Eirion as on Earth, and souls arrive in the order they died.** Alexander arrived about 2,300 years before Bray. Lannes arrived 12 years before Napoleon. The presence chooses who crosses, not when. | **Decided 10/16/2026.** Matches the existing notes ("Lannes would have been in Eirion long before Napoleon"; Gordon has spent "decades" in the Bastion). | Explains how Alexander's empire is old enough to be worshipped, why the Bastion is built from many eras, and why it is the *Last* Cohort. |
| R2 | **Eternal Ones are rare.** A handful cross each year across the whole grey plain, not thousands. | Proposed. The Uncrowned entry says "more arrive with each passing moment." Recommend softening that line. | Makes every arrival worth fighting over. Creates the race to reach newcomers first. |
| R3 | **Armies are mostly native.** A few Eternal Ones command; native soldiers fill the ranks. The Covenant runs a native empire. The Cohort draws on escaped and free natives. | Proposed. Fits the world guide. | Without it, no faction has enough people to fight a war. It also makes every battle cost natives, which feeds Bray's Book 3 position. |
| R4 | **The crossing gives Eternal Ones a shared understanding of one another's speech and writing. It does not cover native languages.** | Proposed. Already implied on the page: Hale hears "a language unspoken but fully known" (Ch 1), and Bray reads Spartacus's inscription as "not a language, but an articulation" (Ch 6). | Lets Toussaint and Napoleon talk, and lets Bray read Spartacus. Keeps a language wall between Eternal Ones and natives, as in Vietnam. |
| R5 | **What is worn crosses; what is held does not.** Clothes, boots, dog tags, and rings come through. A rifle in the hands does not. | Proposed. Answers the open question of why Bray has no rifle. | Keeps firearms out of Eirion and keeps the powder ban meaningful. Also explains the Cromwell dog tag (see section 9). |
| R6 | **Eternal Ones eat, sleep, bleed, and heal normally. Killed is final.** | Decided in substance ([Eternal Ones entry](../codex/lore/the-eternal-ones.md)). | Bray starves in Ch 7. A death in the rescue has to be permanent to matter. |
| R7 | **The grey plain is a high, cold plateau under a permanent mineral haze.** Thin air, no visible sun, little food. Armies cannot live on it for long. | Proposed. Grounds what Bray already experiences: harsh air, breathlessness, cold, no sun. | No army can hold the plain, so no faction controls arrivals. Small parties race to find newcomers instead. Bray's first sight of the sun below the haze becomes a strong beat. |
| R8 | **The Bastion Accord.** Long ago the Bastion's keepers and Alexander agreed: the Uncrowned take no part in the war, and the Covenant does not enter the Bastion. | Proposed. Grounds the Codex line that the Bastion is "the only place in Eirion where one is not pursued." | Alexander keeps it because it serves him: the Bastion holds would-be enemies in reflection. Bray breaking it is the midpoint. Ending it is what the vote means. |
| R9 | **Alexander suppresses innovation, not just powder.** | Proposed. Extends the decided powder ban. | Explains why Eirion is still medieval after 2,000 years of arrivals who knew more. |

**Two more grounded answers the plot relies on (proposed):**

- **Who built the Bastion?** Spartacus. The inscription Bray finds in Ch 6 ends: "This will be my eternal vigil. A monument to the failures of men." That reads as the founding idea of a fortress hung with broken banners. Spartacus built the first walls to mourn, then left because mourning freed no one, and founded the first cohort. Later arrivals built on what he left. This gives Spartacus a personal reason to distrust people who stay.
- **Why "Last" Cohort?** Spartacus has raised cohort after cohort over two thousand years, and Alexander has destroyed each one. This is the one that is still standing. The name carries the whole history in one word.

### Charles Gordon (viewpoint, 7 chapters)

The author's own specialty. Fuller arc in the [cast arcs](arcs/book-1-cast-arcs.md#charles-gordon).

- **Starts:** Keeper of the Bastion for sixty years and guarantor of the Accord. Already thinking of leaving: he walked out to the cairns a month ago, left an honest page under the bayonet, and walked home again (the C.G. note in Ch 7).
- **Flaw:** Obedience. He keeps the Accord because he was handed it, not because he ever judged it just. "I will not raise my hand" was his faith at Khartoum and is his hiding place now.
- **Pressure:** The village burns while he stands still (Ch 17). Toussaint names it (Ch 18). Jackson demands Bray (Ch 19).
- **Choices:** Refuses Jackson and ends the Accord (Ch 22). Goes to the parley and is taken (Ch 22). Refuses Napoleon's offer to govern the marches and end slavery there, the Sudan again (Ch 25). Raises his hand at last, to protect someone else (Ch 28).
- **Gift to the plot:** As keeper of the Hall he is the only one who can read what "crossed" might mean (Ch 25, 33). His sixty years of scratched names are the alliance's first lead.
- **Ends Book 1:** Leaves the Bastion, but will not let his name be scratched: he is leaving for something, not for good. He stops at the Mirror Hall's doors and does not go in. Not yet.

### Toussaint Louverture (viewpoint, 6 chapters)

All beats come from the [arc notes](arcs/toussaint-louverture.md) **[Decided]**. This outline adds the reasons and the order.

- **Starts:** The Cohort's voice of reason. Distrusts the Uncrowned as one more kind of inaction.
- **Pressure:** Napoleon, the man who denied him a seat in life, offers him one in death.
- **Choice:** Refuses the seat in chains. Then votes to bring in the Uncrowned, because people who refuse every crown are the Cohort's best defense against becoming another empire. The man who refused one seat votes to make sure no one in his alliance builds one.
- **Proposed deepening:** His flaw is believing the structure needs him at its center. He once made himself governor for life (verify). In captivity Napoleon names it: "You are me without the crown." His vote is a check on himself.
- **Proposed addition:** He walks into the parley knowing it may be a trap, because captives' lives are on the table. That makes him principled, not careless, and it makes his capture a mirror of 1802 rather than a repeat of the same mistake.

## 5. Structure

**Length target:** About 105,000 to 115,000 words, 34 chapters. The prologue stays short (about 2,600 words). Main chapters average about 3,700 words.

**Point of view (decided 10/16/2026: three voices).** Of the 29 main chapters:

| Voice | Chapters | Count | Share of words (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bray** | 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 14, 17, 19, 21, 24, 27, 31, 34 | 13 | 43 percent |
| **Gordon** | 12, 15, 18, 22, 25, 28, 33 | 7 | 25 percent |
| **Toussaint** | 8, 16, 20, 23, 29, 32 | 6 | 25 percent |
| **Interludes** | 13 and 30 (Napoleon), 26 (Lannes) | 3 | 7 percent |

**How the three voices divide the book:**

- **Bray** is the protagonist and carries the arrival, the Bastion, the village, the Mirror Hall, the approach to the rescue, and the last chapter.
- **Gordon** carries the Bastion from the inside, his failure at the village, his answer to Jackson, the parley, captivity beside Toussaint, the rescue from inside the cell, and leaving the Bastion. He is the bridge between the other two: he lives with Bray in the first half and shares a cell wall with Toussaint in the second.
- **Toussaint** carries the Cohort, the battle, the trap he walks into, the offer, the reveal, and the vote. Every decided Toussaint beat stays in his own voice.

**What changed from the two-voice plan:** Toussaint's campaign chapter folded into the war council (Ch 8) and Napoleon's interlude (Ch 13). The Forage interlude folded into the clearing (Ch 17). "Below the Haze," "Ashes," and the parley moved to Gordon. Two Gordon chapters are new: "The Keeper" (Ch 12) and "The Governor" (Ch 25). The ending split into Gordon's last night (Ch 33) and Bray's short final chapter (Ch 34).

### The threads on one map

The plot works because every thread is about the same piece of ground: the passes between the grey plain and the marches below it.

- Whoever holds the passes catches new arrivals before they reach the Bastion or the Cohort.
- Alexander sends Napoleon to take them **[Proposed]**. That is why the campaign happens now.
- Toussaint denies Napoleon the passes. Napoleon's retreat falls on the villages at the plain's edge, which is where Bray and Gordon are.
- Napoleon then holds Toussaint and Gordon in a fort guarding one of those passes. Only native paths through the haze reach it unseen, and Bray has walked them.

## 7. What Alexander fears: the Book 1 portion

**[Decided]** The Gate's true nature is revealed at the end of Book 2. Book 1 delivers only a hint, and the hint must point to a grounded answer.

**Proposed chain of clues across the series:**

1. **Book 1 (Ch 23, 25, 33):** Alexander guards a distant place. Attila's men hold it. Covenant records list souls sent there as "crossed." The Bastion's Hall of Names has unexplained scratched-out names going back centuries.
2. **Book 2:** The quests follow those names and that place. The alliance learns it is the Gate of Still Flame, that it is real, and that Alexander has taught for two thousand years that it means oblivion.
3. **Why he fears it (Book 2 payoff):** If Eternal Ones learn they can leave in peace, his empire of the dead dissolves. If natives learn their gods can walk away, the worship that holds his empire up breaks. And for him personally, it would ask him to admit he is a man, not a god, as his Codex entry says.

Readers know the Gate exists from the Codex and from the Ch 3 tag. In Book 1 the characters know it only as a rumor of oblivion, so the hint can be fair without giving the game away.

## 8. Lannes: proposed answers to the open questions

These answer the three questions in the [Toussaint arc notes](arcs/toussaint-louverture.md#questions-for-the-author).

- **Why the Cohort and not waiting for his friend?** Lannes died in 1809 after Aspern-Essling. Accounts say Napoleon sat at his bedside, and some later accounts claim Lannes reproached him for his endless wars (disputed; verify before use). Lannes arrived in Eirion and found Alexander's empire, everything he feared his friend becoming, already built. He joined the Cohort to fight that. When Napoleon arrived twelve years later and chose Alexander, Lannes could not face him.
- **How has he stayed hidden for about 150 years?** (Napoleon arrived in 1821; Bray arrives in 1970.) He has not hidden so much as stayed away. He goes by his Cohort title, the Shieldbearer. He takes commands on fronts where the eagle is not. Napoleon never looked for an infantry commander among rebels. Until this campaign, the Cohort was never a big enough threat to draw Napoleon himself.
- **After the reveal?** Recommend Book 2 opens with Napoleon trying to win him back, and only when that fails does he name him traitor. That is truer to how Napoleon treated those he loved, and it is more painful.
