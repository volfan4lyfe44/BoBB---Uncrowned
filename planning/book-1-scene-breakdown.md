# Book 1 Scene Breakdown

**Status:** Proposal, 10/16/2026. The scene-level layer under the [Book 1 plot outline](book-1-plot-outline.md). Characters are in [Book 1 cast arcs](arcs/book-1-cast-arcs.md). Places, history, and the Accord are in [Book 1 history and setting](worldbuilding/book-1-history-and-setting.md).

These are beats, not prose. The author writes the scenes.

**Each chapter lists:**

- **Goal:** what the viewpoint character wants in this chapter.
- **Obstacle:** what stands in the way.
- **Turn:** what changes by the end.
- **Plants and payoffs:** threads opened (P) and closed (✓).

## 2. Chapter by chapter

### Part 2: The Bastion of Broken Banners

#### Ch 6. The Grey Waking (Bray) [Drafted]

- P: Spartacus's inscription (paid off in Ch 34). The geometric scars (✓ Ch 15). The feeling of being watched (✓ Ch 9). The tricorn and "Keep walking" (✓ Ch 11). Tate. Captain Thompson's voice (✓ Ch 19). "It wasn't supposed to end like that" (✓ Ch 29).

#### Ch 7. Away From What I Left (Bray) [Scenes 1 to 3 drafted]

- **Scene 4 (proposed):** The distant thunk is hooves on stone. From a ridge, Bray sees five riders in mixed armor cross the plain, with a led horse carrying a bound figure in clothes he cannot place. One rider turns to study the ground where Bray's tracks are. Bray lies flat in the grit until they go. He is shaking, and he is relieved, and he is ashamed of the relief. He did nothing.
- **Scene 5 (as outlined):** Journal, then the watcher.
- **Turn:** He is no longer alone in the world, and the world is dangerous.
- P: The Finders. The taken arrival (✓ Ch 25, 30, 34). Bray doing nothing while someone is taken (✓ Ch 19, where he acts).

#### Ch 8. The War Council (Toussaint)

- **Setting:** The Cohort's hill fort in the western marches. Rain.
- **Goal:** Keep Spartacus from striking a column on open ground where they would lose.
- **Obstacle:** Spartacus is right that waiting has a cost. The river lord is watching which way the Cohort jumps.
- **Beats:**
  - Lewes reports: a Covenant army on the river road, heading for the stairs. Big, and drilled.
  - Spartacus wants to hit the column on the road. Toussaint asks what happens after the strike.
  - Joan is quiet until she is not.
  - Lewes's scouts describe the standard: a golden eagle. Toussaint knows it and keeps it to himself.
  - After the council, Lannes asks to take the far flank. Toussaint grants it without asking why. Make the reader notice that he didn't ask.
  - Introduce the sergeant among Lewes's people (one line is enough).
- **Turn:** Toussaint commits the Cohort to a plan of not fighting yet.
- P: The eagle. Lannes's request (✓ Ch 12, 27). The sergeant. The river lord.

#### Ch 9. The Watcher on the Plain (Bray)

- **Goal:** Survive. Find the place on the stone map.
- **Obstacle:** A Finder party is sweeping for him. No language in common with the watcher.
- **Beats:**
  - The watcher steps out of the haze. Bray nearly attacks.
  - The watcher kneels to him. Bray doesn't understand.
  - The watcher sees him eat moss with shaking hands, and slowly stands up. The kneeling doesn't happen again.
  - They hide together from the Finders in a fold of the plain.
  - The watcher reads the half-erased guide-lines Bray saw in Ch 6. They mean something.
  - At the edge of sight of the Bastion, the watcher stops and points, and will go no further.
  - Bray walks the last stretch alone. Shaw at the open gate: "Then you'd better come inside. We've been waiting for you."
- **Turn:** Someone helped him for no reason he can see.
- ✓ Feeling watched. P: The watcher's disappointment (✓ Ch 33).

#### Ch 10. The Hall of Names (Bray)

- **Goal:** Find out where he is and what happened to him.
- **Obstacle:** The answers are worse than not knowing. He is dead, he will not age, and he can die again.
- **Beats:**
  - Food.
  - Shaw walks him through the Hall in memory-light. The custom of carving your name. Bray won't.
  - Gordon introduced: courteous, contained, watching.
  - The rules come up in conversation, not as a lecture: the shared tongue, the Accord, and the open gates.
  - The C.G. note: Bray mentions the cairn. Gordon goes quiet.
- **Turn:** He has a roof, and refuses to belong under it.
- ✓ The C.G. note (Gordon's). P: Carving his name (✓ Ch 35). The open gates (✓ Ch 23).

#### Ch 11. The Quiet Courtyard (Bray)

- **Goal:** Be left alone.
- **Obstacle:** Sophie.
- **Beats:**
  - Sophie at the black well: "What did you die for?" "Nothing." "No one dies for nothing. They die for something they didn't choose."
  - Then, when he mentions it was his second tour: "You went back? Why?" He has no answer. She lets it sit.
  - Shaw explains the world: the Covenant, the Cohort, the natives. Bray hears "slaves" and thinks of Spartacus's wall.
  - The tricorn and the note were Hale's. Hale left the Bastion twenty years ago for the Cohort.
  - That night, undressing, Bray finds his own chain broken, with one tag. He wore Tate's beside his own from the night Tate died, and he forgot. The tag did not come out of nowhere. He brought it.
- **Turn:** The strangest thing he has found here was something he carried himself.
- ✓ The tricorn and "Keep walking". ✓ The dog tag. P: Why he went back (sharpened Ch 23; Book 3).

#### Ch 12. The Ground Napoleon Wants (Toussaint)

- **Goal:** Draw Napoleon deep into the marches and stretch his supply.
- **Obstacle:** Giving ground burns the marches' villages and the river lord's patience. Spartacus's warband is near mutiny.
- **Beats:**
  - Napoleon's columns come up the river valley fast. Toussaint withdraws and strips the forage ahead of them.
  - The river lord's price: a sworn promise that the Cohort will rule no one in the marches after the war. Toussaint swears it. It is the first time he has answered his own question.

- **Turn:** Napoleon is now committed to the single river road, with the marches clans on his flanks.
- P: Lannes's knowledge of Napoleon (✓ Ch 31). The river lord's oath (✓ Ch 34).

#### Ch 13. Interlude: The Eagle in the Marches (Napoleon)

#### Ch 14. The Envoy (Bray)

#### Ch 19. The Clearing (Bray). MIDPOINT

- **Goal:** Keep the village alive.
- **Obstacle:** A Covenant detachment of 200 under the colonel, and Gordon's Accord.
- **Beats:**

#### Ch 22. Safe Conduct (Toussaint)

- **Goal:** Get the captives back.
- **Obstacle:** Napoleon's letter, and Toussaint's own history.
- **Beats:**
  - Napoleon offers to exchange the village captives for the Covenant officers taken in the battle, at a parley under safe conduct. He will treat only with the Marshal of the Reclaimed: "the man who beat me."

#### Ch 23. The Mirror Hall (Bray)

- **Goal:** Decide whether to give himself up.
- **Obstacle:** He doesn't trust his own reasons.
- **Beats:**
  - Bray asks to go in. Gordon, who never has, opens the steel doors for him.
  - Darkness, his face, time.
  - He already knows why he let Tate stay (Ch 7). That is not what the dark gives him.
  - What comes, slowly, is a man who looks like him: home in Greeneville in 1969, alive, ordinary, coaching linemen on a Friday night, a life he had in his hands and set down. He cannot hold that man's eyes.
  - He tries to say why he went back and cannot. The fence and Tate's words ("Neither of us walks outta this unless we're walkin' together") come close, and he turns from them.
  - What he can see is the pattern: the pact, the second tour, "Victory or death." He always chooses the noose.
  - He comes out. Giving himself up would be the same thing again: an ending he picks so he doesn't have to keep living.
  - To Gordon: "I'll go if you ask me. But I won't go to be forgiven."
  - Gordon writes to Jackson. No.
  - Shaw, Bray, and the refugees put their shoulders to the rusted gates. They close for the first time in a thousand years.
- **Turn:** The Accord is over by the keeper's choice. The Bastion is now a fortress at war.
- ✓ The open gates. P: Why he went back, sharpened, not answered.

## 3. The battle (Ch 16 and 17)

**Ground:** A valley below the Middle Stair. A shallow river ford in front, with the Cohort's line on the slope behind it. The Cohort's left rests on the cliffs under the plain. The right rests on wooded hills cut by goat tracks. Behind Napoleon, the river road runs back east through a narrow gorge.

**Forces:**

| Covenant (Napoleon) | Cohort (Toussaint) |
|---|---|
| About 12,000 spear infantry in drilled blocks | About 5,500 total |
| About 2,000 heavy horse | Lannes's line infantry (the steadiest troops on Eirion) |
| Torsion engines | Spartacus's warband (in reserve) |
| | Joan's native volunteers |
| | Marches riders and archers (the river lord's) |
| | Lewes's scouts |

**What Napoleon wants:** One decisive battle, ending with the Cohort pinned against the cliffs.

**What Toussaint offers him:** Exactly that, or what looks like it. A thin center behind the ford, the cliffs at his back, apparently nowhere to go.

**The sequence:**

1. **Morning.** Napoleon's engines and blocks pin the center across the ford. Toussaint's center (his own troops, with Hale) holds behind stakes, gives a little, and holds again.
2. **Napoleon's signature move.** He sends a column through the wooded hills on the right to come around behind the Cohort. Lannes predicted it in council: "He will go around. He always goes around." Lannes is waiting on those goat tracks, out of Napoleon's sight, with his line and Lewes's scouts. In the trees and narrow tracks, the column cannot form, and is mauled.
3. **Noon.** The river lord's riders, sent the night before, fall on Napoleon's supply train in the gorge and burn it.
4. **Afternoon.** Covenant heavy horse breaks through on the left, where Joan's volunteers waver. Joan rides forward under her banner, and they hold.
5. **The turn.** Word of the gorge and the flank reaches Napoleon. He sees it at once and begins pulling back his right wing in good order. In that moment the wing is half-turned and exposed. Toussaint signals Spartacus, held in reserve all day. The charge breaks the wing.
6. **Evening.** Napoleon saves his army with a fighting withdrawal. That is his genius, and he shows it even in defeat. But with his supplies burned and the river road cut, he cannot stay in the marches.

**Result:** No rout, no glory. Napoleon loses the campaign. Toussaint walks the field and counts native dead on both sides, and thinks of Saint-Domingue.

**Why it is plausible:** It is Toussaint's real way of war (terrain, allies, patience, refusing the enemy's kind of battle) against Napoleon's real habits (concentration and the flanking march), on ground Toussaint chose. Napoleon is never stupid. He is beaten by preparation and by someone who knows him.

## 4. The rescue (Ch 29 to 31)

**The Watch-Fort:**

- An old Covenant fortress at the top of the East Stair, on the Pall's edge.
- A gatehouse faces the stair road. The keep sits at the back, against a cliff.
- Under the keep is a deep cistern, fed by a cleft in the cliff.
- Garrison of about 150. Napoleon's army is camped in the valley below, an hour's march up the road.

**The plan (Lewes's):**

1. **The diversion.** At moonrise, Spartacus and Joan attack the stair road below the gatehouse with a few hundred, loud and with fire. The garrison runs to the gate walls. The army in the valley starts to climb.
2. **The way in.** The team comes down the cliff into the cistern cleft on ropes. The watchers have known this cleft for generations.
3. **The door.** The clerk unbars the cistern door into the keep's undercroft.
4. **The cells.** They reach Toussaint.

**What goes wrong:**

1. Toussaint will not leave without the taken arrival in the next cell. Lannes argues for ten seconds and gives up. The keys are in the guardroom. That costs minutes.
2. A guard patrol finds the open cistern door. The alarm goes up inside the keep.
3. The team has to cross the inner yard to the cleft. The guards hold the stair down to the undercroft.
4. Hale moves to hold the inner stair alone. The sergeant pulls him back: "You already did this once, son." The sergeant holds it with one of Lewes's people.
5. In the yard, a guard goes for the watcher. Gordon, who would not raise his hand at Khartoum or at the village, raises it now, to protect someone else.
6. Napoleon comes out of the keep, and sees Lannes (Ch 31).
7. They go up the ropes. The sergeant comes last, wounded, and makes the top.

**In the Pall:** Bray carries the sergeant on his back through the haze, talking to him, as he carried Tate through the jungle. At the first halt he sets him down and finds he has been carrying a dead man again. This time he knows it, and he lets the others take him.

**Why it works:**

- Every piece was planted: the haze paths (Ch 15), the knot (Ch 7), the clerk (Ch 28), the taken arrival (Ch 7), the diversion (Ch 27).
- Every choice that goes wrong is a character being true to themselves: Toussaint won't leave anyone, Hale wants to die well, and Gordon finally acts.
- Napoleon's choice not to pursue into the Pall is both sound tactics (horses balk in it, Ch 19) and a hint of grief.
