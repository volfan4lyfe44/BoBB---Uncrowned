# Book 1 Scene Breakdown

**Status:** Proposal, 10/16/2026. **Restructured 10/16/2026 for three viewpoints: Bray, Gordon, and Toussaint.** The scene-level layer under the [Book 1 plot outline](book-1-plot-outline.md). Characters are in [Book 1 cast arcs](arcs/book-1-cast-arcs.md). Places, history, and the Accord are in [Book 1 history and setting](worldbuilding/book-1-history-and-setting.md).

These are beats, not prose. The author writes the scenes.

**Each chapter lists:**

- **Goal:** what the viewpoint character wants in this chapter.
- **Obstacle:** what stands in the way.
- **Turn:** what changes by the end.
- **Plants and payoffs:** threads opened (P) and closed (✓).

## 2. Chapter by chapter

### Part 2: The Bastion of Broken Banners

#### Ch 6. The Grey Waking (Bray) [Drafted]

- P: Spartacus's inscription (✓ Ch 32). The geometric scars (✓ Ch 15). The feeling of being watched (✓ Ch 9). The tricorn and "Keep walking" (✓ Ch 11). Tate. Captain Thompson's voice (✓ Ch 17). "It wasn't supposed to end like that" (✓ Ch 27).

#### Ch 7. Away From What I Left (Bray) [Scenes 1 to 3 drafted]

- **Scene 4 (proposed):** The distant thunk is hooves on stone. From a ridge, Bray sees five riders in mixed armor cross the plain, with a led horse carrying a bound figure in clothes he cannot place. One rider turns to study the ground where Bray's tracks are. Bray lies flat in the grit until they go. He is shaking, and he is relieved, and he is ashamed of the relief. He did nothing.
- **Scene 5 (as outlined):** Journal, then the watcher.
- **Turn:** He is no longer alone in the world, and the world is dangerous.
- P: The Finders. The taken arrival (✓ Ch 23, 28, 32). Bray doing nothing while someone is taken (✓ Ch 17). The C.G. note (✓ Ch 12).

#### Ch 8. The War Council (Toussaint)

- **Setting:** The Cohort's hill fort in the western marches. Rain.
- **Goal:** Keep Spartacus from striking a column on open ground where they would lose, and hold the marches clans together.
- **Obstacle:** Spartacus is right that waiting has a cost. The river lord is watching which way the Cohort jumps.
- **Beats:**
  - Lewes reports: a Covenant army on the river road, heading for the stairs. Big, and drilled.
  - Spartacus wants to hit the column on the road. Toussaint asks what happens after the strike.
  - Joan is quiet until she is not. Hale, young and eager, at Lannes's shoulder.
  - Lewes's scouts describe the standard: a golden eagle. Toussaint knows it and keeps it to himself.
  - After the council, Lannes asks to take the far flank. Toussaint grants it without asking why. Make the reader notice that he didn't ask.
  - The river lord's price for the clans: a sworn promise that the Cohort will rule no one in the marches after the war. Toussaint swears it. It is the first time he has answered his own question.
  - Introduce the sergeant among Lewes's people (one line is enough).
- **Turn:** Toussaint commits the Cohort to a plan of not fighting yet, and buys the ground to do it on.
- P: The eagle. Lannes's request (✓ Ch 16, 26, 29). The river lord's oath (✓ Ch 32). The sergeant.

#### Ch 9. The Watcher on the Plain (Bray)

- **Goal:** Survive. Find the place on the stone map.
- **Obstacle:** A Finder party is sweeping for him. No language in common with the watcher.
- **Beats:**
  - The watcher steps out of the haze. Bray nearly attacks.
  - The watcher kneels to him. Bray doesn't understand.
  - The watcher sees him eat moss with shaking hands, and slowly stands up. The kneeling doesn't happen again.
  - They hide together from the Finders in a fold of the plain.
  - The watcher reads the half-erased guide-lines Bray saw in Ch 6. They mean something.
  - At the edge of sight of the Bastion, the watcher stops and points, and will go no further.
  - Bray walks the last stretch alone. Shaw at the open gate: "Then you'd better come inside. We've been waiting for you."
- **Turn:** Someone helped him for no reason he can see.
- ✓ Feeling watched. P: The watcher's disappointment (✓ Ch 31).

#### Ch 10. The Hall of Names (Bray)

- **Goal:** Find out where he is and what happened to him.
- **Obstacle:** The answers are worse than not knowing. He is dead, he will not age, and he can die again.
- **Beats:**
  - Food.
  - Shaw walks him through the Hall in memory-light. The custom of carving your name. Bray won't.
  - Gordon introduced, seen from outside: courteous, contained, watching.
  - The rules come up in conversation, not as a lecture: the shared tongue, the Accord, and the open gates.
  - The C.G. note: Bray mentions the cairn. Gordon goes quiet.
- **Turn:** He has a roof, and refuses to belong under it.
- P: Carving his name (✓ Ch 34). The open gates (✓ Ch 22).

#### Ch 11. The Quiet Courtyard (Bray)

- **Goal:** Be left alone.
- **Obstacle:** Sophie.
- **Beats:**
  - Sophie at the black well: "What did you die for?" "Nothing." "No one dies for nothing. They die for something they didn't choose."
  - Then, when he mentions it was his second tour: "You went back? Why?" He has no answer. She lets it sit.
  - Shaw explains the world: the Covenant, the Cohort, the natives. Bray hears "slaves" and thinks of Spartacus's wall.
  - The tricorn and the note were Hale's. Hale left the Bastion twenty years ago for the Cohort.
  - That night, undressing, Bray finds his own chain broken, with one tag. He wore Tate's beside his own from the night Tate died, and he forgot. The tag did not come out of nowhere. He brought it.
- **Turn:** The strangest thing he has found here was something he carried himself.
- ✓ The tricorn and "Keep walking". ✓ The dog tag. P: Why he went back (sharpened Ch 21; Book 3).

#### Ch 12. The Keeper (Gordon)

Gordon's first chapter. The Bastion from the inside, and the man who has kept it for sixty years.

- **Goal:** Keep the day in order. Keep the Accord.
- **Obstacle:** A newcomer who found the page Gordon left at the cairn, and a letter from Jackson that is warmer than it should be.
- **Beats:**
  - Before dawn, his round of the Hall. He knows every name. He has scratched a line through every soul who left in sixty years, as the custom requires. He has never known where most of them went. Some told him, as they left, that they were going to finish. He never asked what they meant.
  - Prayer. Scripture, read as he has read it every morning since Gravesend and China. (The author knows his habits better than any outline; verify details.)
  - Jackson's latest letter, brought by a Covenant rider: scripture, courtesy, and the news that an envoy, Rommel, will come to meet the new arrival. Their correspondence is decades old. Each thinks the other is wrong and worth writing to.
  - The page at the cairn. A month ago he walked out of the Bastion meaning not to come back. He left an honest page under the bayonet, as the custom is, and walked home again by evening. The American has read it.
  - He watches Bray refuse to carve his name and sees a doubter who has not yet learned to hide it. He wonders whether he is looking at what he would have been without God.
  - Khartoum, briefly: the boy with the figs, the message he wrote, "I will not raise my hand."
  - The village below needs medicine and his trading trip is due. He decides to take the American with him. He tells himself it is for the American's sake.
- **Turn:** For the first time in years, Gordon is doing something that is not routine.
- ✓ The C.G. note, from his side. P: The scratched names (✓ Ch 25, 33). Jackson's letters (✓ Ch 19, 22). Gordon's hand (✓ Ch 17, 28).

#### Ch 13. Interlude: The Eagle in the Marches (Napoleon)

- **Beats:**
  - Napoleon's columns come up the river valley fast, and find nothing to fight. The Cohort withdraws ahead of him and strips the forage.
  - He tries a feint to bait them, splitting his force on paper. They don't take it. Whoever commands them knows how he thinks. (Plant: Lannes.)
  - He is now committed to the single river road, with the marches clans on his flanks.
  - He longs for guns, which no one on his staff can understand. The sealed wagon, and the chemist he trusts more than his officers.
  - A captured letter signed with a title. A prisoner, offered his freedom, gives him the name behind it.
- **Turn:** He knows who he is fighting.
- P: Powder (✓ Ch 23, 30). "Louverture." Someone who knows him (✓ Ch 29).

#### Ch 14. The Envoy (Bray)

- **Goal:** Stay out of everything.
- **Obstacle:** Rommel, who is good at his job.
- **Beats:**
  - Rommel arrives at the open gate with two escorts, under the Accord.
  - The pitch to Bray, quiet and professional. "Our own side killed us both."
  - Sophie walks in. The room goes still.
  - Rommel tells her about the leaflet. She leaves without answering.
  - Bray says neither yes nor no. Rommel: "That is an answer. Not a good one."
  - Gordon is going down to the village to trade and treat the sick. He asks Bray to carry for him. Bray goes, mostly to get away from Rommel's offer.
- **Turn:** Bray leaves the Bastion. **End of Act I.**
- P: Friendly fire, his and others' (✓ Ch 27). Sophie's leaflet (✓ Ch 32). Rommel (✓ Ch 30).

### Part 3: The Grey March

#### Ch 15. Below the Haze (Gordon)

- **Goal:** Do the work he came to do: medicine, trade, letters.
- **Obstacle:** The American, who sees the village the way a soldier sees ground.
- **Beats:**
  - The West Stair. The Pall thins. Gordon has seen many arrivals see the sun for the first time in Eirion. He has never seen one sit down in the road. Bray's first sun, seen from outside.
  - The village is Gordon's real ministry. He is a different man here: patient, skilled, funny in the native tongue in a way he never is in English.
  - The watcher's family. A younger sibling who follows Gordon everywhere.
  - Gordon notices Bray counting the paths out of the village, the cover, the approaches. He understands what Bray was sent to do in his own war, and that Bray understands it too.
  - The watcher teaches Bray a few words and walks him up the haze paths. Gordon watches them go, and is glad of it, and does not ask himself why.
  - The earthworks, explained to Bray in gestures and to the reader through Gordon: guide-lines to the Bastion.
- **Turn:** Gordon admits to himself how much he has to lose here.
- ✓ The sun. ✓ The earthworks. P: The haze paths (✓ Ch 17, 27). The sibling (✓ Ch 17, 22).

#### Ch 16. The Battle (Toussaint)

About 6,500 words. See section 3 below for the full plan.

- **Opening:** The night before, Lannes, privately: "He will go around. He always goes around." Toussaint asks how he knows. Lannes doesn't answer. Toussaint lets it go, again.
- **Turn:** Napoleon loses the campaign, but not his army. Toussaint wins, and pays in native dead.
- ✓ Lannes's knowledge. ✓ Spartacus's patience. P: Napoleon's retreat route along the terraces.

#### Ch 17. The Clearing (Bray). MIDPOINT

- **Goal:** Keep the village alive.
- **Obstacle:** A Covenant detachment of 200 under the colonel, and Gordon's Accord.
- **Beats:**
  - Dawn. Horns below the terraces.
  - The colonel's terms, and his reasons. Napoleon's army must eat on the way east, and villages that guided rebel scouts are to be punished. (This replaces the earlier Forage interlude.)
  - Gordon steps forward and invokes the Accord. The colonel, with real respect: the Accord protects your walls, not this hillside. Gordon will not raise his hand.
  - The colonel's order to his men: hit whatever you see. Bray hears it in Thompson's voice.
  - Bray takes the watcher's family and as many as will follow up the haze paths, where horses balk and the air thins.
  - Covering the last of them, he fights a native soldier who is cutting at a child. Bray kills him with the soldier's own blade. Bray sees his face: young, frightened, doing what he was told.
  - From the haze they watch the village burn. Twenty captives are marched off. The watcher's sibling is among them.
- **Turn:** Bray acted, and the Accord is broken, and it was not enough.
- ✓ Thompson's voice. ✓ Ch 7's failure to act. P: The captives (✓ Ch 22). The dead soldier (✓ Ch 19).

#### Ch 18. Ashes (Gordon)

- **Goal:** Bury the dead and find the living.
- **Obstacle:** His own failure, and a stranger who sees it at once.
- **Beats:**
  - The day after. Gordon among the ashes, burying, praying, and knowing that he stood still while the American moved.
  - Riders on the terrace road: the Cohort's van, following Napoleon's retreat.
  - Hale. They have not spoken since Hale left the Bastion. A short, careful exchange; Gordon disapproved, and Hale knew it.
  - Toussaint. Gordon watches him take Bray's measure in one look, and reads the verdict on his face: important, still lost. (The decided beat of Toussaint sizing up Bray, seen by Gordon.)
  - Bray asks Toussaint to help get the captives back. Toussaint: "And then what?" Bray has no answer.
  - Toussaint and Gordon. Two men who once governed provinces for distant masters. Toussaint, courteous and exact: "You are the Keeper. You kept them very well, until yesterday."
  - The Cohort moves on. Gordon and Bray take the refugees up the West Stair toward the Bastion, against every term of the Accord.
- **Turn:** **The threads touch.** Gordon's duty has failed the people it was meant to serve, and he has just broken it himself.
- P: Gordon and Toussaint (✓ Ch 25).

### Part 4: A Seat at the Table

#### Ch 19. The Broken Accord (Bray)

- **Goal:** Find out what he has cost the Bastion.
- **Obstacle:** Everything.
- **Beats:**
  - Forty native refugees in a fortress that has never sheltered natives at war with the Covenant.
  - The Uncrowned meet in the Hall. Some want Bray gone.
  - Shaw: the gates are open; they come in.
  - Sophie makes Bray tell all of it, including the soldier's face.
  - Jackson's letter arrives with a Covenant rider, addressed to Gordon. Courteous, scriptural, absolute: deliver the man who bore arms beyond your walls by the next envoy, and the Accord stands. Refuse, and it does not.
- **Turn:** Bray's life is now the price of everyone else's safety.
- ✓ The dead soldier, owned. P: Jackson's demand (✓ Ch 22).

#### Ch 20. Safe Conduct (Toussaint)

- **Goal:** Get the captives back.
- **Obstacle:** Napoleon's letter, and Toussaint's own history.
- **Beats:**
  - Napoleon offers to exchange the village captives for the Covenant officers taken in the battle, at a parley under safe conduct. He will treat only with the Marshal of the Reclaimed, "the man who beat me," and asks that the exchange be witnessed by the Keeper of the Bastion, "whose word both sides honor."
  - Toussaint reads the wording twice. Safety is promised for the captives and the exchange. Nothing is said of him, or of the Keeper.
  - Council: Spartacus calls it a trap. Toussaint: "I know. I have been here before."
  - Alone with Lewes, he lays out the bet: Napoleon will want to talk before anything else, and talk buys time.
  - He sends Hale to the Bastion with the request, and with a warning for Gordon: Napoleon may want him too.
  - He orders Lannes to stay away, to keep his secret from Napoleon.
  - Last scene: he thinks about the big American in the ashes. Important, still lost, and the kind of soldier who asks what happens after. He files it away.
- **Turn:** Toussaint decides to walk into the trap with his eyes open.

#### Ch 21. The Mirror Hall (Bray)

- **Goal:** Decide whether to give himself up.
- **Obstacle:** He doesn't trust his own reasons.
- **Beats:**
  - Bray asks to go in. Gordon, who never has, opens the steel doors for him.
  - Darkness, his face, time.
  - He already knows why he let Tate stay (Ch 7). That is not what the dark gives him.
  - What comes, slowly, is a man who looks like him: home in Greeneville in 1969, alive, ordinary, coaching linemen on a Friday night, a life he had in his hands and set down. He cannot hold that man's eyes.
  - He tries to say why he went back and cannot. The fence and Tate's words ("Neither of us walks outta this unless we're walkin' together") come close, and he turns from them.
  - What he can see is the pattern: the pact, the second tour, "Victory or death." He always chooses the noose.
  - He comes out. Giving himself up would be the same thing again: an ending he picks so he doesn't have to keep living.
  - To Gordon: "I'll go if you ask me. But I won't go to be forgiven."
- **Turn:** Bray hands the choice back to the keeper.
- P: Why he went back, sharpened, not answered (Book 3).

#### Ch 22. The Keeper's Answer (Gordon)

- **Goal:** Answer Jackson.
- **Obstacle:** Sixty years of obedience, and a second request that may be a trap.
- **Beats:**
  - Night. Gordon at his desk with Jackson's letter and Bray's words. He has kept the Accord because he was handed it. No one ever asked him whether it was just. He asks now.
  - His answer to Jackson, written out in full: no. The author can write this letter as Gordon would, scripture and all.
  - Morning. Shaw, Bray, and the refugees put their shoulders to the rusted gates. They close for the first time in a thousand years. **The Accord is over by the keeper's choice.**
  - Hale arrives with Toussaint's request and his warning: Napoleon wants the Keeper to witness the exchange, and may want the Keeper himself.
  - Gordon goes. The captives were his patients. And he has stayed behind walls for sixty years. Unlike Toussaint, he half believes the forms of honor will hold.
  - The road east with Hale. Beyond the village for the first time in decades.
  - **The parley.** A ruined shrine on the terrace road below the Watch-Fort. Napoleon in person, gracious, smaller than his legend, delighted to meet "the English saint."
  - The exchange goes as promised. The captives, the watcher's sibling among them, walk to Lewes's riders, and Hale goes with them.
  - Then Napoleon's guards close around Toussaint, and around Gordon. "I promised their safety. I made neither of you any promise at all. And the Keeper is wanted in the east, by an old correspondent."
- **Turn:** Gordon's name was the bait. His honor brought Toussaint to the table.
- ✓ The open gates. ✓ Jackson's demand. ✓ The captives. P: The rescue.

#### Ch 23. The Offer (Toussaint)

- **Setting:** A cell in the Watch-Fort, cold, high, wind off the plain. Fort de Joux in everything but name. Gordon in the next cell. The taken arrival from Ch 7 beyond him, waiting to be "sent east."
- **Beats:**
  - Napoleon comes the first night, alone, with wine.
  - The letters: "I read the first."
  - The offer: a seat among the Covenant's gods. Then, quietly, more: a place beside Napoleon when he takes the center from Alexander.
  - The bait: what Napoleon knows of Alexander's secret. A place far to the east. Attila's men guard it. No general may go without leave. Jackson went once and won't speak of it.
  - The mirror: "You made yourself governor for life. You are me without the crown." It lands.
  - Toussaint refuses. Exact words are the author's. Suggested direction: he has been offered a seat by this man before, and it was a cell.
  - Napoleon leaves the wine. Toussaint smells sulfur from the cellars. Through the wall, the Englishman is praying.
- **Turn:** He has refused, and he has learned what Napoleon wants most.
- ✓ The decided offer and refusal. ✓ The decided hint (first half). P: Powder. P: Alexander's guarded place.

#### Ch 24. Someone Must Go (Bray)

- **Beats:**
  - Lewes's riders bring the freed captives up the West Stair, with Hale. The watcher's sibling is home.
  - Hale tells it: the Marshal and the Keeper are both taken.
  - The Cohort will try a rescue. Lewes needs a way to the Watch-Fort's back that no sentry watches, and the watchers know one. The watcher will guide only if Bray comes.
  - Hale says it, without meaning to echo anything: someone must go.
  - Bray volunteers. This time it is personal: they have taken the one man in the Bastion who stood up for him.
  - Sophie, privately: atonement or choice? He tells her, for the first time aloud, that he let Tate stay because he did not want to be alone. Then: "Not for a banner."
  - Shaw stays to hold the gates.
  - The sergeant is with Hale's party. Bray and the sergeant talk on the wall that night.
- **Turn:** Bray chooses a side without choosing a flag.

#### Ch 25. The Governor (Gordon)

The heart of the Gordon thread in Book 1.

- **Beats:**
  - Napoleon visits Gordon. He has never met him: Gordon was born after Napoleon died. He knows him from Covenant files and later arrivals' stories. "Your empire beat mine. Govern for mine now."
  - **The offer:** governor of the western marches when they are taken, with authority to close the Covenant's slave estates there. It is the Sudan again: a good governor inside an empire he does not trust, ending slavery with a despot's authority. (Verify the details of Gordon's anti-slavery work as governor-general of the Sudan.) It tempts him, and he knows exactly why.
  - **Night, through the wall.** Gordon and Toussaint talk for the first time as equals. Saint-Domingue and the Sudan. Governing for France, governing for the Khedive. Toussaint: "They will give it to you, and take it back when it suits them. I know. I held it." The question neither can answer: what do we do after we win?
  - Gordon refuses Napoleon. It is the second refusal in that corridor.
  - **The ledger.** The clerk who brings the food has seen the chemists and is afraid. Gordon, a scholar of records, asks the right question. The clerk shows him the transfer ledger. Eternal prisoners sent east are entered not as dead, not as deserted, but as "crossed." The taken arrival's name is on the next line.
  - Gordon has scratched a line through every name that left the Bastion for sixty years and never known where they went. Some said they were going to finish. He tells Toussaint through the wall. Neither sleeps.
- **Turn:** Gordon refuses obedience twice in one week, and holds a clue only the keeper of the Hall could read.
- ✓ The scratched names (first half). ✓ The decided hint (second half: Toussaint learns it from Gordon). P: The clerk (✓ Ch 28).

#### Ch 26. Interlude: The Backbone (Lannes)

- **Beats:**
  - Lannes's memory of Aspern-Essling and the bedside.
  - 150 years of postings where the eagle wasn't.
  - Council: Spartacus wants the whole army on the Watch-Fort. Lannes: the army in the valley would destroy them. A small team goes in the back, and Spartacus and Joan make noise at the front.
  - Joan: "No one came for me."
  - Lannes names himself the team's leader. Lewes: "He ordered you to stay away." Lannes: "He isn't here to order anything."
- **Turn:** Lannes stops hiding.

### Part 5: The Vote

#### Ch 27. Through the Haze (Bray)

- **Team:** Lannes, Lewes, Hale, Bray, the watcher, the sergeant, and three of Lewes's people.
- **Beats:**
  - Three days along the plain's edge inside the Pall, with no fires.
  - Lewes and Bray on watch. Bray says it for the first time: the shot came from his own line. Lewes: "Mine came from an aeroplane. Doesn't matter much whose it was in the end." Then, after a while: "It matters a bit."
  - The sergeant and Hale talk about the gallows and the parapet.
  - The cliff above the Watch-Fort's cistern. Lewes ties the ropes with the same knot Bray found in Ch 7.
- ✓ "It wasn't supposed to end like that." ✓ The J.L. knot.

#### Ch 28. The Fort (Gordon)

The rescue from inside the cells. See section 4 for the full plan.

- **Beats:**
  - The clerk's warning at the evening meal: tonight, be awake.
  - Noise at the gatehouse far off: the diversion. Then footsteps in the undercroft, from the wrong direction.
  - Faces at the bars. Gordon knows Bray and Hale. Toussaint knows the big man with the lantern, and his face changes: Lannes, who was ordered to stay away.
  - Toussaint will not leave without the taken arrival. Minutes lost for the keys.
  - The alarm. The sergeant holds the inner stair so the rest can cross the yard. Hale tries to stay with him and is sent on.
  - In the yard, a guard goes for the watcher. Gordon, who would not raise his hand at Khartoum or in the village, takes a guard's staff and raises it now, to protect someone else. His first blow in a hundred years.
  - The keep door opens behind them, and torchlight spills across the yard.
- **Turn:** Gordon has acted. The man coming out of the keep is Napoleon.
- ✓ Gordon's hand. ✓ The clerk.

#### Ch 29. Two Wounds (Toussaint)

- **Beats:**
  - The yard, the torches. Napoleon comes out of the keep.
  - He sees Lannes. Lannes sees him. Neither says much. Recommended: Napoleon says "Jean," and Lannes does not answer.
  - Napoleon understands how Toussaint read him all campaign.
  - The ropes. The sergeant comes last, wounded, and makes the top.
  - Napoleon orders no pursuit into the Pall. Make the reader wonder whether that is tactics or grief.
- ✓ The decided rescue and reveal.

#### Ch 30. Interlude: The Empty Cell (Napoleon)

- **Beats:**
  - Morning. Rommel arrives from Alexander to review the failed campaign.
  - Napoleon tells him the prisoners escaped. He does not say who freed them, or that the Keeper of the Bastion was among them.
  - Rommel walks the cellars, smells the sulfur, says nothing.
  - Alone, Napoleon orders the powder work doubled.
  - One private moment for Lannes that no one sees.
- P: Book 2.

#### Ch 31. The Cost (Bray)

- **Beats:**
  - **In the Pall:** Bray carries the sergeant on his back through the haze, talking to him, as he carried Tate through the jungle. At the first halt he sets him down and finds he has been carrying a dead man again. This time he knows it, and he lets the others take him.
  - The sergeant buried in the Pall, with a cairn in the plain-watchers' way. Gordon reads over him.
  - Hale cannot speak, and then he can. **Hale's true last words** (set up in Ch 1: "But that wasn't the last thing he said"). He tells Bray because Bray is the one person here who never wanted a hero.

## 3. The battle (Ch 16)

**Ground:** A valley below the Middle Stair. A shallow river ford in front, with the Cohort's line on the slope behind it. The Cohort's left rests on the cliffs under the plain. The right rests on wooded hills cut by goat tracks. Behind Napoleon, the river road runs back east through a narrow gorge.

**Forces:**

| Covenant (Napoleon) | Cohort (Toussaint) |
|---|---|
| About 12,000 spear infantry in drilled blocks | About 5,500 total |
| About 2,000 heavy horse | Lannes's line infantry (the steadiest troops on Eirion) |
| Torsion engines | Spartacus's warband (in reserve) |
| | Joan's native volunteers |
| | Marches riders and archers (the river lord's) |
| | Lewes's scouts |

**What Napoleon wants:** One decisive battle, ending with the Cohort pinned against the cliffs.

**What Toussaint offers him:** Exactly that, or what looks like it. A thin center behind the ford, the cliffs at his back, apparently nowhere to go.

**The sequence:**

1. **Morning.** Napoleon's engines and blocks pin the center across the ford. Toussaint's center (his own troops, with Hale) holds behind stakes, gives a little, and holds again.
2. **Napoleon's signature move.** He sends a column through the wooded hills on the right to come around behind the Cohort. Lannes predicted it the night before: "He will go around. He always goes around." Lannes is waiting on those goat tracks, out of Napoleon's sight, with his line and Lewes's scouts. In the trees and narrow tracks, the column cannot form, and is mauled.
3. **Noon.** The river lord's riders, sent the night before, fall on Napoleon's supply train in the gorge and burn it.
4. **Afternoon.** Covenant heavy horse breaks through on the left, where Joan's volunteers waver. Joan rides forward under her banner, and they hold.
5. **The turn.** Word of the gorge and the flank reaches Napoleon. He sees it at once and begins pulling back his right wing in good order. In that moment the wing is half-turned and exposed. Toussaint signals Spartacus, held in reserve all day. The charge breaks the wing.
6. **Evening.** Napoleon saves his army with a fighting withdrawal. That is his genius, and he shows it even in defeat. But with his supplies burned and the river road cut, he cannot stay in the marches.

**Result:** No rout, no glory. Napoleon loses the campaign. Toussaint walks the field and counts native dead on both sides, and thinks of Saint-Domingue.

**Why it is plausible:** It is Toussaint's real way of war (terrain, allies, patience, refusing the enemy's kind of battle) against Napoleon's real habits (concentration and the flanking march), on ground Toussaint chose. Napoleon is never stupid. He is beaten by preparation and by someone who knows him.

## 4. The rescue (Ch 27 to 29)

Three viewpoints carry it: Bray on the approach (Ch 27), Gordon from inside the cells (Ch 28), Toussaint in the yard (Ch 29). Bray carries the sergeant out in Ch 31.

**The Watch-Fort:**

- An old Covenant fortress at the top of the East Stair, on the Pall's edge.
- A gatehouse faces the stair road. The keep sits at the back, against a cliff.
- Under the keep is a deep cistern, fed by a cleft in the cliff.
- Garrison of about 150. Napoleon's army is camped in the valley below, an hour's march up the road.

**The plan (Lewes's):**

1. **The diversion.** At moonrise, Spartacus and Joan attack the stair road below the gatehouse with a few hundred, loud and with fire. The garrison runs to the gate walls. The army in the valley starts to climb.
2. **The way in.** The team comes down the cliff into the cistern cleft on ropes. The watchers have known this cleft for generations.
3. **The door.** The clerk unbars the cistern door into the keep's undercroft.
4. **The cells.** They reach Toussaint and Gordon.

**What goes wrong:**

1. Toussaint will not leave without the taken arrival in the far cell. Lannes argues for ten seconds and gives up. The keys are in the guardroom. That costs minutes.
2. A guard patrol finds the open cistern door. The alarm goes up inside the keep.
3. The team has to cross the inner yard to the cleft. The guards hold the stair down to the undercroft.
4. Hale moves to hold the inner stair alone. The sergeant pulls him back: "You already did this once, son." The sergeant holds it with one of Lewes's people.
5. In the yard, a guard goes for the watcher. Gordon raises his hand, to protect someone else.
6. Napoleon comes out of the keep, and sees Lannes.
7. They go up the ropes. The sergeant comes last, wounded, and makes the top.

**In the Pall (Ch 31):** Bray carries the sergeant on his back through the haze, talking to him, as he carried Tate through the jungle. At the first halt he finds he has been carrying a dead man again. This time he knows it, and he lets the others take him.

**Why it works:**

- Every piece was planted: the haze paths (Ch 15), the knot (Ch 7), the clerk (Ch 25), the taken arrival (Ch 7), the diversion (Ch 26).
- Every choice that goes wrong is a character being true to themselves: Toussaint won't leave anyone, Hale wants to die well, and Gordon finally acts.
- Napoleon's choice not to pursue into the Pall is both sound tactics (horses balk in it, Ch 17) and a hint of grief.
