# Book 1 History and Setting

**Status:** Proposal, 10/16/2026. Fills the world gaps behind the [Book 1 plot outline](../book-1-plot-outline.md). Nothing here is final.

**Arrival rule (decided 10/16/2026).** The presence chooses who crosses; souls arrive at the moment of death, in historical order, on the same clock as Earth (outline rule R1). Eirion does not change a soul unless it chooses to change, which is why people can spend centuries here as they were. Rare arrivals (R2) is still a proposal.

**Names.** Every place name here is a working name in plain English so the author can see the function. The author should rename anything that doesn't sing.

## 1. A short history of Eirion, as the Eternal Ones know it

Dates are Earth years of arrival, which under R1 are also Eirion years.

| When | What happened | Why it matters in Book 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Before 323 BCE | Eternal Ones have crossed for as long as natives remember. None united more than a valley. Native legends of "the Ancients" are half-memories of them. | The creation myths are native stories about these earlier arrivals, not facts. Fits "Memory is not truth, only reflection." |
| 323 BCE | **Alexander arrives.** Within a generation he unites the native kingdoms east of the grey plain. Natives who watch him not age for a century decide he is a god. He lets them. | His empire rests on native worship. That is why he cannot let natives see a god walk away (the Gate) or die to a native's weapon (the powder ban). |
| 3rd and 2nd centuries BCE | Alexander gathers Eternal Ones as they arrive. The Covenant's doctrine takes shape: "You were denied completion. I will give you eternity." Hannibal arrives (183 BCE) and joins for the war, not the god. | The Covenant is old, rich, and confident. |
| 71 BCE | **Spartacus arrives.** He finds an empire built on enslaved natives. He carves the inscription Bray finds in Ch 6, and on the high plain he raises the first walls of a fortress as a vigil, hung with the banners of the dead. Then he walks away from it and raises the first cohort among the enslaved. | Spartacus founded the Bastion and then rejected it. That is why he distrusts those who stay. |
| 71 BCE to about 1000 CE | **The cohorts.** Spartacus raises rebellion after rebellion. Alexander crushes each one. Spartacus is the only constant. Meanwhile, souls who will not fight either side drift to Spartacus's empty fortress and build on it. They become the Uncrowned, at first a Covenant slur. | "The Last Cohort" is the current one. Its members say it is the last one that will be needed. Both meanings stand. |
| 453 CE | Attila arrives and becomes Alexander's purge. | Rumor only in Book 1. |
| About 1000 CE (proposed) | **The Bastion is stormed.** A Covenant army breaks the fortress and kills everyone inside. The slaughter feeds the largest cohort in centuries, which nearly breaks the empire before it falls. **Alexander learns a lesson:** a burned Bastion breeds rebels; a tolerated one keeps would-be rebels sitting still. He offers the **Accord** to the Bastion's next keeper. | The Accord is a trap that looks like mercy. That is why Alexander keeps it and why breaking it matters. |
| 1431 | Joan of Arc arrives. Joins the cohort of her time. Natives in the marches take her for holy, and she lets them. | Her quiet guilt: she uses belief the way Alexander does. Seed only. |
| 1718 | Charles XII arrives; joins the Covenant. | Named only. |
| 1776 | **Nathan Hale arrives.** Lives in the Bastion for most of two centuries. | |
| 1803 | **Toussaint arrives.** Joins the cohort and spends 167 years turning a warband into an army: supply, councils, allies, discipline. Under him the cohort stops dying. | **Why the Covenant takes the Cohort seriously now.** |
| 1809 | **Jean Lannes arrives.** Joins the Cohort, and becomes Toussaint's closest military partner. | |
| 1821 | **Napoleon arrives.** Joins the Covenant. Alexander, wary of him, posts him to the far eastern frontier for 150 years. | **Why Napoleon has never faced the Cohort, and why Lannes has never had to face him.** |
| 1863 | Shaw and Stonewall Jackson arrive, months apart, on opposite sides again. Shaw goes to the Bastion. Jackson goes to the Gate, will not surrender, and becomes the Covenant's sword and the guarantor of the Accord. | Jackson writes the demand in Ch 20. |
| 1885 | **Gordon arrives.** Keeper of the Bastion from about 1910. Keeps the Accord as a sacred trust, and corresponds with Jackson through the envoys for sixty years. | |
| 1941 to 1944 | Lewes (1941), Sophie (1943), Rommel (1944) arrive. Lewes joins the Cohort. Sophie goes to the Bastion and challenges everyone in it. Rommel joins the Covenant as a Strategos. | |
| About 1950 | **Hale leaves the Bastion** for the Cohort, pushed by Sophie's question of what his ideals are for if he only sits with them. | Resolves Hale's double listing. |
| Late 1960s | The Cohort, now strong enough to hold the marches, begins sending Lewes's scouts onto the plain to reach new arrivals before the Covenant does. Alexander recalls Napoleon from the east and orders him to take the passes onto the plain. | **Why the campaign happens now.** |
| 1970 | **Bray arrives.** Book 1 begins. | |

### The custom of titles

The Cohort's members go by titles, not names: the First Flame, the Marshal of the Reclaimed, the Shieldbearer, the Flamekeeper, the Captain of Shadows. The custom comes from the Cohort's second ideal: *what you were remembered for isn't always what you lived for.* A name is what history carved; a title is what you chose here.

**This solves two plot problems:**

- Napoleon has fought "the Marshal of the Reclaimed" in dispatches. He learns in Book 1 that the Marshal is Louverture.
- Lannes, as "the Shieldbearer," has never been named in a Covenant report.

## 2. Geography of Book 1

All of Book 1 happens in one region: the western edge of the grey plain and the hill country below it.

```
                 THE GREY PLAIN (high, cold, under the Pall)
        arrival lands ...... the Bastion (on a rise near the center)
                 |                                      |
        THE WEST STAIR                           THE EAST STAIR
   (pass down to the terraces)              (pass down; Covenant side)
                 |                                      |
     plain-watchers' village  ---- the terrace road ----  THE WATCH-FORT
     (high terraces, just                (along the plain's      (old Covenant fort
      below the Pall)                     edge, 2 to 3 days)      at the top of the stair)
                 |                                      |
          THE MIDDLE STAIR (the pass Napoleon wants most)
                 |
     THE MARCHES: hill country, river valley, free native clans
     Cohort camp in the western hills;  river road runs east
                 |
     COVENANT LANDS (east, beyond the river): the old empire
```

| Place | Description | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| **The grey plain** ("the Grey") | A high plateau of crystalline grey grit, thin cold air, almost nothing growing. Where Eternal Ones arrive. No army can live on it for more than a few days. | Ch 6 to 9, 24, 27 |
| **The Pall** | A permanent haze of mineral dust over the plain and its upper edge. No sun is visible inside it. Visibility drops to yards in places; horses balk in it. | Every plain chapter. A refuge in Ch 18 and 27. |
| **The Bastion** | On a rise near the plain's center, about two days' walk from the West Stair. Gates always open under the Accord. | Ch 9 to 14, 20 to 22, 24, 33 |
| **The three stairs** | The only passes down from the plain. Whoever holds them catches arrivals coming off the plain. | Campaign goal |
| **Plain-watchers' village** | On high terraces just under the Pall, below the West Stair. Terraced barley and goats. | Ch 15, 18 |
| **The terrace road** | Runs along the plain's edge between the West and East Stairs. The only route east once the river road is cut. | Napoleon's retreat, Ch 17 to 18 |
| **The Watch-Fort** | An old Covenant fortress at the top of the East Stair, on the edge of the Pall. Small garrison, deep cisterns, cliff at its back. Napoleon's army camps in the valley below it. | Ch 21 to 29 |
| **The marches** | Hill country and a river valley below the plain. Free native clans and river towns, allied to the Cohort only because the Covenant is worse. | Ch 8, 12, 16, 19 and 32 |
| **The Cohort camp** | A hill fort and valley camp in the western marches, a day's ride from the Middle Stair. | Ch 8, 21, 25, 32 |

**Rough travel times on foot:** Bastion to West Stair, two days. West Stair to the village, half a day. Village to the Watch-Fort along the terrace road, two to three days. Village to the Cohort camp, two days.

## 3. The Bastion Accord

| | |
|---|---|
| **Made** | About 1000 CE, after the storming of the Bastion. |
| **Between** | Alexander and the Bastion's keeper of that time. (The author may want that keeper to be a historical figure. Not needed for Book 1.) |
| **Guaranteed by** | Since 1863, Stonewall Jackson for the Covenant and the keeper for the Bastion. Gordon since about 1910. |

**Terms (proposed):**

1. The Covenant shall not enter the Bastion in arms or harm anyone within its walls.
2. The Uncrowned shall bear no arms beyond the walls.
3. The Bastion shall shelter no one at war with the Covenant.
4. The Bastion's gates shall stand open, and Covenant envoys may speak to any new arrival.

**The visible sign** is the open gates. The Codex already describes "entrances once defended now left open." Those gates have not closed in a thousand years. Their hinges have rusted.

**Why each side keeps it:**

- **Alexander:** Every soul sitting in the Bastion is a soul not in the Cohort. The envoy clause lets him recruit there.
- **The Uncrowned:** It has kept them alive. Most have forgotten what it costs.

**How Book 1 breaks it:**

- Ch 18: Bray bears arms beyond the walls (term 2).
- Ch 20: The Bastion takes in the village refugees, who guided Cohort scouts (term 3).
- Ch 22: Gordon refuses Jackson's demand. The gates close.

## 4. The peoples of Eirion in Book 1

### The plain-watchers

A small hill people on the high terraces under the West Stair.

- **Belief:** The Grey is where gods walk out of the ash. A god who reaches the "house of the open gate" (the Bastion) is free; a god the Covenant takes becomes a slave-master. So they watch the plain and guide arrivals to the Bastion. It is a religious duty.
- **History:** Spartacus's first cohort freed their ancestors from a Covenant estate. They have helped every cohort since, in secret. Lewes's scouts work through them.
- **The earthworks:** The geometric scars Bray finds in Ch 6 are their guide-lines, cut into the plain to point arrivals toward the Bastion. Wind and centuries have half-erased them. Only a watcher can read them now.
- **Trade with the Bastion:** Barley, goat cheese, and wool go up the stair. Medicine, bone-setting, clean-water methods, and letters come down. Gordon has made the trip for sixty years.
- **Language:** Their own. A few know the Covenant trade tongue. No Eternal One understands them without learning (rule R4).
- **The cost:** Every generation, the Covenant punishes them for something. They endure because the duty matters more.

### The marches clans

Free hill clans and river towns between the plain and the empire.

- They want neither side's gods. They ally with the Cohort because it promises to leave once the Covenant is gone.
- **The river lord (new, proposed):** Leader of the strongest clan. Toussaint's diplomacy with them wins the battle: their riders cut Napoleon's river road. Their price, which Toussaint pays in Ch 12, is a sworn promise that the Cohort will rule no one in the marches after the war. It is the first concrete answer to Toussaint's own question, "What do we do after we win?" It also plants Bray's Book 3 position.

### The Covenant's natives

The heartland of Alexander's empire, east of the river.

- Two thousand years of worship. Most believe sincerely.
- Soldiers are drilled, paid, and proud.
- Estates run on enslaved labor, taken from border peoples like the plain-watchers.
- Educated clerks keep the empire's records in a Greek-descended script. That script is the "familiar" one on the carved posts. Bray reads it by the crossing's gift only when an Eternal One wrote it.

## 5. How the factions are organized in Book 1

### The Covenant

- **Alexander:** Far east, offstage.
- **Napoleon's western army:** About 12,000 native infantry in drilled blocks with long spears, about 2,000 heavy horse, a siege train of torsion engines, and a dozen Eternal officers. Napoleon was an artillery officer; the powder ban took away the weapon his genius was built on. **This is his real motive for powder:** not only power, but getting his own way of war back.
- **The Finders:** Small mounted parties that sweep the plain for arrivals. They bring them east to be "offered the Covenant." Those who refuse are held, then "sent east." Bray sees a Finder party in Ch 7.
- **Envoys:** Rommel visits the Bastion under the Accord's fourth term.

### The Last Cohort

Under 6,000 under arms in the marches:

- Spartacus's warband (fast, light, savage)
- Lannes's line infantry (shield wall, the steadiest troops on Eirion)
- Joan's wing (native volunteers who believe she is holy)
- Lewes's scouts and network
- Hale serving under Lannes
- Allied marches riders

**The council:** The five titled members. Decisions are reached by talking until there is agreement. A vote has never been needed. Book 1's vote is the first.

### The Uncrowned

About forty souls in the Bastion. Most are minor and quiet. **On page:** Gordon (keeper), Shaw, Sophie, and two or three background faces the author can invent. **No hierarchy;** the keeper holds the Accord, nothing more.

## 6. Rules for the Mirror Hall and the Hall of Names

**The Hall of Names**

- New arrivals carve their own name when they choose to claim it. No one carves it for them.
- When a soul leaves the Bastion for good, the keeper scratches a single line through their name. It is not erased.
- Names written over themselves belong to souls who changed what they called themselves.
- The Hall is the only continuous record of arrivals on Eirion that the Covenant does not control. **That is why it matters in Ch 32 and 33.**

**The Mirror Hall**

- A sealed chamber of polished steel and old glass, with near-total darkness and silence and one shielded light.
- A soul sits alone with their reflection for as long as they can stand it.
- What happens is psychological: isolation and one's own face until the mind gives up what it has been holding. "It does not show your past. It shows your possibility" means you see who you could have been and admit it.
- Most last minutes. Some never go in. Gordon has never gone in. (Seed for later.)
