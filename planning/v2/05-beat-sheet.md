# Rethink v2: Book 1 Beat Sheet

**Status:** Shape approved by the author, 10/20/2026, including the Ch 17 midpoint and the four open beats below. Chapter numbers may still shift during outlining.

## Shape

- **Length:** About 115,000 words, 34 chapters, about 3,400 words each.
- **Viewpoints:** Bray (17 chapters), Toussaint (8), Gordon (6), the plain-watcher (3 short), plus one short Napoleon epilogue.
- **Interludes:** Each death interlude opens the chapter where that character first matters. Bray's death is Chapter 1.

| Quarter | Chapters | Movement | Ends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Act 1 | 1 to 8 | Arrival | The village clearing (about 25 percent) |
| Act 2, first half | 9 to 17 | Into the war | Victory, then the broken word (midpoint, 50 percent) |
| Act 2, second half | 18 to 26 | Consequences | Toussaint, Scholl, and Shaw lost (low point, about 75 percent) |
| Act 3 | 27 to 34 | The Uncrowned form | The first mission, on the natives' terms |

## Act 1: Arrival (Ch 1 to 8)

| Ch | Viewpoint | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bray | **Opening.** Vietnam. Bray dies three months into his second tour. Tate, Thompson's order, and the friendly fire are planted. |
| 2 | Bray | Wakes on the grey plain. Covenant hunters take him and a Vietnamese soldier who died in the same war. |
| 3 | Watcher | The watcher's people guide arrivals to the refuge. The watcher gets Bray out through the haze. The Vietnamese soldier is left behind. |
| 4 | Toussaint | The Cohort and the river lord negotiate in the marches. His condition: the Cohort rules no one afterward. Spartacus agrees. Toussaint vouches for him. Napoleon's army is coming. |
| 5 | Bray | Opens with Shaw's death interlude. Shaw, on patrol, meets Bray and the watcher. The road to the Bastion. |
| 6 | Bray | The Bastion of Broken Banners: a refuge of the waiting dead. Bray finds Hale's "Keep walking" note. Gordon's rule: no one here takes part. |
| 7 | Gordon | Opens with Gordon's death interlude (Khartoum). Gordon as keeper. He sees in Bray the restlessness he buried. A Covenant envoy asks for the escaped arrival. Gordon refuses, on neutrality grounds only. |
| 8 | Bray | **End of Act 1.** Bray and Shaw, with the watcher, go down to a native village at the plain's edge. The colonel clears it to punish villages that help the resisters. Thompson's order again, from the other side. Bray acts, too late to save most of them. |

## Act 2, first half: Into the war (Ch 9 to 17)

| Ch | Viewpoint | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | Bray | The aftermath. Survivors name the 54th sergeant as one of the resisters who came to help. Shaw learns his own sergeant has been fighting while he waited. |
| 10 | Gordon | Shaw tells Gordon he is leaving. Gordon forbids it. Shaw goes anyway. Decades of friendship break. Gordon stays. |
| 11 | Toussaint | The campaign. Toussaint maneuvers against Napoleon with the river lord's fighters. Toussaint studies the man who betrayed him in life. |
| 12 | Bray | Opens with Hale's death interlude. Bray, Shaw, and the watcher go down into the marches. Lewes's scouts find them. Bray meets Hale in person at last. |
| 13 | Watcher | The watcher's divided family. Some believe in Alexander. Saving Bray has cost them at home. The watcher chooses to stay with Bray anyway. |
| 14 | Bray | With the resisters. Bray meets the 54th sergeant and the river lord. The eve of battle. Bray is asked to fight and refuses to take a banner. He agrees to hold a crossing with the sergeant, for the villages, not for the Cohort. |
| 15 | Toussaint | The battle, part 1. Toussaint baits Napoleon onto the river lord's ground. |
| 16 | Bray | The battle, part 2. Bray holds the crossing with the sergeant. He fights for people, not a cause, and it works. |
| 17 | Toussaint | **Midpoint.** Napoleon retreats. Victory. Within days Spartacus garrisons a native town "for its protection" and overrules its council. The broken word. Toussaint must choose between his word and his commander. He stays silent. **Joan, who lived through the Maid's Cohort, speaks against the garrison at the council and is overruled; she stays.** Hale sees everything. |

## Act 2, second half: Consequences (Ch 18 to 26)

| Ch | Viewpoint | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 18 | Bray | Hale turns. "We traded one master for another." It costs him Lewes's friendship. Hale joins Bray, Shaw, and the watcher. |
| 19 | Gordon | Alone at the Bastion. The Covenant envoy returns with Napoleon's demand. Gordon sees Alexander's god-king rule as the empire he served in the Sudan. He refuses, but still will not leave. |
| 20 | Toussaint | Compromised by his silence. Napoleon, beaten, proposes a parley over prisoners. Toussaint suspects a trap. |
| 21 | Bray | The clerk's copied text reaches Bray: words of doubt spreading inside Covenant lands. Its writer is Scholl. Bray's group crosses into Alexander's territory. |
| 22 | Bray | Opens with Scholl's death interlude. Bray meets Scholl among natives who believe in a god-king. She challenges his complicity and will not let him off the hook. |
| 23 | Gordon | In the Bastion's records Gordon finds a pattern in who passed through and where they went. A first lead for later. Word reaches him of where Shaw has gone. |
| 24 | Toussaint | The parley. Toussaint walks in knowing it may be a trap, because captives' lives are at stake. He is taken, the 1802 betrayal again. |
| 25 | Bray | **Low point.** Bray learns where the hunters hold the Vietnamese soldier and goes for him, against Scholl's advice. His guilt from Ch 2 drives it. The attempt leads the colonel to her readers. The raid: Shaw covers the escape, is badly wounded, and is left for dead. Scholl is taken. Guilt for one man costs many. |
| 26 | Watcher | The bottom. Bray wants to go back to the Bastion and wait. The watcher tells him what waiting costs their people. |

## Act 3: The Uncrowned (Ch 27 to 34)

| Ch | Viewpoint | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 27 | Gordon | News of Shaw's death reaches the Bastion. Gordon blames Bray, then himself. |
| 28 | Toussaint | In Napoleon's fort: Toussaint, Scholl, the Vietnamese soldier, and Shaw, alive, captured off the field. Napoleon offers Toussaint a seat. He refuses. With the clerk, he plans his own escape. |
| 29 | Bray | The clerk gets word out that Shaw is alive. Joan pushes the Cohort to go for them: "No one came for me." Hale: "Someone must go." Bray volunteers. |
| 30 | Gordon | **Gordon commits, the last to join.** Learning Shaw is alive, he leaves the Bastion. He brings what he found in the records and the authority of an elder. |
| 31 | Toussaint | The escape, on Toussaint's plan. Lannes carries out the outside half and reveals himself to Napoleon. |
| 32 | Bray | Bray faces the colonel, his mirror. The watcher leads the escapees out by their people's hidden haze path, then closes it to every immortal afterward: Covenant, Cohort, and Bray. The land belongs to the natives. The Uncrowned's belief in action before anyone says it. |
| 33 | Bray | After. The Vietnamese soldier is free and refuses to join Bray's group. The Cohort's broken word stands between Toussaint and Spartacus. Scholl names what they share: no immortal should rule. |
| 34 | Bray | The Uncrowned. The river lord asks them to get the Cohort out of the town Spartacus garrisoned at the midpoint. Bray, Shaw, Hale, Scholl, and Gordon accept. Their first act is against their own ally, proof they mean "no one rules." The larger goal is hinted, not stated. |
| Epilogue | Napoleon | After Lannes's betrayal. Napoleon begins making the forbidden powder. The hook into Book 2. |

## Decided 10/20/2026

- Midpoint: the battle and the broken word, Ch 17.
- Ch 25: Bray goes for the Vietnamese soldier, which exposes Scholl.
- Ch 32: The watcher closes the haze path to all immortals.
- Napoleon: one short epilogue, no other viewpoint chapters.
- Ch 34: The first mission is to free the town the Cohort garrisoned.
