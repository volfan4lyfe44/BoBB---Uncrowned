# Rethink v2: Scene Outline, Chapters 1 to 3

**Status:** Decided by the author, 10/20/2026. This is a scaffold for the author's draft, not prose. Word counts are targets. "Source" notes which existing pages can be reused.

## Chapter 1: The Unwritten Ending (Bray, about 3,000 words)

*Epithet: The Reluctant Warrior.*

**Job of the chapter:** Open in combat with the protagonist. Plant what Eirion will echo: Thompson's order, the village mission, Tate, the friendly fire, and Hải.

**Source:** Current Ch 5, "The Unwritten Ending" (about 470 words), expanded. Keep the Chesterton epigraph.

| Scene | Words | Beats |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The night before | ~800 | The perimeter in the heat. Bray awake. Tate's absence is felt: Bray turns Tate's Zippo over in his hands. The question he cannot answer: why he came back. Greeneville and "Moose," compressed from the existing paragraph. |
| 2. The briefing | ~600 | Captain Thompson briefs the village mission: suspected Viet Cong. His order: "Hit whatever you see." Bray's unease, unspoken. Private Jenkins, young and eager or afraid. |
| 3. The march and the firefight | ~1,200 | From the existing chapter: three klicks out, the shots, the left flank. The movement is Hải: a young man's face, clear for one beat. Both hesitate. In that beat, Bray is shot from behind. The same fire kills Hải. Bray sees him fall. |
| 4. The death and the presence | ~400 | From the existing chapter: the radio, the lieutenant, Jenkins's "No, no, no," Tate, the leaves moving where no wind moves, the presence's words. Keep the closing verse unchanged. |

**Plants:**
- **Friendly fire (planted only):** Bray faces the left flank; the shot comes from behind and hits high on his back. Bray does not know. "Something about this story wasn't right" already points at it.
- **Hải:** Neither man fires at the other. They share the moment of death. Neither will recognize the other in Eirion until later in the book.
- **Closing verse as prophecy:** "Found his name written among the Uncrowned" stays in all five death chapters. It is the presence's foresight. The Uncrowned do not exist until these five found them.

## Chapter 2: The Grey Waking (Bray, about 3,400 words)

**Job of the chapter:** Wake, find the first signs of others, and lose freedom by the end. Conflict arrives in the second half.

**Source:** Current Ch 6, trimmed hard. Cut the Coach Johnson, Ryan, and Mr. Smith flashbacks from this chapter (Coach Johnson can return later).

| Scene | Words | Beats |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The waking | ~600 | From Ch 6, compressed. His hand finds no rifle. He reaches for the wound high on his back and finds whole skin, with a detail he pushes away. The tags. The silence. The grey. |
| 2. The cave | ~1,100 | From Ch 6. The basin, the amphora, the rucksack, the tricorn, and Hale's note: "Keep walking." Spartacus's inscription. The fire, lit with Tate's Zippo. Bray leaves the tricorn at the cave. |
| 3. The night | ~700 | From Ch 6, trimmed: the Tate dream (the cards, the ribs, the opium). He wakes and does not sleep again. |
| 4. The hunters | ~1,000 | **New.** At first light, the sound of horses (from Ch 7's "distant thunk"). Native troops under a minor Eternal officer who serves Colonel Marchal. Bray understands the officer and none of his men. On a led horse, a bound man: Vietnamese, a stranger to him. Bray, weak and starving, is taken. Last image: bound beside Hải, Bray sees a figure on a far ridge, still, watching. |

**Notes:**
- The language rule is on the page from the start: Eternal Ones understand one another; no Eternal understands native speech without learning it. Bray and Hải can talk. Neither can talk to the troops.
- Ch 7's ending, "a lone figure, watching," moves here. The figure is Ishel.

## Chapter 3: The Watcher (Ishel, about 2,500 words, a short viewpoint chapter)

**Job of the chapter:** Show the natives from the inside, give Ishel her own duty and judgment, and start Bray's guilt with his own choice.

**Source:** New. No existing pages.

| Scene | Words | Beats |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The watch | ~700 | Ishel's duty: her people guide new arrivals to the refuge before the Covenant takes them, for their own reasons (every Eternal the Covenant gains makes Alexander stronger). She has watched Bray since he woke. She saw his fire. The hunters were faster. A hint of her divided family (developed in Ch 13). |
| 2. The camp | ~800 | She follows and watches. The Eternal officer. The two captives speak to each other in a tongue no one else in the camp uses, which tells her both are arrivals. To her, Bray's English and Hải's Vietnamese are equally foreign. |
| 3. The cut | ~600 | She can reach only the man at the edge of the camp. She cuts Bray loose and signals him to follow. From her viewpoint: he looks back at Hải, bound among the guards, and goes anyway. She sees the look, and the choice. |
| 4. The haze, and the turn back | ~400 | She leads him by gesture and touch through the haze; he reads her hand signals like a soldier. That night, safe, Bray starts back toward the camp for Hải. She blocks his way. Their first clash of wills. She wins. |

**Notes:**
- Gesture only. No shared words in this chapter.
- Her viewpoint is the first in the book that is not an immortal's. Let Eirion feel ordinary to her and the Eternal Ones feel strange.

## Decided 10/20/2026

- **Zippo:** Tate's. Bray carries it across (what is on the body crosses) and lights his first fire in Eirion with it.
- **Lieutenant and Jenkins:** Keep both. The lieutenant stays unnamed.
- **The hunters' officer:** Serves under Marchal and recurs at the village clearing in Ch 8, where Bray recognizes him. He is named there.
- **Tricorn:** Left at the cave. Hale finds it later and knows someone read his note, a hook for their meeting in Ch 12.
- **Titles:** Ch 1 "The Unwritten Ending" (epithet: The Reluctant Warrior). Ch 2 "The Grey Waking." Ch 3 "The Watcher."
- **Epithets:** Death chapters only. Regular chapters carry a title alone.
- **File renumbering:** Waits until the new drafts exist.
