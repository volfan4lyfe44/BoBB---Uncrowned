# Rethink v2: The Gathering Map

**Status:** In progress. Decided by the author, 10/19/2026, person by person.

Book 1 is the story of how the Uncrowned form. Each squad member arrived in Eirion in the order they died and is scattered when the book opens. This map records where each one starts, how Bray reaches them, and what each brings.

| Character | Died | Years in Eirion by 1970 |
|---|---|---|
| Nathan Hale | 1776, age 21 | About 194 |
| Robert Gould Shaw | 1863, age 25 | About 107 |
| Charles Gordon | 1885, age 51 | About 85 |
| Sophie Scholl | 1943, age 21 | About 27 |
| Peyton Bray | 1970, age 21 | Arrives |

## Charles Gordon

- **Starts:** Keeper of the Bastion, for decades.
- **Stance:** The last to join. He holds out until the climax, and his joining completes the group.
- **Brings:** Memory and records (the Bastion's history and who passed through it), and the wisdom of an elder. He was 51 at death, older than most of the squad, and has lived more life on Earth than most Eternal Ones.
- **Colonial past:** Mirrored by Alexander. In a god-king ruling natives he sees the empire he served in the Sudan and China. Fighting Alexander means admitting what he took part in, a parallel to Bray's complicity.

## Robert Gould Shaw

- **Starts:** Lives at the Bastion as Gordon's closest companion, but spends much of his time outside it on patrol.
- **Meets Bray:** Through both roles: on patrol and at the gate. Exact first meeting to be placed in the outline.
- **Role in the call to action:** First in the Bastion to side with Bray. He breaks with Gordon, his closest friend, to do it. That break is the emotional cost of the first act.
- **Motive:** The 54th sergeant. What happens to the sergeant, or to natives under Alexander, shows Shaw he has been waiting while others pay the cost. The sergeant is a man with his own choices, not Shaw's lesson.
- **Gate line:** The author writes a new line to replace "We've been waiting for you." It greets a stranger to a refuge, not a recruit to a group.

## Nathan Hale

- **Starts:** A Cohort scout with Lewes's team, sweeping the plain for new arrivals. Fits his old life as a spy. He left the "Keep walking" note and the tricorn that Bray finds.
- **Meets Bray:** Note first, person later. Bray finds the note and tricorn early. By the time they meet, the note has made Hale a guide Bray has never seen.
- **Turn:** Sees the Cohort's limits. He watches it start to act as a power of its own, deciding for the natives instead of beside them. His idealism cannot accept new rulers, even good ones.
- **Brings:** A spy's craft (moving unseen, reading people) and contacts inside the Cohort. His "someone must go" becomes Bray's volunteer moment.

## Sophie Scholl

- **Starts:** Inside Covenant lands, among natives who believe in Alexander, quietly spreading doubt. The White Rose again: resistance by words in the god-king's own territory. The most dangerous position of the five, and the farthest from the Bastion.
- **Meets Bray:** Her words reach him first, through a doubting native (such as the clerk) or a copied text. Reaching her means going into Covenant territory, a turning point in the journey.
- **Role:** Both Bray's challenger and the one who names the belief. She will not let him off the hook on complicity, and she is first to say what they share: no immortal should rule. The book's realization is the others catching up to her.
- **Conflict:** Words versus weapons. Her fear is that words without weapons are forgotten. She must choose whether to take up arms or stay with words when the people she lives among are being hurt.

## Peyton Bray

- **First contact:** Covenant hunters who search the plain for new arrivals find him first. Conflict from his first scene in Eirion. Replaces the long solitary walk in the current Ch 6 and 7, which will change substantially.
- **Route:** Shaw (the Bastion and patrol), then Hale (note early, person mid-book), then Scholl (Covenant lands, second half), then Gordon (commits last, at the climax).
- **Inciting act:** A village clearing. Covenant soldiers clear a native village under the fictional colonel's order, an echo of Vietnam. Bray sees it from the other side. Whether he acts or fails to act, it gets him moving.
- **Goal at the end of Book 1:** Free or protect one specific native region, on the natives' terms. The larger aim (end immortal rule) is hinted at, not stated. Book 1 ends with a team, a shared belief, and a first mission.
- **Escape:** The plain-watcher gets him out by a path through the haze the hunters do not know. Bray owes his first escape to Eirion's own people. The watcher then takes him toward the Bastion.

## The Vietnamese Eternal One (fictional)

- **In life:** A soldier of the North Vietnamese Army or the Viet Cong, possibly killed in the same engagement as Bray. The enemy Bray was trained to see, now a person in the same place.
- **Capture:** Taken by the same Covenant hunters as Bray. Bray escapes; the Vietnamese Eternal does not. Two men from opposite sides of one war, and only the American gets out. That guilt runs through Book 1.
- **Membership:** Never joins the Uncrowned. They choose the Cohort or their own path, and remain Bray's mirror, not a member.

## The plain-watcher (native lead)

- **Why they save Bray:** Duty to their people. The watchers guide new arrivals to the neutral refuge so the Covenant cannot take them. An old tradition, done for their own reasons: every Eternal the Covenant gains makes Alexander stronger.
- **Their people:** Divided. Some believe in Alexander and some resist. Saving Bray costs the watcher at home.
- **Arc:** Stays with Bray through the journey (the haze paths, the language, native politics). The person through whom Bray sees Eirion. At the end, the watcher makes the decisive choice, and it is theirs, not Bray's.
- **Uncrowned?** Never a member. The Uncrowned are immortals who choose not to rule. The watcher is their partner and conscience. Fits the series ending: immortals leave the natives alone.

## Toussaint Louverture

- **Crossing with Bray:** The village clearing. The Cohort and native resisters are fighting Napoleon nearby. Napoleon's retreat, or his punishment of villages that help the resisters, causes the clearing that starts Bray moving. Both threads begin in the same place.
- **View of the Uncrowned:** A respectful rival. He knows how revolutions become new thrones and fears it for the Cohort, but he sees refusing to rule as a luxury while natives are enslaved. A sharp, respectful argument with Bray and Scholl.
- **The Napoleon rematch:** Stays in Book 1, all of it: he outfights Napoleon, is captured, is offered a seat, and refuses. It runs alongside Bray's journey in Toussaint's viewpoint chapters, and the threads meet at the climax.
- **Rescue:** He frees himself. He plans the escape from inside, using the clerk; Lannes and the natives carry out his plan outside. Bray's group helps, but the plan is his.

## The rest of the Cohort

- **Spartacus:** The Cohort's risk. A liberator whose drive to win could turn the Cohort into a new power, and who does not trust anyone who will not fight. Not a villain. He is what Hale sees when Hale sees the Cohort's limits.
- **Jock Lewes:** Hale's commander and friend. Hale's turn to the Uncrowned costs him that friendship. A few scenes, and the "J.L." clue in Ch 7.
- **Joan of Arc:** Pushes the rescue. "No one came for me." She insists the Cohort go for Toussaint and the Vietnamese Eternal held with him, which forces the Cohort and Bray's group into the same operation.
- **Jean Lannes:** The outside half of Toussaint's escape plan. Napoleon sees him at the moment of escape: the marshal he loved most, on the other side. The climax's sharpest personal moment.

## Supporting characters

- **The colonel (fictional):** A recurring mirror. After the clearing he leads hunters or guards later in the book, and Bray faces him at the climax. Bray sees in him what he could have become: a man who calls himself a professional. Survives into Book 2, unrepentant.
- **The 54th sergeant:** With the native resisters. He left the Bastion years ago, tired of waiting. Shaw must face that his own sergeant acted while he waited. The sergeant has his own goal and story.
- **The clerk:** Scholl's reader. He has read her words, which is how Bray first hears of her. Inside Napoleon's fort he helps with Toussaint's escape. One character linking two threads, making his own choice at real risk.
- **The river lord:** Sets the terms. Leads the native resistance and allies with the Cohort for the battle on one condition: the Cohort rules no one afterward. The Uncrowned's first mission at the end happens on his terms.

## The schism in the Cohort

- Spartacus agrees to the river lord's condition to win the alliance. Toussaint vouches for him.
- After the battle, the Cohort starts deciding for the natives anyway. Toussaint must choose between his word and his commander.
- Hale sees it happen. This is the specific thing that turns him to the Uncrowned.
- The Uncrowned's belief is the river lord's condition taken all the way: no immortal rules anyone, ever.
- The split widens across the series and sets up the Cohort crossing the Gate first in Book 3.
