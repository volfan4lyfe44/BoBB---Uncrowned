## José Rizal (1861 to 1896), Philippines

**Who:** Physician, novelist, poet, and reformer in the Spanish-ruled Philippines. His novels *Noli Me Tangere* (1887) and *El Filibusterismo* (1891) exposed colonial and clerical abuse, were banned, and helped spark Filipino nationalism. He worked for reform inside the Spanish system, not armed revolt. Exiled to Dapitan. Today the national hero of the Philippines.

**Death:** Executed by firing squad at Bagumbayan, Manila, about 7:00 a.m. on 12/30/1896, after a court-martial for rebellion. In mid-1896 he had told the Katipunan the planned revolution was premature. On 12/15/1896 he wrote a manifesto denouncing the rising; it was suppressed. **Unfinished:** died at 35, condemned for a revolution he opposed, claimed by both sides.

**Fit:** Uncrowned, possibly by way of the Covenant. He could begin as a Covenant scholar or official who believes Alexander's rule can be made humane, then break with it, as he broke with Spain. *He would argue:* a people that trades one master for another has not been freed.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The inside man: 74 years of access to imperial records gives the Uncrowned proof of what Alexander does to the natives. (2) The writer who drafts the Uncrowned's founding argument and warns that words get co-opted. (3) Gordon's counterpart: a colonized man and a colonizer in the same faction.

**Represents:** Reform against revolution; the power and limits of the pen; heroes shaped by those in power.

**Risks:** National hero, studied in every Philippine school. Renato Constantino's "Veneration Without Understanding" (1969) argues the American regime promoted him to discourage armed independence. The deathbed retraction controversy is unresolved (unverified details). Do not make him a saint or Bray's teacher.

## Emiliano Zapata (1879 to 1919), Mexico

**Who:** Horse trainer and village leader from Anenecuilco, Morelos; the leading agrarian commander of the Mexican Revolution. His Plan de Ayala (11/1911) demanded that hacienda land be returned to the villages. He fought Díaz, Madero, Huerta, and Carranza in turn and never sought national office. Watchword: "Tierra y Libertad."

**Death:** Killed 04/10/1919 at the Hacienda de Chinameca. Colonel Jesús Guajardo, under General Pablo González, faked a defection and lured him to a meeting. **Unfinished:** died at 39, betrayed by men of the same revolution; land reform came later, on other men's terms.

**The chair, 12/1914:** *Documented:* Villa sat in the presidential chair at the National Palace and Zapata did not, as the photographs show. *Legend:* his remark that the chair should be burned "to put an end to ambitions" appears only in secondary sources. Samuel Brunk introduces it with "some say." Another version credits his brother Eufemio. Use it as legend, with characters arguing over it.

**Fit:** The Cohort, as the conscience of its midpoint break. *He would argue:* the land belongs to those who work it, and any leader who sits in the palace before returning it has already betrayed the cause.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The midpoint walkout: he confronts Spartacus over the garrison, and fighters follow him out, a bridge to the Uncrowned. (2) The chair scene in Eirion: he refuses a captured seat of power. (3) He distrusts defectors, having died by a fake one, which creates tension with any Covenant turncoat.

**Represents:** Land against crown; power refused on principle; revolutions betrayed by their own victors.

**Risks:** A national icon claimed by the left, the state, and the EZLN. His grandson threatened legal action over a 2019 painting. Avoid the bandit caricature, the noble-peasant cliché, and sombrero-and-bandolier shorthand.

## Yaa Asantewaa (c. 1840 to 1921), Asante, Ghana

**Who:** Queen Mother (ohemaa) of Ejisu in the Asante kingdom. After the British exiled the Asantehene Prempeh I in 1896, she acted as regent. In March 1900 Governor Frederick Hodgson demanded the Golden Stool, the sacred seat holding the soul of the Asante nation. She rallied the leaders, led the War of the Golden Stool, and besieged the British in the Kumasi fort.

**Death:** Captured or surrendered in 1901, exiled to the Seychelles, died there 10/17/1921. **Unfinished:** died in exile, three years before Prempeh I returned; the British never got the Stool, but she never knew the kingdom would survive.

**Fit:** Uncrowned, as the advocate of native sovereignty and its hardest critic, or an independent ally of the native resisters. *She would argue:* outsiders, kind or cruel, do not get to decide who sits on another people's stool, including deciding there should be no stool at all.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Guardian of a sacred native object Alexander demands as proof of submission. (2) The check on the white-savior risk: she calls out Bray, Shaw, and Gordon whenever they speak for the natives. Gordon is a direct parallel to the officers she fought. (3) A siege commander, giving the Uncrowned a victory not led by a Westerner.

**Represents:** Sovereignty and the sacred; women's leadership in a non-Western political tradition; exile and the long wait for return.

**Risks:** National heroine in Ghana; the Ejisu Queen Mother office still exists. Her famous war speech survives only in oral versions; present it as tradition. Avoid "warrior queen" exoticism and generic "tribal" mysticism; Asante was a complex state. The Golden Stool is sacred to living people. Get Asante terms right.

## Tupac Amaru II (José Gabriel Condorcanqui, c. 1738 to 1781), Peru

**Who:** An Indigenous noble and kuraka in colonial Cusco, and a mule-train operator, who claimed descent from the last Inca, Túpac Amaru I. After years of legal petitions against forced labor, he seized and executed the corregidor Antonio de Arriaga in 11/1780, starting the largest anti-colonial rising in 18th-century Spanish America. His wife, Micaela Bastidas, was a central organizer and commander.

**Death:** Captured in spring 1781 (March or April; sources conflict). On 05/18/1781 in Cusco's Plaza de Armas he was made to watch the executions of his wife and eldest son, then horses failed to pull him apart and he was beheaded. **Unfinished:** the rising went on two more years without him, and historians still argue whether he sought reform or independence.

**Fit:** The Cohort, as a senior general whose claim to rule is the danger: a liberator the natives might crown. *He would argue:* the natives need a ruler who knows their suffering, not no ruler at all.

**Plot jobs:** (1) "The other king": natives start hailing him as a restored ruler, the Uncrowned's founding question in its hardest form. (2) Bray tells him that Peru's military government made him the symbol of its 1969 land reform; he learns he has become a state icon. (3) The cost of terror: his rising saw reprisals on both sides, a debate with Toussaint and Spartacus.

**Represents:** Restoration against liberation; legitimacy by blood; history turning rebels into state symbols.

**Risks:** National hero across the Andes; his name was taken by the Tupamaros and the MRTA. Scholars disagree on his aims. Avoid "last Inca" mysticism, the noble savage, and gore. Do not sideline Micaela Bastidas. He was literate, Catholic, and bilingual, and fought through lawsuits before arms.

2. Ward Stavig and Ella Schmidt, eds., *The Tupac Amaru and Catarista Rebellions: An Anthology of Sources* (Hackett, 2008). Primary in translation. https://search.worldcat.org/oclc/174501545

## Rani Lakshmibai (c. 1828 or 1835 to 1858), Jhansi, India

**Who:** Queen of Jhansi. When her husband died in 1853, the East India Company refused to recognize their adopted son and annexed the state in 1854 under the Doctrine of Lapse. She became a leader of the 1857 rebellion, escaped the fort when Sir Hugh Rose stormed Jhansi in 1858, and helped take Gwalior.

**Death:** Killed in June 1858 at Kotah-ki-Serai near Gwalior (17 or 18 June; India commemorates 06/18). Battle details are contested. **Unfinished:** died mid-campaign, in her twenties, with her son's claim unresolved.

**Fit:** The Cohort, as the Uncrowned's hardest test: she fought both to keep her people's land free and to restore her son's throne. *She would argue:* I did not fight the Company so a better master could take its place, but a people at war needs someone to lead it.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Cavalry commander who rides with native fighters, so the broken word costs her personally. (2) The regent's argument: what if the natives ask an Eternal to lead them? (3) Mirror to Napoleon: both lost to British-led forces; one chose empire, one chose the resisters.

**Represents:** Resistance to annexation dressed as legal process; fighting for your people versus your crown.

**Risks:** Major national hero with a contested image, including Hindu-nationalist use. Avoid the exoticized "warrior princess." Her role in the 1857 killings at Jhansi is disputed. **Overlap: she is an FGO Servant (4-star Saber, 2019).**

## Zumbi dos Palmares (c. 1655 to 1695), Brazil

**Who:** The last leader of Palmares, the largest and longest-lasting quilombo (community of people who escaped slavery) in colonial Brazil. In 1678 the earlier leader, Ganga Zumba, accepted a treaty: freedom for those born in Palmares, in exchange for returning the others to slavery. Zumbi led the side that rejected it, and led Palmares until its main settlement fell in 1694.

**Death:** Killed in an ambush 11/20/1695 after his hiding place was betrayed. Reports that his head was displayed in Recife to disprove his immortality are widely repeated but not confirmed from a primary document. **Unfinished:** he fought on for more than a year after Palmares fell; his people believed he could not die.

**Fit:** Starts in the Cohort; the most likely Cohort defector to the Uncrowned, and with 275 years in Eirion, one of its longest-serving officers. *He would argue:* I rejected a peace that freed some of us by returning the rest to chains. Do not ask me to accept one now.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The man who says no at the midpoint, as he refused the 1678 treaty, giving the Uncrowned their first veteran. (2) Maroon-war expert who teaches native resisters to survive without a protector, know-how that does not come from a Westerner. (3) A betrayal thread: he was given up by someone close to him.

**Represents:** Freedom not on a master's terms; refusing freedom bought by betraying others.

**Risks:** A national figure in Brazil; 11/20 is a national holiday since 2023. Very few sources survive, and none show his inner life; the story of his baptism as "Francisco" rests on disputed letters. Some historians argue Palmares had its own hierarchy; do not make him a flawless saint.

2. Silvia Hunold Lara, *Palmares & Cucaú: o aprendizado da dominação* (2021, Portuguese). Scholarly; publisher unverified. https://revistapesquisa.fapesp.br/en/original-documents-reveal-new-aspects-of-the-history-of-palmares/

## Qin Shi Huang (259 to 210 BCE), China

**Who:** King of Qin who conquered the Warring States and proclaimed himself First Emperor in 221 BCE. Ruled by Legalist principles; standardized script, weights, measures, and roads; ordered the book burning of 213 BCE. His tomb near Xi'an, with the Terracotta Army, is still largely unopened.

**Death:** Died in 210 BCE at Shaqiu while touring the east. Zhao Gao and Li Si hid the death and forged a succession; the dynasty fell within about four years. **Documented** (Sima Qian's *Shiji*): his search for immortality and the mercury rivers in his tomb. Modern measurements found elevated mercury at the site. **Legend:** that elixir pills killed him. **Unfinished:** he spent his last decade trying not to die.

**Fit:** The Covenant, as Alexander's most dangerous partner. *He would argue:* gods are a story for the people; law, roads, and one script hold a realm together for ten thousand years.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Architect of the Covenant's administration. (2) A rival god-king, which sets up a split inside the Covenant. (3) The man who most wanted immortality meets Bray, who expected to die.

**Represents:** Immortality as obsession; order bought with terror; systems that outlast rulers.

**Risks:** **The highest overlap of any candidate:** FGO's Lostbelt King, plus *Record of Ragnarok* and the manga *Kingdom*. The *Shiji* was written by a hostile successor dynasty. Avoid the "Oriental despot." As the only East Asian Eternal and a villain, he would be a tokenism risk.

## Timur (1336 to 1405), Central Asia

**Who:** Turco-Mongol conqueror from Transoxiana who founded the Timurid empire, from Anatolia to Delhi. Sacked Delhi (1398) and Baghdad (1401); defeated the Ottoman sultan at Ankara (1402). Not a descendant of Chinggis Khan, he never took the title khan. He ruled as amir through figurehead khans. A major patron of architecture and learning in Samarkand.

**Death:** Died of fever at Otrar in February 1405 (17, 18, or 19 February) while marching east to invade Ming China. The campaign was abandoned. **Unfinished:** died at about 68, on the march toward the one empire he had not challenged.

**Fit:** The Covenant, as the kingmaker behind Alexander. He ruled without a crown, which makes him the dark mirror of the Uncrowned's name. *He would argue:* a crown is a costume; I never wore one, and half the world obeyed me.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The power behind the god-king: he commands the armies and decides who holds the title. (2) His unfinished campaign drives Covenant war plans. (3) The Uncrowned's warning: refusing a crown does not mean refusing to rule, so their belief must be about power, not titles.

**Represents:** Power without a title; terror alongside patronage of art and learning.

**Risks:** A national hero in Uzbekistan, remembered for massacres in Iran, India, Syria, Iraq, and Georgia. Avoid "horde" imagery; he was a literate, devout Muslim and a sophisticated politician. The "Curse of Timur" is legend. **Lowest overlap of the Covenant candidates.**

## Che Guevara (1928 to 1967), Argentina and Cuba

**Who:** Argentine physician radicalized by travel and the 1954 Guatemala coup. A top commander of the Cuban Revolution, then commander of La Cabaña fortress, president of the National Bank, and Minister of Industries. Left Cuba in 1965 to export revolution: the Congo (failed), then Bolivia.

**Death:** Captured 10/08/1967 and executed 10/09/1967 in the schoolhouse at La Higuera, Bolivia, on the Bolivian high command's order; Sgt. Mario Terán fired. The CIA and US Special Forces trained the unit that caught him. **Unfinished:** died at 39, a prisoner, his continental revolution never started.

**Documented facts that matter for the story:**
- **"Two, three, many Vietnams":** "Message to the Tricontinental," published 04/16/1967. Quote from one named translation.
- **Bolivia:** His own diary records that "the peasant masses are not helping us" (September 1967; wording varies by translation). His column studied Quechua; the region spoke Guaraní.
- **Foco theory:** *Guerrilla Warfare* (1960): the insurrection can create the conditions for revolution. Régis Debray, who popularized it, was released from a Bolivian prison in late 1970, Book 1's year.
- **La Cabaña, 1959:** He oversaw the revolutionary tribunals and signed off on executions. Estimates under his command range from about 55 to about 176 depending on scope; partisan claims run far higher (unverified).

**Fit:** The Cohort, as the sincere voice for the garrison at the midpoint. *He would argue:* we do not wait for conditions, we create them; a council that cannot defend itself is not free.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Wins the argument at the war council for garrisoning the town; Spartacus or Toussaint makes the decision, so blame is shared. (2) The man the Uncrowned cannot dismiss: he lives poorly and fights in front, so the case against him must rest on the principle that he rules the natives. (3) Possibly the garrison commander. Keep Vietnam to one line so he does not crowd out Hải.

**Represents:** The liberator who rules without wanting to; the vanguard idea; the gap between a symbol and a man.

**Risks:** The most polarizing candidate; many Cuban Americans see him as an executioner. Leaving out La Cabaña reads as hagiography; making it his only trait reads as caricature. Died within living memory; children living. **Does not widen the cast racially:** Argentine of Spanish and Irish descent. Do not use the Korda photograph without a license. Legal points are general information only; confirm with a publishing attorney.
