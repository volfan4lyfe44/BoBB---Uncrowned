**Decision rule (from the author):** Add one or two figures only if each has a real plot job. Avoid tokenism, white-savior framing, and stereotypes.

## José Rizal (1861 to 1896), Philippines

**Who:** Physician, novelist, poet, and reformer in the Spanish-ruled Philippines. His novels *Noli Me Tangere* (1887) and *El Filibusterismo* (1891) exposed colonial and clerical abuse, were banned, and helped spark Filipino nationalism. He worked for reform inside the Spanish system, not armed revolt. Exiled to Dapitan. Today the national hero of the Philippines.

**Death:** Executed by firing squad at Bagumbayan, Manila, about 7:00 a.m. on 12/30/1896, after a court-martial for rebellion. In mid-1896 he had told the Katipunan the planned revolution was premature. On 12/15/1896 he wrote a manifesto denouncing the rising; it was suppressed. **Unfinished:** died at 35, condemned for a revolution he opposed, claimed by both sides.

**Fit:** Uncrowned, possibly by way of the Covenant. He could begin as a Covenant scholar or official who believes Alexander's rule can be made humane, then break with it, as he broke with Spain. *He would argue:* a people that trades one master for another has not been freed.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The inside man: 74 years of access to imperial records gives the Uncrowned proof of what Alexander does to the natives. (2) The writer who drafts the Uncrowned's founding argument and warns that words get co-opted. (3) Gordon's counterpart: a colonized man and a colonizer in the same faction.

**Represents:** Reform against revolution; the power and limits of the pen; heroes shaped by those in power.

**Risks:** National hero, studied in every Philippine school. Renato Constantino's "Veneration Without Understanding" (1969) argues the American regime promoted him to discourage armed independence. The deathbed retraction controversy is unresolved (unverified details). Do not make him a saint or Bray's teacher.

## Emiliano Zapata (1879 to 1919), Mexico

**Who:** Horse trainer and village leader from Anenecuilco, Morelos; the leading agrarian commander of the Mexican Revolution. His Plan de Ayala (11/1911) demanded that hacienda land be returned to the villages. He fought Díaz, Madero, Huerta, and Carranza in turn and never sought national office. Watchword: "Tierra y Libertad."

**Death:** Killed 04/10/1919 at the Hacienda de Chinameca. Colonel Jesús Guajardo, under General Pablo González, faked a defection and lured him to a meeting. **Unfinished:** died at 39, betrayed by men of the same revolution; land reform came later, on other men's terms.

**The chair, 12/1914:** *Documented:* Villa sat in the presidential chair at the National Palace and Zapata did not, as the photographs show. *Legend:* his remark that the chair should be burned "to put an end to ambitions" appears only in secondary sources. Samuel Brunk introduces it with "some say." Another version credits his brother Eufemio. Use it as legend, with characters arguing over it.

**Fit:** The Cohort, as the conscience of its midpoint break. *He would argue:* the land belongs to those who work it, and any leader who sits in the palace before returning it has already betrayed the cause.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The midpoint walkout: he confronts Spartacus over the garrison, and fighters follow him out, a bridge to the Uncrowned. (2) The chair scene in Eirion: he refuses a captured seat of power. (3) He distrusts defectors, having died by a fake one, which creates tension with any Covenant turncoat.

**Represents:** Land against crown; power refused on principle; revolutions betrayed by their own victors.

**Risks:** A national icon claimed by the left, the state, and the EZLN. His grandson threatened legal action over a 2019 painting. Avoid the bandit caricature, the noble-peasant cliché, and sombrero-and-bandolier shorthand.

## Yaa Asantewaa (c. 1840 to 1921), Asante, Ghana

**Who:** Queen Mother (ohemaa) of Ejisu in the Asante kingdom. After the British exiled the Asantehene Prempeh I in 1896, she acted as regent. In March 1900 Governor Frederick Hodgson demanded the Golden Stool, the sacred seat holding the soul of the Asante nation. She rallied the leaders, led the War of the Golden Stool, and besieged the British in the Kumasi fort.

**Death:** Captured or surrendered in 1901, exiled to the Seychelles, died there 10/17/1921. **Unfinished:** died in exile, three years before Prempeh I returned; the British never got the Stool, but she never knew the kingdom would survive.

**Fit:** Uncrowned, as the advocate of native sovereignty and its hardest critic, or an independent ally of the native resisters. *She would argue:* outsiders, kind or cruel, do not get to decide who sits on another people's stool, including deciding there should be no stool at all.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Guardian of a sacred native object Alexander demands as proof of submission. (2) The check on the white-savior risk: she calls out Bray, Shaw, and Gordon whenever they speak for the natives. Gordon is a direct parallel to the officers she fought. (3) A siege commander, giving the Uncrowned a victory not led by a Westerner.

**Represents:** Sovereignty and the sacred; women's leadership in a non-Western political tradition; exile and the long wait for return.

**Risks:** National heroine in Ghana; the Ejisu Queen Mother office still exists. Her famous war speech survives only in oral versions; present it as tradition. Avoid "warrior queen" exoticism and generic "tribal" mysticism; Asante was a complex state. The Golden Stool is sacred to living people. Get Asante terms right.

## Tupac Amaru II (José Gabriel Condorcanqui, c. 1738 to 1781), Peru

**Who:** An Indigenous noble and kuraka in colonial Cusco, and a mule-train operator, who claimed descent from the last Inca, Túpac Amaru I. After years of legal petitions against forced labor, he seized and executed the corregidor Antonio de Arriaga in 11/1780, starting the largest anti-colonial rising in 18th-century Spanish America. His wife, Micaela Bastidas, was a central organizer and commander.

**Death:** Captured in spring 1781 (March or April; sources conflict). On 05/18/1781 in Cusco's Plaza de Armas he was made to watch the executions of his wife and eldest son, then horses failed to pull him apart and he was beheaded. **Unfinished:** the rising went on two more years without him, and historians still argue whether he sought reform or independence.

**Fit:** The Cohort, as a senior general whose claim to rule is the danger: a liberator the natives might crown. *He would argue:* the natives need a ruler who knows their suffering, not no ruler at all.

**Plot jobs:** (1) "The other king": natives start hailing him as a restored ruler, the Uncrowned's founding question in its hardest form. (2) Bray tells him that Peru's military government made him the symbol of its 1969 land reform; he learns he has become a state icon. (3) The cost of terror: his rising saw reprisals on both sides, a debate with Toussaint and Spartacus.

**Represents:** Restoration against liberation; legitimacy by blood; history turning rebels into state symbols.

**Risks:** National hero across the Andes; his name was taken by the Tupamaros and the MRTA. Scholars disagree on his aims. Avoid "last Inca" mysticism, the noble savage, and gore. Do not sideline Micaela Bastidas. He was literate, Catholic, and bilingual, and fought through lawsuits before arms.

2. Ward Stavig and Ella Schmidt, eds., *The Tupac Amaru and Catarista Rebellions: An Anthology of Sources* (Hackett, 2008). Primary in translation. https://search.worldcat.org/oclc/174501545

## Rani Lakshmibai (c. 1828 or 1835 to 1858), Jhansi, India

**Who:** Queen of Jhansi. When her husband died in 1853, the East India Company refused to recognize their adopted son and annexed the state in 1854 under the Doctrine of Lapse. She became a leader of the 1857 rebellion, escaped the fort when Sir Hugh Rose stormed Jhansi in 1858, and helped take Gwalior.

**Death:** Killed in June 1858 at Kotah-ki-Serai near Gwalior (17 or 18 June; India commemorates 06/18). Battle details are contested. **Unfinished:** died mid-campaign, in her twenties, with her son's claim unresolved.

**Fit:** The Cohort, as the Uncrowned's hardest test: she fought both to keep her people's land free and to restore her son's throne. *She would argue:* I did not fight the Company so a better master could take its place, but a people at war needs someone to lead it.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Cavalry commander who rides with native fighters, so the broken word costs her personally. (2) The regent's argument: what if the natives ask an Eternal to lead them? (3) Mirror to Napoleon: both lost to British-led forces; one chose empire, one chose the resisters.

**Represents:** Resistance to annexation dressed as legal process; fighting for your people versus your crown.

**Risks:** Major national hero with a contested image, including Hindu-nationalist use. Avoid the exoticized "warrior princess." Her role in the 1857 killings at Jhansi is disputed. **Overlap: she is an FGO Servant (4-star Saber, 2019).**

## Zumbi dos Palmares (c. 1655 to 1695), Brazil

**Who:** The last leader of Palmares, the largest and longest-lasting quilombo (community of people who escaped slavery) in colonial Brazil. In 1678 the earlier leader, Ganga Zumba, accepted a treaty: freedom for those born in Palmares, in exchange for returning the others to slavery. Zumbi led the side that rejected it, and led Palmares until its main settlement fell in 1694.

**Death:** Killed in an ambush 11/20/1695 after his hiding place was betrayed. Reports that his head was displayed in Recife to disprove his immortality are widely repeated but not confirmed from a primary document. **Unfinished:** he fought on for more than a year after Palmares fell; his people believed he could not die.

**Fit:** Starts in the Cohort; the most likely Cohort defector to the Uncrowned, and with 275 years in Eirion, one of its longest-serving officers. *He would argue:* I rejected a peace that freed some of us by returning the rest to chains. Do not ask me to accept one now.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The man who says no at the midpoint, as he refused the 1678 treaty, giving the Uncrowned their first veteran. (2) Maroon-war expert who teaches native resisters to survive without a protector, know-how that does not come from a Westerner. (3) A betrayal thread: he was given up by someone close to him.

**Represents:** Freedom not on a master's terms; refusing freedom bought by betraying others.

**Risks:** A national figure in Brazil; 11/20 is a national holiday since 2023. Very few sources survive, and none show his inner life; the story of his baptism as "Francisco" rests on disputed letters. Some historians argue Palmares had its own hierarchy; do not make him a flawless saint.

## Qin Shi Huang (259 to 210 BCE), China

**Who:** King of Qin who conquered the Warring States and proclaimed himself First Emperor in 221 BCE. Ruled by Legalist principles; standardized script, weights, measures, and roads; ordered the book burning of 213 BCE. His tomb near Xi'an, with the Terracotta Army, is still largely unopened.

**Death:** Died in 210 BCE at Shaqiu while touring the east. Zhao Gao and Li Si hid the death and forged a succession; the dynasty fell within about four years. **Documented** (Sima Qian's *Shiji*): his search for immortality and the mercury rivers in his tomb. Modern measurements found elevated mercury at the site. **Legend:** that elixir pills killed him. **Unfinished:** he spent his last decade trying not to die.

**Fit:** The Covenant, as Alexander's most dangerous partner. *He would argue:* gods are a story for the people; law, roads, and one script hold a realm together for ten thousand years.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Architect of the Covenant's administration. (2) A rival god-king, which sets up a split inside the Covenant. (3) The man who most wanted immortality meets Bray, who expected to die.

**Represents:** Immortality as obsession; order bought with terror; systems that outlast rulers.

**Risks:** **The highest overlap of any candidate:** FGO's Lostbelt King, plus *Record of Ragnarok* and the manga *Kingdom*. The *Shiji* was written by a hostile successor dynasty. Avoid the "Oriental despot." As the only East Asian Eternal and a villain, he would be a tokenism risk.

## Timur (1336 to 1405), Central Asia

**Who:** Turco-Mongol conqueror from Transoxiana who founded the Timurid empire, from Anatolia to Delhi. Sacked Delhi (1398) and Baghdad (1401); defeated the Ottoman sultan at Ankara (1402). Not a descendant of Chinggis Khan, he never took the title khan. He ruled as amir through figurehead khans. A major patron of architecture and learning in Samarkand.

**Death:** Died of fever at Otrar in February 1405 (17, 18, or 19 February) while marching east to invade Ming China. The campaign was abandoned. **Unfinished:** died at about 68, on the march toward the one empire he had not challenged.

**Fit:** The Covenant, as the kingmaker behind Alexander. He ruled without a crown, which makes him the dark mirror of the Uncrowned's name. *He would argue:* a crown is a costume; I never wore one, and half the world obeyed me.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The power behind the god-king: he commands the armies and decides who holds the title. (2) His unfinished campaign drives Covenant war plans. (3) The Uncrowned's warning: refusing a crown does not mean refusing to rule, so their belief must be about power, not titles.

**Represents:** Power without a title; terror alongside patronage of art and learning.

**Risks:** A national hero in Uzbekistan, remembered for massacres in Iran, India, Syria, Iraq, and Georgia. Avoid "horde" imagery; he was a literate, devout Muslim and a sophisticated politician. The "Curse of Timur" is legend. **Lowest overlap of the Covenant candidates.**

## Che Guevara (1928 to 1967), Argentina and Cuba

**Who:** Argentine physician radicalized by travel and the 1954 Guatemala coup. A top commander of the Cuban Revolution, then commander of La Cabaña fortress, president of the National Bank, and Minister of Industries. Left Cuba in 1965 to export revolution: the Congo (failed), then Bolivia.

**Death:** Captured 10/08/1967 and executed 10/09/1967 in the schoolhouse at La Higuera, Bolivia, on the Bolivian high command's order; Sgt. Mario Terán fired. The CIA and US Special Forces trained the unit that caught him. **Unfinished:** died at 39, a prisoner, his continental revolution never started.

**Documented facts that matter for the story:**
- **"Two, three, many Vietnams":** "Message to the Tricontinental," published 04/16/1967. Quote from one named translation.
- **Bolivia:** His own diary records that "the peasant masses are not helping us" (September 1967; wording varies by translation). His column studied Quechua; the region spoke Guaraní.
- **Foco theory:** *Guerrilla Warfare* (1960): the insurrection can create the conditions for revolution. Régis Debray, who popularized it, was released from a Bolivian prison in late 1970, Book 1's year.
- **La Cabaña, 1959:** He oversaw the revolutionary tribunals and signed off on executions. Estimates under his command range from about 55 to about 176 depending on scope; partisan claims run far higher (unverified).

**Fit:** The Cohort, as the sincere voice for the garrison at the midpoint. *He would argue:* we do not wait for conditions, we create them; a council that cannot defend itself is not free.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Wins the argument at the war council for garrisoning the town; Spartacus or Toussaint makes the decision, so blame is shared. (2) The man the Uncrowned cannot dismiss: he lives poorly and fights in front, so the case against him must rest on the principle that he rules the natives. (3) Possibly the garrison commander. Keep Vietnam to one line so he does not crowd out Hải.

**Represents:** The liberator who rules without wanting to; the vanguard idea; the gap between a symbol and a man.

**Risks:** The most polarizing candidate; many Cuban Americans see him as an executioner. Leaving out La Cabaña reads as hagiography; making it his only trait reads as caricature. Died within living memory; children living. **Does not widen the cast racially:** Argentine of Spanish and Irish descent. Do not use the Korda photograph without a license. Legal points are general information only; confirm with a publishing attorney.

## The Mahdi: Muhammad Ahmad (1844 to 1885), Sudan

**Who:** A Sudanese Sufi teacher who declared himself the Mahdi in 1881 and led a revolt against British-backed Turco-Egyptian rule. His forces took El Obeid, then Khartoum in January 1885. Kim Searcy calls his state "the first modern Islamic state." His successor, the Khalifa Abdallahi, ruled until the British reconquest in 1898.

**Death:** Died 06/22/1885 (some sources say 06/20 or 06/21), probably of typhus. Sources disagree on Omdurman or Khartoum; his tomb is at Omdurman. Gordon died when Khartoum fell, usually dated 01/26/1885; how he died is disputed. The claim that the Mahdi wanted Gordon taken alive is unverified. **Unfinished:** he took Khartoum and died about five months later, before he could govern the state he made. **Arrives about five months after Gordon.**

**A verified exchange with Gordon:** Gordon offered him the sultanate of Kordofan. The Mahdi refused, sent Gordon a dervish's patched robe, and called on him to become a Muslim (*The Spectator*, 04/05/1884).

**Fit:** No faction at first. An outsider who settles near Gordon's refuge, possibly an ally of the native resisters, possibly edging toward the Uncrowned, which the book would have to earn. *He would argue:* I drove out one foreign master in the name of God; why would I bow to another who calls himself God?

**Plot jobs:** (1) Gordon's reckoning: a living witness from the other side of Khartoum who will not simply forgive him. (2) Exposing the false god: his objection to Alexander's godhood comes from belief, not politics, which no Western character can give. (3) Testing the refuge: Gordon must decide whether the man he fought is protected like anyone else.

**Represents:** Anti-colonial resistance from the colonized side; whether a resister who founds a state becomes a ruler.

**Risks:** Many Muslims reject his claim; the Ansar tradition in Sudan honors him. Do not let the book, or the presence, settle whether he was the Mahdi. British sources written for the 1898 reconquest push the "fanatic" stereotype; use Sudanese documents. **He must have goals of his own, not exist to redeem Gordon.**

**Alternatives:** The Khalifa Abdallahi (killed 11/25/1899 at Umm Diwaykarat), a state-builder closer to an Alexander-style ruler. Osman Digna (died 1926 in Egypt), a long-fighting Mahdist commander.

**Sources:**
1. P. M. Holt, *The Mahdist State in the Sudan, 1881-1898* (Clarendon Press, 1958; 2nd ed. 1970). Monograph.
2. Muhammad Ibrahim Abu Salim, ed., *Al-Athar al-kamila lil-Imam al-Mahdi* (The Writings of the Mahdi), vol. 1 (Khartoum University Press, 1990). Primary, Arabic, edited by a Sudanese scholar.
3. Kim Searcy, *The Formation of the Sudanese Mahdist State* (Brill, 2011). Monograph. https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/isbn/9789004191075/html
4. Dominic Green, *Three Empires on the Nile* (Free Press, 2007). Popular history.
5. Abu Salim, ed., *Mudhakkirat Uthman Diqna* (Osman Digna's memoirs; Khartoum University Press, 1974). Primary.

## Darius III (died 330 BCE), Persia

**Who:** The last Achaemenid king, about 336 to 330 BCE. Defeated by Alexander at Issus (333 BCE) and Gaugamela (331 BCE). Greek writers made him a coward; Pierre Briant argues that image is propaganda to magnify Alexander.

**Death:** July 330 BCE, near Hecatompylos (one source says Rhagae). Bessus led the conspiracy; Arrian (*Anabasis* 3.21.10) says Nabarzanes and Barsaentes struck the blows as Alexander closed in. Alexander gave him a royal burial at Persepolis. **Unfinished:** betrayed by his own nobles, died in chains, remembered only as Alexander's foil. **Arrives 7 years before Alexander.**

**Fit:** At odds with everyone; leans Uncrowned, a king who lost his crown and comes to believe no one should hold one. *He would argue:* I was a king, and my own lords sold me. A crown is only a target.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The only Eternal who saw the Covenant founded. (2) His betrayal mirrors the broken word; he sees it coming. (3) If Alexander broke or enlisted him early, his defection costs the Covenant.

**Represents:** Who writes history.

**Risks:** Avoid the coward of Greek legend and "Oriental despot" imagery. **Confirmed FGO Servant** (Berserker, framed as Iskandar's rival). A Darius and Alexander rivalry sits very close to Fate's.

**Sources:**
1. Arrian, *The Landmark Arrian*, trans. Pamela Mensch, ed. James Romm (Pantheon, 2010). https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/?p=38977
2. Arrian, *The Campaigns of Alexander*, trans. Aubrey de Sélincourt, rev. J. R. Hamilton (Penguin Classics).
3. Pierre Briant, *Darius in the Shadow of Alexander*, trans. Jane Marie Todd (Harvard University Press, 2015). https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2015/2015.10.14
4. Pierre Briant, *From Cyrus to Alexander*, trans. Peter T. Daniels (Eisenbrauns, 2002). Reference.

## Cyrus the Great (died 530 BCE), Persia

**Who:** Founder of the Achaemenid Empire; conquered Media, Lydia, and Babylon (539 BCE). The Cyrus Cylinder records how he presented himself to the Babylonians. Greek writers, including Xenophon, idealized him. A major national symbol in modern Iran.

**Death:** Usually 530 BCE. Herodotus (*Histories* 1.204-214): killed fighting the Massagetae; Queen Tomyris puts his head in a skin of blood (Herodotus says he heard several versions). Xenophon (*Cyropaedia* 8.7): dies peacefully in bed, read by scholars as idealized. No Persian text describes his death. **Arrives about 207 years before Alexander.**

**Alexander and the tomb (Arrian 6.29, via Aristobulus):** Alexander found Cyrus's tomb at Pasargadae robbed, was distressed, and ordered it restored, walled up, and sealed with the royal seal.

**Fit:** An elder who stands apart, or the source of the Uncrowned's way of thinking. If he chose not to rule in Eirion, Alexander's godhood looks smaller beside him. *He would argue:* I took kingdoms. Here I learned the land was never mine to take.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The man Alexander admired: a refusal from Cyrus wounds Alexander as no army can. (2) The Eternal who knows the natives best, after 2,500 years. (3) Gordon's mirror: a conqueror who came to regret conquest.

**Represents:** Legitimacy versus rule; whether the memory of a just conqueror is earned.

**Risks:** A contested national symbol. The claim that the Cylinder is "the first charter of human rights" is disputed (British Museum curator John Curtis called it misleading); do not state it as fact. **Placing him in Eirion before Alexander may overshadow Alexander and changes the Covenant's backstory.**

**Sources:**
1. Herodotus, *The Histories*, trans. Tom Holland (Penguin Classics, 2013).
2. Herodotus, *The Landmark Herodotus*, trans. Andrea L. Purvis, ed. Robert B. Strassler (Pantheon, 2007).
3. Xenophon, *The Education of Cyrus*, trans. Wayne Ambler (Cornell University Press, 2001).
4. Arrian, *Anabasis* 6.29, Chinnock translation. https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Anabasis_of_Alexander/Book_VI/Chapter_XXIX
5. Pierre Briant, *From Cyrus to Alexander* (Eisenbrauns, 2002).

## Amir Kabir (Mirza Taqi Khan, 1807 to 1852), Persia

**Who:** Chief minister to the young Naser al-Din Shah, 1848 to 1851. Curbed corruption, modernized the army, founded the Dar al-Fonun school. Court rivals and the shah's mother turned the shah against him; dismissed 11/11/1851. A national hero of reform in Iran.

**Death:** Exiled to Fin Garden near Kashan and killed there in the bath on 01/10/1852, on the shah's order. The opening of his veins comes from 19th-century testimony (Lady Sheil). **Unfinished:** three years of reform, ended by the king he served.

**Fit:** Uncrowned, as the administrator who now distrusts kings. *He would argue:* I built a state for a king, and the king opened my veins. Build for the people, or do not build.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Runs the logistics of Gordon's refuge. (2) Reads Alexander's court from long experience of courts.

**Risks:** **He ordered the execution of the Bab in July 1850, and the Babis were persecuted under his ministry.** To Babis and Bahais he is a persecutor. If he and Tahirih both appear (both arrive 1852), that history must be faced on the page.

**Sources:**
1. Abbas Amanat, "The Downfall of Mirza Taqi Khan Amir Kabir and the Problem of Ministerial Authority in Qajar Iran," *International Journal of Middle East Studies* (year unverified).
2. Abbas Amanat, *Pivot of the Universe* (University of California Press, 1997). Coverage of Amir Kabir unverified.
3. "Amir-e Kabir, Mirza Taqi Khan," *Encyclopaedia Iranica*. https://iranicaonline.org/articles/amir-e-kabir-mirza-taqi-khan

## Babak Khorramdin (died 838), Persia

**Who:** Leader of the Khurramite revolt in Azerbaijan against the Abbasid Caliphate for about 20 years (about 816 to 837). The Khurramites were a rural movement rooted in pre-Islamic Iranian tradition. The general Afshin took his fortress in 837; a former ally handed him over.

**Death:** Executed at Samarra before Caliph al-Mutasim in 838 (month disputed). Reports of mutilation are unverified in scholarship. **Unfinished:** 20 years of resistance ended by betrayal.

**Fit:** The Cohort, beside the native resisters. *He would argue:* a foreign power told my mountains what to believe; I know what the natives here are fighting.

**Plot jobs:** (1) The Cohort's conscience at the midpoint, refusing the betrayal, a bridge to the Uncrowned. (2) Speaks to native resisters as an equal: he fought for local belief against an imperial religion.

**Risks:** Claimed by both Iranian and Azerbaijani nationalists, an active dispute. Sources are scarce and mostly from the Abbasid side. Avoid making his execution a spectacle.

**Sources:**
1. Patricia Crone, *The Nativist Prophets of Early Islamic Iran* (Cambridge University Press, 2012). Monograph.
2. Al-Tabari's chronicle in English translation; the volume for 838 needs identifying (unverified).
3. Kaveh Farrokh, "Babak Khorramdin: The Freedom Fighter of Persia." Popular and partisan; use with caution.

## Tahirih (Qurrat al-Ayn, c. 1817 to 1852), Persia

**Who:** A Persian poet and religious scholar, one of the Bab's first disciples. At the Conference of Badasht in 1848 she appeared before men unveiled, declaring the Babi break with Islamic law. Honored in the Bahai Faith as a heroine and an early advocate for women.

**Death:** Executed in Tehran in August 1852, strangled, during the crackdown after a Babi attempt on the shah's life. About 35. The detail that her own veil was used is unverified.

**Fit:** Uncrowned, the voice against "Belief is not required. Only obedience." *She would argue:* no law that men wrote binds a soul.

**Risks:** **The highest sensitivity of any candidate.** She is revered in a living faith whose members are persecuted in Iran today. Do not depict the Bab or Bahaullah on the page. Use Bahai sensitivity readers. Do not reduce her to "the woman who unveiled"; she was first a theologian and poet.

**Sources:**
1. Abbas Amanat, *Resurrection and Renewal: The Making of the Babi Movement in Iran, 1844-1850* (Cornell University Press, 1989).
2. Farzaneh Milani, *Veils and Words* (Syracuse University Press, 1992). Coverage of Tahirih unverified.
3. Negar Mottahedeh, "The Mutilated Body of the Modern Nation," *Comparative Studies of South Asia, Africa and the Middle East* 18(2), 1998.

## Bambatha kaMancinza (died 1906), Natal

**Who:** Chief of the amaZondi in the Mpanza Valley near Greytown, Natal. In 1906 he refused the colonial poll tax and became the best-known leader of the uprising, fighting a guerrilla campaign from the Nkandla forest. Jeff Guy argues the events were a colonial war of conquest more than a "rebellion."

**Death:** Natal reported him killed at Mome Gorge on 06/10/1906; colonial accounts say his head was cut off for identification. **Disputed:** many Zondi people still say he escaped. A *Historia* article ("Bambatha after Mome: Dead or Alive?", author unverified) finds the colonial evidence "persuasive, but not conclusive." **Unfinished:** a lost war and a death never firmly settled. Do not let the book "settle" a dispute his descendants still care about.

**Fit:** Uncrowned, the cleanest ideological fit. *He would argue:* no one who does not belong to a land has the right to take from it, immortal or magistrate.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Witness against the garrison: he lived a colonial power arriving "for order" and staying. (2) Counterweight to Gordon: refuses an apology without action. He must not serve under Shaw or be led by any Western member. (3) Died in Eirion, with his survival disputed there too.

**Risks:** Honored in South Africa since 1994. Much detail comes from hostile colonial sources (James Stuart, 1913). Avoid the "savage warrior in the forest"; he was a political leader with grievances about land, tax, and chieftaincy.

**Sources:**

## Cetshwayo kaMpande (c. 1826 to 1884), Zulu kingdom

**Who:** Zulu king from 1872. Britain issued an ultimatum he could not meet and invaded in January 1879. His army won at Isandlwana (01/22/1879) and lost at Ulundi (07/04/1879). Captured and held in Cape Town while Britain split Zululand into thirteen chiefdoms. In 1882 he went to London, met Gladstone and Queen Victoria, and won a partial restoration in January 1883.

**Death:** After Zibhebhu attacked his capital in 07/1883, he fled to Eshowe and died there 02/08/1884. Certified as heart disease; the Zulu believed he was poisoned (unproven). **Unfinished:** won back his throne by diplomacy, saw it gutted, lost it to civil war, and died a guest of the power that broke his kingdom. **Arrives about 11 months before Gordon.**

**Fit:** A contested ally of the Uncrowned, or no faction: a legitimate king testing the creed. *He would argue:* I was king of my own people by their law; your Alexander is a king by theft.

**Plot jobs:** (1) Gordon's mirror and judge: both men were undone by the same empire within a year, and Cetshwayo is already in Eirion when Gordon wakes. No record shows they met on Earth. (2) Critic of the creed: does "no immortal should rule" apply to a kingship natives might choose? (3) Died in Eirion, leaving Gordon his records or treaties; if used, give him his own voice, for example through documents.

**Risks:** A direct ancestor of the current Zulu royal line, whose succession has been in the courts. British 1879 sources painted him as a tyrant. Avoid the "warrior king" of *Zulu* (1964) fan culture; Isandlwana was his generals' victory, and he was a political ruler.

**Sources:**
1. Jeff Guy, *The Destruction of the Zulu Kingdom* (Longman, 1979). Monograph.
2. Jeff Guy, *The View Across the River: Harriette Colenso and the Zulu Struggle against Imperialism* (David Philip, 2001).
3. Ian Knight, *Zulu Rising* (Macmillan, 2010). Uses Zulu oral sources.
4. C. de B. Webb and J. B. Wright, eds., *The James Stuart Archive* (University of Natal Press, 1976 to 2014). Zulu oral testimony. https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/000829131
5. South African History Online, "King Cetshwayo." https://www.sahistory.org.za/people/king-cetshwayo

## Dinuzulu kaCetshwayo (c. 1868 to 1913), Zulu kingdom

**Who:** Cetshwayo's son and heir, leader of the royalist uSuthu. Exiled to St Helena about 1890 to 1897 for treason; tried again in 1907 to 1909 over the 1906 uprising, convicted of harboring rebels, and released by Louis Botha to a Transvaal farm.

**Death:** 10/18/1913 near Middelburg, Transvaal. **Unfinished:** a king who never really reigned, twice exiled and tried, dying far from Zululand.

**Fit and jobs:** Uncrowned or none. Best as a link: a father-and-son meeting with Cetshwayo, or a reckoning with Bambatha, since his 1907 trial turned on whether he supported Bambatha. Works well as a record or legend. Two Zulu royals in Book 1 would crowd the cast.

**Risks:** Even closer to the living royal line than Cetshwayo; a complicated record of Boer alliances and civil war.

**Sources:** Marks, *Reluctant Rebellion* (1970); Guy, *Remembering the Rebellion* (2006); South African History Online, "King Dinuzulu." https://sahistory.org.za/people/king-dinuzulu

## Shaka kaSenzangakhona (c. 1787 to 1828), Zulu kingdom (brief)

**Who and death:** Built the Zulu kingdom into a regional power in the 1810s and 1820s; killed at KwaDukuza, most likely 09/22/1828, by his half-brothers Dingane and Mhlangana. How much of his reputation is evidence and how much is colonial invention is still argued.

**Fit:** Covenant, as a peer or rival of Alexander.

**Risks (high):** The most stereotyped African figure in Western media and heavily used in games. As the only Zulu in the villain faction, he would read as the "savage warrior." Use only with Napoleon-level depth.

**Sources:** Carolyn Hamilton, *Terrific Majesty* (Harvard University Press, 1998); Dan Wylie, *Myth of Iron: Shaka in History* (2006); *The James Stuart Archive*.

## Comparison: plot-job slots

Many candidates compete for the same few jobs. Book 1 can hold at most one living figure per slot. The others can appear as the dead (legends, records, remembered battles) or wait for later books.

| Slot | Candidates | Strongest |
|---|---|---|
| **Gordon's reckoning** (a colonized witness to his empire) | The Mahdi, Cetshwayo, Yaa Asantewaa, Rizal, Bambatha | **The Mahdi** (Khartoum itself; the only religious objection to a false god) or **Cetshwayo** (same empire, a legitimate king who tests the creed) |
| **The midpoint walkout** (refuses the Cohort's broken word) | Zumbi, Zapata, Babak, Bambatha, Lakshmibai | **Zumbi** (refused the 1678 treaty; 275 years in the Cohort) or **Zapata** (refused the chair) |
| **The garrison's voice** (argues for the broken word) | Che Guevara, Tupac Amaru II | **Che** (the vanguard theory), but he does not widen the cast |
| **Inside the Covenant** | Timur, Qin Shi Huang, Rizal (defector), Darius III, Shaka | **Timur** (rules without a crown; lowest overlap) |
| **Elder witness** (older than Alexander in Eirion) | Cyrus, Darius III | **Cyrus**, if the Covenant backstory can bear him |
| **The voice of words** | Rizal, Tahirih | **Rizal** (Tahirih carries the highest sensitivity) |

**Recommendation for Book 1 (author decides):** Add two living figures: **the Mahdi** (Gordon's reckoning) and **Zumbi** (the midpoint walkout). Use several others as Eirion's dead, for example Cetshwayo, Lakshmibai, Tupac Amaru II, and Bambatha. Hold Timur and Rizal for Book 2.
