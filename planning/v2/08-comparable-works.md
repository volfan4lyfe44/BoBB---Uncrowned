## Summary

- **Most overlap:** the Fate franchise (5 of 16: Alexander, Napoleon, Spartacus, Joan, Attila); *Heroes in Hell* (4: Alexander as a lead, Napoleon, Joan, Attila); *Tengen Hero Wars* (4: Napoleon, Spartacus, Joan, Hannibal); *Drifters* (2: Hannibal, Joan).
- **No overlap found:** *Riverworld* (except Joan in cover copy), *Record of Ragnarok*, *The Ministry of Time*.
- **Clean in every work checked:** Toussaint, Lannes, Lewes, Hale, Shaw, Gordon, Scholl, Rommel, Jackson, Charles XII. **This is the whole core cast except Alexander.**
- **Most-used figure:** Joan of Arc (five works).

## Closest premises

| Work | Premise | How this novel differs |
|---|---|---|
| ***Drifters*** (Kouta Hirano) | **Closest.** Historical figures pulled from history at death into a fantasy world with native peoples, split into warring factions. | Eirion is non-magical; *Drifters* runs on magic and centers Japanese figures. |
| ***Tengen Hero Wars*** (manga, Titan Manga in English) | Historical heroes hold territory and fight in another world. | Its viewpoint character is a living modern teenager pulled through a portal; battle-manga tone. |
| ***Riverworld*** (Philip José Farmer) | All of humanity wakes after death on one alien world. | Selected figures only, in a world with its own native peoples; focused on a war. |
| ***Heroes in Hell*** (Janet Morris and others) | The famous dead continue old conflicts in a theological, satirical Hell. | No imposed theology; Alexander's divinity is a fraud, not cosmic fact. |
| **Fate franchise** | Heroic spirits magically summoned to fight for a wish-granting Grail. | No magic, no summoners; mortal people made immortal, building their own politics. |
