# Rethink v2: A History of Eirion's Wars

**Status:** Decided by the author, 10/20/2026, unless marked **Open**. Revised from the history in `planning/worldbuilding/book-1-history-and-setting.md` (v1), fixed to match the v2 decisions.

**Rules this history follows:**
- Souls arrive in the order they died; Eirion years match Earth years.
- Eternal Ones die rarely, and each death is remembered. They are few and usually command rather than fight in the ranks.
- Every war between immortals kills natives by the thousands. The natives remember that first.
- **The natives have their own names and calendars for these wars**, different from the immortals' names. The names below are the Eternal Ones' names.
- Captured Eternal Ones are **"sent east."** No one outside the Covenant's inner circle knows what that means. It pays off in Books 2 and 3. It is what Hải faces in Book 1. **Private answer (decided 10/20/2026, not revealed in Book 1):** the Gate lies east. Alexander sends captives to try it. Those it accepts vanish; those it turns away come back broken and are held. Alexander knows the Gate is real, while the Purge taught everyone else it is a myth. Fits the rule that the Gate turns away souls who are not ready.

## The five great wars

| # | War (Eternal name) | When | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The First Cohort | About 70 BCE to about 40 BCE | Crixus dies and Vercingetorix is taken holding the rear; Spartacus escapes |
| 2 | The Purge | 5th and 6th centuries | Attila destroys those who sought the Gate |
| 3 | The Burning of the Bastion | About 1000 CE | The refuge massacred; the great cohort falls; the Accord |
| 4 | The Maid's Cohort | 15th century | The cohort rules through Joan's holiness and falls |
| 5 | The Last Cohort | 1803 to now | Still fighting. Book 1 |

### War 1: The First Cohort (about 70 BCE onward)

- **Before:** Alexander arrives in 323 BCE and unites the native kingdoms east of the grey plain. Natives who watch him not age for a century decide he is a god, and he lets them.
- **Crixus** arrives in 72 BCE, a year before Spartacus. On Earth he was Spartacus's fellow gladiator commander and split from him, dying separately at Mount Garganus. His age at death is not recorded.
- **Spartacus** arrives in 71 BCE. He finds an empire built on enslaved natives. He builds the first walls of the Bastion on the high plain as a vigil for the dead and carves the inscription Bray later finds. Then he leaves, because mourning frees no one, and raises the first cohort among the enslaved.
- **Spartacus** arrives in 71 BCE and finds Crixus already there. The two reconcile. **Vercingetorix**, the Gallic chieftain executed in Rome in 46 BCE (about 36), arrives about 25 years into the war and joins it: two men who fought Rome, now fighting Alexander.
- **The end (about 40 BCE):** Alexander's armies trap the first cohort. **Crixus and Vercingetorix hold the rear** so Spartacus and the survivors can escape into the highlands. **Crixus dies there:** on Earth he died apart from Spartacus; here he dies so Spartacus can live. **Vercingetorix is captured and sent east.** The Gate turns him away, and he is held, alive, in the east, an echo of the six years he spent in a Roman prison before his execution. A thread for Books 2 and 3. Spartacus has carried the debt ever since.
- **What it leaves behind:** The highland natives who hid the survivors begin the tradition of guiding new arrivals to safety. **Ishel's people still keep it.**

### War 2: The Purge (5th and 6th centuries)

- A movement grows among natives and Eternals who have heard rumors of a way out of Eirion: the Gate.
- **The leader: Julian the Apostate.** The Roman emperor, killed in battle in Persia in 363 at 31, arrives and spends about 90 years in Eirion seeking old mysteries and hidden truths. He gathers the rumor of a way out into a movement of natives and Eternals.
- **Attila** arrives in 453. Alexander sends him to destroy the movement and every trace of the rumor. This is how he earned his title, "the Scourge of the Gate." **Julian dies in the Purge.** The rest of its dead are unnamed: the Forgetting erased their names, which is what the highland name for this war remembers.
- **What it leaves behind:** The first sign that Alexander fears the Gate. Most Eternals today believe the Gate is a myth, because the Purge made sure they would. (Pays off in Books 2 and 3.)

### War 3: The Burning of the Bastion (about 1000 CE)

- In the centuries after Spartacus left, souls who would not fight either side drifted to his empty fortress and built on it. It became a refuge for the waiting dead.
- **The cause:** The keeper of the time, **Boethius** (executed on Earth in 524; author of *The Consolation of Philosophy*, a book about refuge in thought when all is lost), takes in a cohort's wounded and refuses to hand them over. Verify his dates and details against a biography.
- **The Burning:** The Covenant storms the Bastion and kills everyone inside, keeper and wounded alike.
- **The great cohort:** The massacre provokes the largest cohort in centuries, led by **Babak Khorramdin** (executed on Earth in 838; see the [dossier](07-wider-cast-dossier.md)). It nearly breaks the empire before it falls, and Babak dies with it.
- **The Accord:** Alexander learns that a burned refuge breeds rebels and a tolerated one keeps would-be rebels sitting still. He offers the next keeper the Accord: the Bastion takes no part in the war, and the Covenant never enters it.
- **What it leaves behind:** This is why Gordon keeps the Accord so strictly. The last keeper who broke neutrality got everyone killed.

### War 4: The Maid's Cohort (15th century)

- **Joan of Arc** arrives in 1431 and joins the cohort of her time.
- The cohort wins a region in the marches. Natives there take Joan for holy, and she lets them.
- **The fall:** The cohort begins ruling the region through the natives' belief in her. It governs in her name. Breaking its word to the natives and her holiness are the same event. The natives withdraw their support, and Alexander destroys the cohort to remove a rival god.
- **Jan Hus** (burned in 1415 at about 45 for refusing to recant) warns the cohort against ruling through Joan's holiness. He is ignored. Two people burned on Earth for their faith stand on opposite sides of the cohort's mistake.
- **Wat Tyler** (leader of the English Peasants' Revolt, killed during a parley with the king in 1381 at about 40) commands in the cohort and knows the clans are being ruled. **Both Hus and Tyler die when the cohort falls.** Tyler's death at a parley on Earth foreshadows Toussaint's parley in Book 1.
- **Joan survives.** Her quiet guilt: she used belief the way Alexander does.
- **What it leaves behind:** Two lessons in one history: cohorts that rule die, and so do immortals who let themselves be worshipped. Spartacus and Joan have seen the Book 1 midpoint before, and they repeat it anyway.
- **Placement rule:** The reveal comes after the Ch 17 midpoint (for example, Hale learns it in Ch 18) so the midpoint is not spoiled.

## The wars in native words (decided 10/20/2026)

Each region names a war for what it cost them; the heartland names them in the god-king's propaganda.

| # | Eternal name | Highland watchers (Ishel) | Marches clans (Dravik) | Covenant heartland (Andros) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The First Cohort | The Hiding | The Year the Gods Bled | The God's First Victory |
| 2 | The Purge | The Forgetting | The Year of Ash | The Correction |
| 3 | The Burning of the Bastion | The Fire on the Plain | The Great Rising | The Peace |
| 4 | The Maid's Cohort | The False Dawn | The Diverted River | The Pretender's War |
| 5 | The Last Cohort | The Long Watch | The Free Years | The Western Rebellion |

**Notes for the page:**
- **The Year the Gods Bled:** the first time natives saw an Eternal die (Crixus). The memory the Covenant would most like erased.
- **The Forgetting:** a people who know they were made to forget something, but not what.
- **The Diverted River:** the cohort forced the clans off their own course through their own faith. Explain once in a native voice, for example Dravik: you don't ask a river where it wants to go; you dig a ditch and it follows.
- **The Free Years:** proud in 1970, bitterly ironic after the Ch 17 broken word.
- **The Peace** and **The Correction:** the heartland names only the outcome, never the cost.

## Changes from the v1 history

- **The Uncrowned do not grow out of the Bastion over centuries.** In v2 they do not exist until Book 1. The Bastion is a refuge only.
- **Hannibal** is cut by the age rule (died at about 64). **Crixus and Vercingetorix** take his place in the first cohort's last stand.
- **Stonewall Jackson and Erwin Rommel** are removed from this history while they are on hold. If they return in a later book, their arrivals (1863 and 1944) can be added.
- **Sophie Scholl** goes into Covenant lands, not the Bastion, so she no longer pushes Hale to leave.
- **The Purge** now targets the Gate seekers, tying Attila's title to the series' central mystery.
- **The Maid's Cohort** now falls by ruling through Joan's holiness.
